### 2.3 The testing record — PCR, antibody, and specimen, in general laboratory terms

All of this section is general laboratory science from the three research reports behind the PCR analysis, not a recommendation for any person.

- PCR asks whether the organism's genetic material is present, right now, in the specimen tested, during the window when that nucleic acid is present in that specimen. A positive result does not by itself prove live organism, active disease in a particular organ, or disease causation. [D, from Analysis — PCR, Blood PCR, and Antibody Parallels (2026-11-02)]
- Antibody testing asks whether the immune system has reacted — IgM generally the earlier class, IgG later and generally longer-lasting. A positive IgG alone usually means past exposure or past infection, not current disease. Antibody titers alone do not prove a generational infection. [D, from the same analysis]
- Timing is the load-bearing difference. Acute-phase serum PCR windows in the reports are short — for example, chikungunya ideally under 7 days from onset, dengue acute-phase with serology needed after more than a week, Florida acute serum PCR for Eastern equine, St. Louis, and West Nile under 5 days, Powassan serum PCR in the first 7 days. A PCR taken after its window has closed cannot answer, however good the assay is. [D, from the same analysis]
- Specimen matters. The same virus can be PCR-positive in one specimen and PCR-negative in another at the same time; sensitivity differs even within blood fractions. [D, from the same analysis]
- No country-specific Mayo arbovirus panel exists in the reports. The United States baseline is the arbovirus antibody panel, serum, IgG and IgM — exactly 8 results, four viruses, IgG and IgM each — with no Jamestown Canyon analyte and no coronavirus analyte. Region changes selection, not that panel: outside the United States, chikungunya, dengue, and Zika are added separately; West Nile is added in any region. [D, from the Mayo ARBO report]
- Mayo has no multiplex blood meningitis or encephalitis PCR panel in the reports; the only syndromic panel is cerebrospinal-fluid-only, 14 targets. Blood PCRs that do exist in the reports are organism-specific or second-tier, and a cerebrospinal-fluid-validated panel cannot simply be run on blood — a specimen-type change is a modified test requiring full validation under the laboratory regulations cited in the reports, with documented inhibition and sensitivity limits, and a negative blood result does not exclude central nervous system infection. [D, from the encephalitis/meningitis PCR report]
