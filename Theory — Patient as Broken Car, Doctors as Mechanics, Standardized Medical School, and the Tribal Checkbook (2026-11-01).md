## Tests

### P1 — Patient and broken car, same need
**HOLDS as an analogy, BREAKS as an identity.** The three needs he names — interest, superior knowledge, willingness to act — are genuinely the same on both sides, and they connect directly to tonight's earlier addendum: a patient underreports until somebody wants to do something, and a car owner cannot diagnose what they cannot see [H→D]. The analogy breaks where all analogies break: a patient is not property, consent and autonomy govern medicine in a way they do not govern car repair, and a car has a manual and interchangeable parts while a patient does not. Analogy: strong. Identity: no.

### P2 — Doctors and mechanics both know a lot about a lot
**HOLDS.** Both fields genuinely require broad systems knowledge: a mechanic must know engines, electronics, hydraulics, and diagnostics; a physician must know anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, and diagnostics [D, general]. The comparison is fair at the level of breadth and diagnostic reasoning.

### P3 — Mechanics systematic; medicine made to be that way
**HOLDS.** Automotive diagnostics are famously systematic (symptom → code → test → isolate → repair), and medicine was historically built the same way (history → examination → differential → test → treatment) [D, general]. The Hippocratic method itself is a systematic approach. This postulate is the bridge between P1 and P4: the system exists in principle, and the question is who actually uses it.

### P4 — Good doctors build their own system, learning outside medical school
**HOLDS partial [H→D].** It is documented that clinical expertise continues well beyond formal schooling — residency, fellowship, continuing education, and bedside experience are where diagnostic systems are refined [D, general]. It breaks as a universal about medical school: schools do teach diagnostic systems (differential diagnosis, clinical reasoning courses), so "outside medical school" holds for the *refinement* of a personal system, not for the absence of any system in school. His own account — that he went to school to be a car mechanic — is the source of the comparison, and it is filed as his.

### P5 — Standardized medical school classes, government control
**HOLDS as a proposal, with a documented starting point.** U.S. medical education already has standardization: LCME accreditation of schools, the USMLE licensing sequence, and core clerkship structures [D, general]. What varies is curriculum detail, teaching method, and emphasis — so his call is not for standardization from zero, but for *more* standardization, and for government involvement in setting it. Whether government should take control is a policy position [H], not a tested fact, and it is filed as his position. The tension to keep visible: standardization improves consistency, and it can also narrow the very outside-school system-building he praises in P4.

### P7 — Stop withholding antibiotics; government admission tied to the 1990s balanced budget
**SPLIT verdict — the two halves do not stand or fall together.**
- **"Stop withholding antibiotics" [H, position]:** Filed as his position. The documented rationale for antibiotic stewardship — the reason clinicians restrict antibiotics — is antimicrobial resistance, not budget policy [D, general]: overuse selects resistant organisms, and stewardship programs exist to preserve antibiotic effectiveness. Withholding vs. stewardship is the key distinction: stewardship withholds an antibiotic *when it will not help or will harm* (wrong organism, viral illness, resistance risk); his position, as stated, does not make that distinction. This file does not recommend any antibiotic change for any person — medication questions belong to Kelsey.
- **"Due to the pre-balanced-budget 1990s" [H, causal claim]: BREAKS as a documented cause.** No documented link in his works or in general stewardship literature ties antibiotic stewardship to 1990s budget balancing. Stewardship's documented drivers are resistance data, guidelines, and hospital programs from the 1990s–2010s onward, on a different timeline and for a different reason. The 1990s budget era itself (see P8) is real; its causal link to antibiotic withholding is not documented and is filed as his claim, not as a finding.

"Came from both sides of the table" holds for that history.
