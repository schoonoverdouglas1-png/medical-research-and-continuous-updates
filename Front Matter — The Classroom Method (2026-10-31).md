# Front Matter — How This Book Was Taught: The Classroom Method

**To be placed at the front of the textbook, at the author's direction. Written October 31, 2026, from the author's own statement of his method.**

## To the reader

This book was not written the usual way. It was taught.

The author postulates. He gives theories. And the questions are perfect whether they are wrong or right — because this is like a class. He is building a textbook, and he is teaching classroom material through the postulating itself.

Some of the theories in this book may actually be intentionally wrong. They are posed deliberately, so that the conversation can be had — so that the theory can be tested against the record, and the testing can show what holds and what does not. The conversation is the method. The showing is the point.

In the author's words: the conversation exists "to show me the true miracle of what AI is able to show me."

## How to read a verdict

Every postulate in this book is tested, and every test ends in a verdict. Read them this way:

- **HOLDS** — the record supports it.
- **BREAKS** — the record does not support it, as stated.
- **PARTIAL** — part holds, part does not, and both halves stay visible.

A verdict of BREAKS is not a failed theory, and it is not a correction of the author. It is the class working. A wrong postulate that gets tested honestly teaches more than a right postulate that never gets questioned, and the tested answer is the lesson. Nothing is softened to spare the theory, and nothing is discarded because it broke — the old stays beside the new, so the reader can see the whole conversation.

## How to read the labels

So that the reader always knows where a claim comes from, every claim carries its provenance:

- **[D]** — documented, from the published record.
- **[H]** — his, filed as the author's account.
- **[H→D]** — his, with a documented foothold.
- **[LOGIC]** — an inference, with its steps shown.
- **[GAP]** — unknown. Left empty, never invented.

Wherever the treatment runs deep, a "Simply put" telling runs beside it in plain language — because complex information in deserves simple telling out, and the simple is never left out.

## The author's standing rules for this book

- Every idea is formed as a theory or postulate and saved.
- Compound dictations are split, so no sub-idea is lost.
- Vaccines, viruses, diseases, illnesses, sicknesses, and infections are thought separately and never collapsed into one another.
- A claim at the majority level of documented support counts — and its honest caveat is filed beside it.
- The old stays alongside the new first. Removal comes later, only if needed.

## One last thing

The author teaches by postulating, and the book learns by testing. If you read a postulate here that turns out to be wrong, do not skip it. Sit in the test. That is where the book is doing its real work.
