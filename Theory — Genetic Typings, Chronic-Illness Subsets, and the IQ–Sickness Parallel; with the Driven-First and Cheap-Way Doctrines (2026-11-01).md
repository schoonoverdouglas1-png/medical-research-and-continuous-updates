## P1 — The driven patients, who already asked, get fixed first [H, triage doctrine]

**His doctrine:** people who don't want to be cured won't be; the driven patients, like him, who have already asked for it, should be the ones fixed first — and they will be, because they asked.

**Tested:**
1. *Willingness matters [D as background]:* adherence and engagement predict outcomes across medicine — a patient who follows a regimen does better than one who does not. Willingness is a real outcome variable.
2. *As triage [H]:* "asked first, fixed first" is his priority rule — first-asked, first-served among the willing. Filed as his doctrine, not as standard triage (standard triage ranks by urgency and survivability, not by asking order). The difference is kept visible: his rule rewards asking; standard triage rewards acuity.
3. *Honest line:* "won't be cured" for the unwilling overstates — unwilling patients are still treated (emergency care, public-health treatment, pediatric care). As a statement about elective, sustained regimens, it holds better than as a universal.

**Verdict:** HOLDS as doctrine [H] — willingness as a sorting principle, with the standard-triage difference noted.

## P2 — Cheap way vs. expensive way to do the same job; all packages needed [H, cost doctrine]

## P3 — Subsets of chronic illness and infection based on genetic typings of individuals [H→D]

**His theory:** there are subsets of chronic illness and infection based on individuals' genetic typings.

**Tested:**
1. *Host genetics determine infection outcomes [D]:* host and microbial genetics are important determinants of infection and disease outcome; increasing data support host genetic factors as important determinants of HIV-1 susceptibility, mother-to-child transmission, and disease progression.
2. *Named typings [D]:* polymorphisms affecting CCR5 expression alter HIV risk, and a 32-bp deletion in CCR5 (Δ32) provides almost complete protection against HIV-1 infection in homozygous carriers; HLA type is a strong host factor for disease course — HLA-B*5701 and HLA-B27 more prevalent among long-term nonprogressors, HLA-B35 associated with accelerated progression. Typing → subset → outcome is documented, not hypothetical.
3. *Chronic illness generally [H→D neighborhood]:* HLA typings also subset autoimmune and chronic conditions (the HLA-disease map is wide). The infection leg is documented; the chronic-illness leg has the same mechanism (immune-gene typing) with disease-specific evidence varying by condition.

**Verdict:** HOLDS [H→D] — genetic typings subset infection outcomes as documented fact; chronic-illness subsets by typing follow the same mechanism, evidence varying by disease.
- Simply put: Your genes can sort you into a group that shakes a germ off or one that can't — proven for HIV by CCR5 and HLA types, and the same sorting logic runs through chronic illness.

## P4 — Genetic similarity between intellect and IQ may force subsets to be sick with certain things [H; heritability and pleiotropy real, "forced" breaks]

**His theory:** it is not impossible that, based on genetic similarity between intellect and IQ, certain population subsets might just be forced to be sick with certain things.

**Tested:**
1. *Intelligence is partly heritable [D as background]:* twin and family studies consistently find a substantial heritable component to cognitive ability — heritability is real, polygenic (many small variants), and not a single "IQ gene." His "genetic similarity between intellect and IQ" is filed as: cognitive traits share genetic influences.
2. *Pleiotropy is real [D as background]:* the same genetic variants can influence multiple traits — the mechanism by which a cognitive-trait variant could also touch disease risk exists. Genetic correlations between cognitive traits and some health traits are reported in the literature.
3. *"Forced to be sick" BREAKS [D as counterweight]:* genetic influence is risk, not destiny. Even for strongly genetic traits, environment dominates the variance — HIV set-point viral load, for example, has an estimated 30% of variance attributable to common heritable effects and roughly 70% to environmental and non-genetic factors. A subset can be at higher risk; it cannot be "forced" by IQ-similarity genetics as a documented matter.
4. *The specific link [GAP]:* no documented mapping ties IQ-similarity subsets to specific sickness subsets as a class. The mechanism (pleiotropy) exists; the mapping does not.

**Verdict:** possibility HOLDS as his hypothesis [H] ("not impossible" is the right hedge, kept); heritability and pleiotropy as mechanisms HOLDS [H→D]; "forced" BREAKS — risk is not force, and the specific IQ→sickness-subset mapping is [GAP].
- Simply put: Brainpower runs partly in families, and one gene can tug two traits at once — so the link isn't crazy. But genes load the dice, they don't force the roll, and nobody has mapped IQ-groups to sickness-groups.
