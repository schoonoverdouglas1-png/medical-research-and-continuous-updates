## His logic first [H], in full

1. Vaccines should be thought about as medication, not as a separate, non-medicine category.
2. Reason he gives: polio vaccine can cure polio, at least the old ones did.
3. His standing view: he always looks at it that way — the right vaccine can cure a person.
4. Consequence he draws: PCPs (primary care physicians) need to be more willing to practice that way — i.e., to think of vaccines as medicine that can, in the right circumstance, give a curative outcome.

## Fact-check second [D], beside his logic, not over it

### HOLDS — Vaccines are medicine, in the regulatory / classification sense
- Vaccines are biologics, regulated as medicines / medicinal products, prescribed and administered as medical interventions. Thinking of them as medicine, rather than as something outside medicine, holds as a classification point.
- "Medication" in ordinary language usually means a drug taken to treat a condition; vaccines are usually classified separately as *immunizations* / *prophylactics* within medicine, but they are still medical products, not non-medical products.

### BREAKS as stated — Polio vaccine does not cure established polio
- Polio vaccines (Salk inactivated polio vaccine, 1955, and Sabin oral polio vaccine, 1960s — the "old ones") are **preventive**, not curative for established poliomyelitis.
- Documented mechanism: they induce immunity before exposure, or, in the case of oral vaccine, intestinal immunity that can interrupt transmission. They do **not** reverse paralysis once poliovirus has damaged motor neurons, and they are not given as a treatment for a person already paralysed by polio.
- No documented case series shows polio vaccine curing paralytic polio. That specific claim — polio vaccine can cure polio — **breaks** against the documented preventive mechanism, for both old and current polio vaccines.

### PARTIAL — Curative / therapeutic use in the right circumstance, in other cases
- The broader claim — "in the right circumstance, a vaccine can give a curative outcome" — has **documented footholds in other circumstances, not in polio**:
  - **Rabies post-exposure prophylaxis:** vaccine given after exposure, before disease onset, prevents rabies from developing. That is preventive after exposure, not a cure for established rabies encephalitis, but it is a life-saving use after the exposure event, in the right window.
  - **BCG:** originally a tuberculosis vaccine, used as a *treatment* for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer, by intravesical instillation — a vaccine product used therapeutically, in a specific, licensed indication.
  - **Therapeutic cancer vaccines and other therapeutic vaccines:** investigational / specific licensed uses exist where a vaccine is given to treat, not just to prevent, a condition. Those are circumstance-specific, product-specific, and indication-specific — not a general rule that any vaccine cures its target disease.
- Those footholds support his *general* framing — vaccines can, in specific circumstances, be used as treatment — but they do **not** rescue the specific polio-cure claim, and they do not support using a preventive vaccine as a cure outside its licensed indication, dose, and timing.

### GAP — PCP practice
- Whether PCPs "need to be more willing to practice that way" is a practice / policy judgment [H], not a documented fact in this file. No survey, guideline, or practice standard is cited here for or against that judgment.
- Any decision to use a vaccine therapeutically, outside prevention, belongs to the patient and their clinician, for a specific product and indication, with the licensed label and evidence for that use. This file does not recommend any such use, for polio or for any other condition, and does not advise any person to seek or to refuse a vaccine as treatment.

## Verdict, as of this filing

- **Vaccines are medicine [H→D, HOLDS as classification]:** Yes, vaccines are medical / biologic products, and thinking of them inside medicine, not outside it, is correct.
- **Polio vaccine cures polio [BREAKS]:** No — old polio vaccines, Salk and Sabin, prevent polio; they do not cure established polio or reverse paralysis.
- **Right vaccine, right circumstance, curative outcome [PARTIAL]:** Yes in specific, documented circumstances (rabies PEP window, BCG for bladder cancer, specific therapeutic vaccines), no as a general rule, and not for polio as a cure.
- **PCP implication [H, his judgment, GAP as a practice claim]:** His call for PCPs to be more willing to think this way is filed as his position, not as a guideline. It is a question to put to a clinician, with a specific product and indication in mind, not a direction in this file.

## Ember's extra addition, labelled as mine

The useful distinction to keep, in one line, is: **preventive, post-exposure preventive, and therapeutic are three different uses, even when the same product name is involved.** Polio vaccine sits in the first box. Rabies vaccine after a bite sits in the second. BCG for bladder cancer sits in the third. Collapsing all three into "cure" loses exactly the timing and indication that make the right-circumstance claim true when it is true.
