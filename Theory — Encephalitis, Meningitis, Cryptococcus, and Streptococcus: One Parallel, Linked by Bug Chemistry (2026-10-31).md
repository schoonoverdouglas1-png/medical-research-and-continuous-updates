## Postulates (split, never collapsed)

### P1 — The four have a parallel [H→D]

- The bridge disease is documented: cryptococcal meningoencephalitis is one disease spanning both meningitis and encephalitis — meninges and brain parenchyma together. *Cryptococcus* is the most common fungal cause of central nervous system infection in the immunocompromised, and cryptococcal meningitis is its classic form.
- The streptococcal leg: *Streptococcus pneumoniae* causes pneumococcal meningitis (G00.1 in his own code tables) — the acute bacterial parallel in the same compartments, reached by the same route: lung → bloodstream → meninges and brain.
- Simply put: One yeast and one bacterium both end up in the same rooms of the nervous system, and one of them names both rooms at once.

**Where the parallel stops:** meningitis (meninges) and encephalitis (brain tissue) are anatomically distinct, and the two organisms differ in tempo and host — *Cryptococcus* chronic and subacute, usually immunocompromised; pneumococcus acute and fulminant, often previously healthy. Parallel at the level of central nervous system infection, not identity.

### P2 — They have relationships with each other, in ways that make sense [H→D partial]

The sensible relationships, named rather than left as "somehow":

1. **Same portal:** inhaled → lung → blood → central nervous system, for both organisms.
2. **Same compartment:** the cerebrospinal fluid and subarachnoid space, where both are diagnosed (culture, antigen).
3. **Same immune chemistry:** cytokines, chemokines, complement, antibodies — the host's shared language for both invaders.
4. **Co-presence:** polymicrobial and sequential central nervous system infections are documented in general; co-carriage of organisms is his established theme (diphtheria theory, polymicrobial membranes).

**Seam [GAP]:** a direct interaction between *Cryptococcus* and *S. pneumoniae* inside the central nervous system — one acting on the other — is not established as a specific mechanism. The relationships above are shared-route, shared-room, and shared-immune-language relationships, not yet a demonstrated organism-to-organism interaction.

- Simply put: They are related the way two visitors to the same house are related — same door, same rooms, same alarm system — not yet proven to be talking to each other.

### P3 — The combination of the chemistry in between [H→D partial]

The "chemistry in between" has three documented layers:

1. **Host mediators:** cytokines and chemokines (IL-6, TNF-α among them) — the gut-brain literature names immunomodulatory mediators such as cytokines and chemokines as carriers between microbiota and the central nervous system.
2. **Microbial chemicals:** cryptococcal capsular polysaccharide is continuously shed into the host's extracellular fluid, which includes blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid; pneumococcal pneumolysin and cell-wall fragments drive inflammation from the bacterial side.
3. **Metabolites at a distance:** the gut microbiota regulates the central nervous system through metabolites — short-chain fatty acids, tryptophan-kynurenine metabolites — and bioactive substances. Short-chain fatty acids modulate neuroinflammation, reinforce the blood-brain barrier, and regulate neurotransmitter synthesis.

- Simply put: The chemistry between them is not one chemical. It is the host's alarm signals, the bugs' shed pieces, and the distant metabolites from the gut — three streams meeting in the same nervous system.

Dysbiosis is characterized by overgrowth of proinflammatory strains, and that overgrowth alters the production of the metabolites that mediate gut-brain communication, most notably short-chain fatty acids and tryptophan. Overgrowth is the quantity leg: too many bugs, wrong mix, shifted chemistry.

## Combined statement

Encephalitis, meningitis, *Cryptococcus*, and *Streptococcus* parallel each other because they share a house — the central nervous system, reached through lung and blood — and they relate through shared rooms, shared immune language, and shared chemistry. That chemistry runs in three streams (host alarms, shed microbial pieces, distant gut metabolites) and is fed by the bug biome, its overgrowth, its overbuilding, and everything the bugs make and excrete. The framework holds; the single mechanism that would tie every item on the excreta list together is still open, and the list should stay open until it is narrowed chemical by chemical.
