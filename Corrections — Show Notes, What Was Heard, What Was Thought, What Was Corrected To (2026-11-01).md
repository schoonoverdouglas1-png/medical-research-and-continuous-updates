## The format — "zero through two"

Every correction entry is numbered 0, 1, 2:

- **0 — What came in (his words as received).** Verbatim, garble intact, never cleaned up first.
- **1 — What Ember thought it was.** The first reading, the first file entry, the first guess — shown even when it was wrong, especially when it was wrong.
- **2 — What it was corrected to be.** The corrected reading, with the source or his confirmation that settles it, and what remains open.

That is the "zero through two" he named: 0 = in, 1 = thought, 2 = corrected. No correction is ever shown as just "2" with 0 and 1 hidden — the hidden step is what makes a correction uncheckable.

## Why this format exists — his account of how his thoughts arrive

His account, Nov 1, 2026, 11:53 PM CST, filed as his:

> His thoughts in his brain actually run 10 or more at a time, and to get his thoughts out they converge always into one set of words somehow.

That is why a single dictation can hold multiple ideas, why a spoken word can carry pieces of two thoughts at once, and why the standing compound-dictation rule (split every idea, lose none) and this show-notes rule belong together: convergence in his head means the output will sometimes blend, and the only honest repair is to show the blend, the first reading, and the correction side by side — not to silently pick one.

Language is imperative here, in his words from the same dictation, because the correction itself is a language act: if the words are wrong, the thought is wrong in the record, and only a visible correction puts it right.
