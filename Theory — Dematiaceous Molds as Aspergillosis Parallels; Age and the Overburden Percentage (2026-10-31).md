## Postulates

### P1 — Dematiaceous molds are a parallel, most likely to aspergillosis [H→D partial]

**Where the parallel holds:**
- Same disease slots: pulmonary disease, sinus disease, central nervous system disease, skin and subcutaneous disease, mycetoma, and disseminated disease in the immunocompromised.
- Same origin: environmental molds from soil, wood, and decaying plant debris, entering by inhalation or traumatic inoculation.
- Same diagnostic pair: culture plus histology — and the pigment is what separates them under the microscope.
- Sinus slot, specifically: dematiaceous fungi are common causes of allergic fungal sinusitis, and may also cause chronic invasive fungal sinusitis and sinus fungus balls — beside *Aspergillus*'s allergic bronchopulmonary slot. Allergy is a shared showing.
- "Phaeohyphomycosis" is the umbrella term: infections caused by pigmented (dematiaceous) fungi that contain melanin in their cell walls.

**Where the parallel breaks:**
- Pigment is the defining split, not a detail: dematiaceous (melanized, phaeohyphomycosis) versus hyaline (non-pigmented, hyalohyphomycosis, where *Aspergillus* sits) are separate categories in the record.
- Different flagship hosts: cerebral phaeohyphomycosis is most common in immunocompetent individuals with no obvious risk factors, while invasive aspergillosis is the disease of neutropenia and profound immunosuppression. The same slots, different favorite hosts.
- Different markers: galactomannan is an *Aspergillus* cell-wall marker with no dematiaceous equivalent.
- Simply put: Same stages, different actors, and the actors are told apart by color — dark-walled versus clear-walled. The parallel is real, and the pigment is why it stops short of identity.

### P2 — The parallel depends on the age of the patient [H→D partial: age-stratified holds, age-determined breaks]

- ***Aspergillus* side, older:** older adults are more prone to fungal infections due to weakened immune function; invasive aspergillosis increasingly affects elderly patients with chronic respiratory diseases and prolonged corticosteroid use; in one pulmonary aspergillosis cohort the age range was 34–72 with mean 56, and structural lung disease was present in 8 of 10 patients; high *Aspergillus*-galactomannan levels were associated with older age.
- **Dematiaceous side, younger:** in a review of 101 cases of primary central nervous system phaeohyphomycosis, the age range was wide but most individuals were young adults with a mean age of 38 years, with male predominance (76%); an immunocompromising condition was noted for only 37%, while over half (52%) had no identifiable underlying condition. The single most common organism was *Cladophialophora bantiana*, implicated in 48% of cases.
- **The honest form:** age shifts which mold and which presentation predominates — older structural lungs toward chronic aspergillosis, younger immunocompetent adults toward cerebral dematiaceous disease — but age is confounded by immune status and lung structure at every step. Age alone does not determine the parallel; age × immunity × lung structure does.
- Simply put: The young brain case is usually the dark mold; the older damaged-lung case is usually Aspergillus. Age points, but immunity and lung structure decide.

### P3 — Disease progression would indicate the overburden percentage of both [H coinage; as an existing measure BREAKS, as a research proposal HOLDS]

- **No metric called "overburden percentage" exists in the record [GAP].** The phrase is filed as his coinage, with three readings kept open for his specification:
- (a) Total fungal burden exceeding the body's clearance capacity, expressed as a percentage over a threshold.
- (b) The relative share of each mold in a mixed burden — e.g., 70% *Aspergillus* / 30% dematiaceous — shifting as disease progresses.
- (c) Each mold's share of the total disease burden.
- **Nearest documented concepts:** galactomannan index (quantitative *Aspergillus* cell-wall antigen, higher levels tracking worse outcomes); fungal DNA by PCR; tissue burden on histology; and progression itself — untreated chronic pulmonary aspergillosis can spread into the bloodstream as fungemia, distributing spores to other parts of the body. Progression is measurable; a two-mold percentage is not yet a standard measure.
- **Verdict:** as a claim that such a percentage already exists and can be read off a progressing case, it breaks [D]. As a proposal — quantify both molds as disease progresses and report their shares — it is testable and stands as a research question [H→D as proposal].
- Simply put: We can measure how much fungus there is, and we can watch disease spread. We do not yet have a standard number for "how much of each mold, as a percentage." That number would have to be invented and then proven.

## Combined statement

Dematiaceous molds parallel aspergillosis across the same disease slots — lung, sinus, brain, skin, disseminated — separated by pigment, by favorite host, and by marker. The parallel is age-stratified: younger immunocompetent adults toward cerebral dematiaceous disease, older adults with damaged lungs toward chronic aspergillosis — with immunity and lung structure deciding alongside age. And disease progression does track fungal burden, but the "overburden percentage of both" is a number still waiting for its definition and its proof, not a reading already on the chart.
