# Viruses of the Streptococcus pneumoniae Package — Best-Known List (2026-10-31)

**Commission:** Douglas, Oct 31, 2026, ~1:40 PM CDT — "Show me a full list of viruses for the streptococcus pneumoniae package, as best known, because there should be more than one virus associated with it."

**Reading:** "package" is kept as his word: the set of viruses traveling with *S. pneumoniae*. The list is kept in three tiers that must not be merged — phages that infect the bacterium, respiratory viruses that co-infect the patient, and his arbopanel association.

**Honest limit on "full":** no single exhaustive named list exists. The named phages below are the documented ones; behind them sits a prophage population of hundreds, clustered into groups, most never given a common name. That population is counted, not hidden.

## Tier A — Bacteriophages that infect *S. pneumoniae* [D]

Named, documented phages (lytic = burst the cell; temperate = integrate as prophage):

- **Dp-1** — lytic, ~56 kb. The classic pneumococcal phage; its PAL endolysin binds choline in the pneumococcal wall.
- **Cp-1** — ~19 kb, Podoviridae (now Madridviridae, genus *Cepunavirus*). Its Cpl-1 endolysin is the textbook pneumococcal phage lysin.
- **EJ-1** — temperate/lytic, ~42 kb. Its endolysin also choline-binding.
- **MM1** — temperate, ~40 kb. Carried by the multidrug-resistant PMEN1 lineage and shown *in vitro* to enhance adherence to human cells.
- **VO1** — listed among the pneumococcal phages alongside Dp-1, Cp-1, EJ-1, MM1.
- **HB-3** — listed among the pneumococcal phages.
- **PH10 and SM1** — streptococcal phages sharing the FOG cell-wall-binding motif with Cp-1, Dp-1, EJ-1, and MM1.
- **K13** — *S. pneumoniae* phage, ~39 kb.
- **SpSL1** — *S. pneumoniae* phage, ~33 kb.
- **phiARI0468-2** — *S. pneumoniae* phage, ~42 kb.
- **phiARI0131-2** — *S. pneumoniae* phage, ~34 kb.

**The population behind the names:**
- A 2025 survey retrieved **205 phage genomes** infecting *Streptococcus* from RefSeq.
- In a 482-genome historical study (1916–2008, 36 countries), **every pneumococcal genome contained prophage DNA**; **286 full-length or putatively full-length prophages** were identified, **163 never previously reported**, clustering into **four major groups** dating from the 1930s–40s onward, integrating at typically five sites. **72% carried the virulence genes *pblA* and/or *pblB***.
- In a 4,003-strain study, only **10.8% lacked temperate phages**; more than **7,000 putative prophages** were found, clustered into **26 groups** by integration site.
- The pneumococcal autolysin LytA is related to phage endolysins — the bacterium's own bursting enzyme is phage kin.

## Tier B — Respiratory viruses that co-travel with pneumococcal disease [D]

These do not infect the bacterium; they infect the patient alongside it, and classically precede secondary pneumococcal pneumonia:

- Influenza A and B (the classic pair — post-influenza pneumococcal pneumonia)
- Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
- SARS-CoV-2
- Rhinovirus
- Parainfluenza viruses
- Adenovirus
- Human metapneumovirus
