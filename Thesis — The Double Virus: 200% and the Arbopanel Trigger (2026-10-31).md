His threshold: for a virology test to read abnormal, the viral DNA present at one time must reach 200% — double.

- Foothold: rise-over-baseline logic is real in serology (see P4). His earlier titer thesis runs on the same doubling mind: "1 over 20 should always show 2 over 20 or 1 over 10" (Oct 18) [H].

- Simply put: Normal is one share. Abnormal needs two shares at once.

### P4 — The reason: the test assumes the virus is going to be there in a latent form after the disease has been cured [H→D principle; "assumes" framing his]

His logic: because the virus (or its shadow) persists after cure, a single detection cannot be the trigger — the test must require double.

- Footholds [D]: herpesvirus latency persists for the life of the host. IgG persists after infection as past exposure — his own arbopanel carries this in Dr. Brown's Sep 25, 2025 note: IgG positive reads as past exposure. Viral RNA can remain detectable by PCR for weeks to months after symptoms resolve, usually noninfectious.
- The logic [LOGIC]: if a baseline (latent virus, memory antibody, residual RNA) is always present, then "found it" cannot distinguish new from old. The test must require a rise over baseline. That is why paired sera with a 4-fold rise is the documented confirmation rule — the same shape as his 200%, with a different number.
- Honest line [D]: tests do not "assume" in the human sense; thresholds are set by assay design and case definitions. The "assumes" wording is his shorthand for baseline persistence [H].
- Verdict: the principle holds [H→D]; the 200% number and the "assumes" framing are his [H].
- Simply put: Because the old virus (or its shadow) is still there after you're cured, a test can't just say "found it." It has to say "found twice as much."

### P6 — The double is one virus in the test tube and one virus within the blood, when it comes to the arbopanel [H]

- Honest lines [D]: the arbopanel is serology — a 2-stage indirect immunofluorescence assay detecting antibodies (IgG and IgM) in serum, not virus particles in the tube. The tube holds blood serum and its antibodies; the virus itself is (or was) in the person. So "virus in the test tube" as literal particles breaks [D]; as his framing — the test reads the blood's response as the virus's presence — it is his [H].
- The panel on file [D]: exactly 8 analytes — California (La Crosse), Eastern Equine, St. Louis, Western Equine, each IgG and IgM. - Verdict: holds as his doubling framework [H]; breaks as literal tube contents [D].
- Simply put: The panel tests blood in a tube for antibodies, not for virus particles. His "double" is one in the blood and one in the reading — filed as his way of seeing it.

## Combined statement

Tests in virology can't just find a virus, because the test knows the old virus (or its shadow) is still there after cure — so abnormal means double: 200% of viral DNA at one time, one over one over zero fold, one in the blood and one in the tube. The record agrees on the reason — baseline persists, so confirmation needs a rise — but the record's rise is four-fold in paired sera, and the panel reads antibodies, not virus particles.
