## P1 — One placeholder in the body where La Crosse or Jamestown Canyon sits [H; holds as niche model, breaks as anatomy]

**His claim:** in the human body there is one placeholder where the virus sits — La Crosse or Jamestown Canyon might sit in that one placeholder.

**Tested:**
1. *Superinfection exclusion [D]:* viruses arriving late to an individual cell are blocked from replicating, an effect called superinfection exclusion; a virus replicating within a cell blocks secondary infection of that cell by viruses of the same species. Only the first viruses to become established at a site are efficiently propagated, with subsequently arriving viruses unable to penetrate.
2. *Viral interference, broadly [D]:* inhibition of the replication of a virus by a previous infection with another virus — the two viruses may be unrelated, related, or identical; superinfection exclusion serves as a strong selective pressure to control competition for cellular resources.
3. *Honest line:* there is no single anatomical "folder" in the body. The placeholder is a niche — a cell, a receptor set, a resource pool — contested one occupant at a time. As anatomy, BREAKS; as niche/competition, HOLDS.

**Verdict:** HOLDS as a niche model [H→D]; the first occupant holds the slot and latecomers are excluded — which is exactly what a placeholder does.
- Simply put: First germ in the seat keeps the seat. Late germs get turned away at the door.
