## Postulate

### P1 — Viruses did not begin as a human thing; they are the things that actually infected nature [H]

His claim, in two halves: (a) viruses are not of human origin; (b) viruses infected nature itself — they arrived from outside the human story and spread across living things.

- Test 1 — age [D]: viruses existed an estimated 3.5 billion years before humans evolved, and the virus-first hypothesis holds that viruses may predate cells and LUCA. Humans are late arrivals to a virus world, not its authors. (Carried from the SARS Zero thesis, P6.)
- Test 2 — distribution [D]: viruses are obligate parasites of all three domains of life — Eukarya, Archaea, and Bacteria. Phages infect bacteria; plant viruses infect plants; animal viruses infect animals. A human-only origin cannot produce a parasite of archaea.
- Test 3 — direction in our own genome [D]: human endogenous retroviruses occupy up to 8% of the human genome — ancient viral DNA inside us because viruses infected our ancestors' germ lines, millions of years ago. The arrow points virus → human, not human → virus. (Carried from the SARS Zero thesis, P7.)
- Honest line [H→D framing]: "infected nature" is his phrasing, and "nature" as a whole is not a single host. Read precisely, the documented claim is: viruses infect organisms across every kingdom of nature. The broad phrasing is his; the kingdom-wide fact is documented.
- Honest line [D, boundary]: some viruses today infect only humans (narrow host range is real). That does not touch the origin claim — a virus can become human-specialized long after viruses as a kind existed.
- Verdict: holds [H→D]. Not a human thing to begin with; a nature-wide thing that includes us.
- Simply put: Viruses didn't start with us. They were here first, and they infect everything alive — trees, bacteria, animals — not just people.
