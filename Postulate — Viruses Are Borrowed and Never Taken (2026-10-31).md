# Postulate — Viruses Are Borrowed and Never Taken (2026-10-31)

**Dictum:** Douglas, Oct 31, 2026, ~2:12 PM CDT — "It would seem as though the viruses are borrowed and never taken."

## Readings

- **Borrowed:** taken up for a time, used, passed along — not owned.
- **Taken:** permanently possessed, owned, removed from circulation.
- Relation: this extends his Oct 18 borrowing/tracking combined statement — borrowing is already his word.

## Tests

### Borrowed — HOLDS [H→D], literally at the cellular level

Viruses carry no metabolism of their own. To replicate, a virus borrows the host cell's ribosomes, polymerases, and membranes — it uses the factory without owning it. That is borrowing in the strictest sense: the machinery is used and left, not carried away.

### Borrowed across hosts — HOLDS [D]

Transmission is a borrowing chain: the virus passes from host to host (and, per today's excretion theory, out through the exit stream to the next host), borrowed by each in turn and owned by none. No host keeps the circulating particle.

### "Never taken" — BREAKS in two documented cases, and the seam reconciles it

- **Endogenous retroviruses:** ~8% of the human genome is retroviral sequence taken permanently into our DNA — syncytin among them. That is taken, not merely borrowed.
- **Latency:** herpesviruses are kept in neurons for life, not just passed through.

**Reconciled form (mine, labeled):** borrowed, never taken — but a copy is kept. The virus particle moves on (borrowed); the body keeps the record — antibody memory, and in the retroviral case, the integrated sequence itself. That reconciles this dictum with his standing doctrine that the body keeps copies of diseases: what is kept is the copy, not the borrowed particle.

- Simply put: We borrow the virus the way a shop borrows a tool — we use it, it moves on, and what stays behind is the record that it was here.
