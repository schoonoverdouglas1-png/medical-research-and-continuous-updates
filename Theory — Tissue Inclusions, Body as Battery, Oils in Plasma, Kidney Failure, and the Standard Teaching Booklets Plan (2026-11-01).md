## Tests — science postulates (P1–P6)

### P1 — Tissue inclusions as the commingling place
**HOLDS as co-presence, BREAKS as merging.**
- Holds: tissues are where co-colonization and co-infection actually happen — multiple organisms, and multiple serotypes of one organism, can be present in the same tissue, biofilm, or respiratory tract at once [D, general]. "Embedded inclusions" as a description of organisms persisting within tissues (intracellular persistence, biofilm, colonized mucosa) has documented footholds [H→D].
- Breaks: commingling does not mean serotypes merge into each other. Capsule switching by genetic recombination is documented *within* a species (e.g. pneumococcal capsule switching), not as meningococcal and pneumococcal capsules blending in tissue into a shared coat [D, from the Nov 1 capsule tests]. Co-presence ≠ fusion.

### P2 — Proteins, sugars, enzymes, lactose, potassium, electrolytes
**HOLDS in substance, with one category correction.**
- Proteins, sugars (including lactose, a disaccharide sugar), enzymes, and electrolytes are all genuinely present in tissues and plasma and genuinely shape microbial life there [D].
- Correction, not a silent fix: **lactose is a sugar, not an electrolyte; potassium is an electrolyte, not a sugar.** Both belong in the list, in different columns. "Electrolytic" as an adjective for the electrolyte system (ion movement, charge balance) holds as a description [H→D].
- Lactose specifically: it is a milk sugar, broken down by lactase into glucose and galactose — both sugars that recur in capsule chemistry (see the meningococcal W/Y second sugars). That is a foothold for lactose belonging in a capsule-adjacent discussion, not proof it builds any capsule [LOGIC — steps: lactose → glucose + galactose; glucose/galactose are capsule monomers in some systems; therefore lactose is upstream of capsule-relevant sugars, not itself a capsule].

### P3 — Body as a battery, chemically
**HOLDS as a model, BREAKS as a literal device.**
- Holds strongly as a model: cells maintain ion gradients across membranes (notably sodium/potassium via Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase), producing membrane potentials; nerves and muscles run on those potentials; the kidney regulates those same ions. Electrochemistry is literally how the body signals and balances [D].
- Breaks literally: the body is not a battery with terminals, a fixed voltage, and a charge that runs down in the same way. It is a set of maintained gradients, constantly rebuilt by metabolism — a battery *analogy*, not a battery *identity*.

### P4 — Different oils suspended in plasma
**HOLDS rewritten.**
- Plasma does not carry free oil droplets in the normal state; it carries lipids packaged as lipoproteins (cholesterol, triglycerides, phospholipids) [D]. "Oils suspended in plasma" holds if read as lipids/lipoproteins in suspension — which is how fats travel in blood — and breaks if read as literal oil floating in plasma.
- This connects to his earlier conjunction work (oils ↔ CRP ↔ lipids ↔ triglycerides ↔ polysaccharides, Nov 1, 2026): the lipid track is real, and lipoproteins are the vehicle. That file's verdicts stand.

### P5 — These things are why kidneys are "all failing in America"
**BREAKS as a universal causal claim; HOLDS as a contributing-mechanism claim.**
- The documented leading causes of chronic kidney disease / kidney failure in the United States are diabetes and hypertension, followed by glomerulonephritis and other causes [D, general — CDC/NIDDK framing]. "All failing" overstates both prevalence and uniformity: many Americans have normal kidney function, and kidney failure has many distinct causes, not one.
- What holds: electrolytes (especially potassium), fluid balance, and blood pressure are exactly what failing kidneys can no longer regulate — so P2–P4 describe the *systems that fail when kidneys fail* and that worsen as they fail, even though they are not the leading *causes* of the failure itself. Cause and consequence must not be swapped: kidney failure causes potassium and fluid problems; potassium and fluid problems alone do not explain the national pattern of kidney failure.

### P6 — Kidney failure is malnutrition first, medicine second
**BREAKS as a ranking; HOLDS in a narrower, rewritten form.**
- As a ranking of causes, it breaks against P5's documented causes: diabetes and hypertension outrank malnutrition as population-level causes of kidney failure in the U.S. [D]. Malnutrition is a major *consequence* of advanced kidney disease (uremic appetite loss, dietary restriction, inflammation) and a *contributor* to poor outcomes, but it is not documented as the first cause for most cases.
- As a narrower claim, it holds in two places: (1) some medicines genuinely injure kidneys — NSAIDs, certain antibiotics, contrast agents, and others are documented nephrotoxic contributors [D] — so "medicine second" has a real, if not ranked-second, foothold; (2) in an individual case, malnutrition and medicine exposure can be the decisive factors, and only that person's clinicians can rank them for that person. Population ranking ≠ individual ranking, and this file ranks neither for any person.
