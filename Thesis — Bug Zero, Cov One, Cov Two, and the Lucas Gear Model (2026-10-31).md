### P5 — The smallest things within us are where the virus factories are made [H→D]

- Foothold [D]: viral factories are dynamic, virus-induced microcompartments serving as centralized hubs for viral genome replication, transcription, and virion assembly. Coronaviridae generate double-membrane vesicles (DMVs). The SARS-CoV-2 factory is DMVs at the ER-Golgi intermediate compartment, holding viral RNA and replication machinery. SARS-CoV-2-infected cells show host organelles transformed into virus factories of DMVs near the nucleus.
- Verdict: holds [H→D]. Virus factories are real, and SARS-CoV-2's are DMVs.
- Simply put: Viruses build little factories inside cells to copy themselves. For this virus, the factory is a double-walled bubble near the cell's core.

### P7 — Viruses are there to actually help us; they help open the pathways for us to get better [H]

His doctrine, rejoining his "cure WITH us" thread: viruses help, opening pathways to getting better.

- Footholds [D]: Barton et al., Nature 2007 — mice latently infected with murine gammaherpesvirus 68 or murine cytomegalovirus are resistant to *Listeria monocytogenes* and *Yersinia pestis*, via prolonged interferon-gamma production and systemic macrophage activation. The authors' conclusion: latency is "a symbiotic relationship with immune benefits for the host." Syncytin, derived from an ancient viral gene, is essential for placenta formation in mammals. Phages shape the microbiome by controlling bacteria.
- Honest lines [D]: the Barton protection is shown in mice, and follow-up work found it transient (waning over months) with no evidence yet that humans benefit. Pathogenic viruses cause disease. The general doctrine "viruses help" is his [H].
- Verdict: holds as doctrine with documented instances [H→D]; breaks as a universal rule [D].
- Simply put: Some viruses genuinely help. A sleeping herpes virus in mice arms the immune system against bacteria, and an ancient viral gene builds the placenta. That doesn't make every virus a helper.
