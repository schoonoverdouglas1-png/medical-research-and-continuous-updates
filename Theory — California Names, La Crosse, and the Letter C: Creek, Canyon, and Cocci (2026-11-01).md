## P1 — California fever and California disease are La Crosse [BREAKS literal; HOLDS as name collision]

**His claim:** California fever and California disease are La Crosse.

**Tested:**
1. *What California fever/disease are:* valley fever's old names — coccidioidomycosis, a fungus (*Coccidioides immitis* / *C. posadasii*), Southwest soil fungus. Established in today's cross-reference table.
2. *What La Crosse is:* a mosquito-borne orthobunyavirus of the California encephalitis serogroup, Upper Midwest, pediatric encephalitis. Different kingdom (fungus vs. virus), different vector (dust vs. mosquito), different geography (Southwest vs. Upper Midwest).
3. *Where his sentence is documented:* "California encephalitis" IS La Crosse's old name — La Crosse disease was initially reported as "California Virus Encephalitis"; the disease is named after La Crosse County, Wisconsin, where the virus was first identified in 1964 following a 1960 outbreak of California encephalitis among children; "Californian encephalitis" is listed as La Crosse encephalitis's other name.

**Verdict:** literal identity BREAKS — California fever/disease (fungal valley fever) are not La Crosse (viral encephalitis). But the collision he is pointing at is REAL and is the finding: the word "California" names three different things — a fungal disease (California fever/disease), a viral disease (California encephalitis = La Crosse), and the whole California serogroup. The name collides; the diseases do not merge. His sentence is wrong as identity and right as collision — and the collision is exactly his letter/name-collision doctrine at work.
- Simply put: California fever is a fungus, La Crosse is a virus. They aren't the same disease. But they share the word California, and that shared word is what tangles them.

## P3 — The letter C: Creek, canyon, and cocci connected through Latin [BREAKS as shared root; HOLDS as letter pattern and by three roads]

**His claim:** all these things connect to the Latin and the letter C, and the C from Creek and canyon might actually connect to the Latin cocci.

**Tested:**
1. *Cocci's root [D]:* the fungus's organisms resembled the protozoan Coccidia, from the Greek kokkis, "little berry"; cocci is the plural of Latin coccus, from Greek kokkos, "berry". The Latin connection is real: Greek kokkos → Latin coccus → cocci, Coccidioides, coccidioidomycosis. The letter C is the Latin alphabet's rendering of the Greek kappa — so "connected to the Latin and the letter C" holds for the cocci family itself.
2. *Creek and canyon roots [D as distinction]:* Creek comes from a Norse water-word (bend, inlet), canyon from a Spanish tube-word — neither derives from kokkos, "little berry." As a shared ROOT, the C-connection BREAKS. Creek, canyon, California, and cocci have different etymologies that happen to share an initial letter in English spelling.
3. *The letter pattern [H→D]:* California, Creek, canyon, cocci, coccidioidomycosis, coccidioidal, coccidiosis, Coccidioides — all C-initial in English. As a spelling pattern, the C-connection HOLDS trivially, and trivially is not nothing: it is his Architecture P8 territory (the letter C colliding across kingdoms), now with a fourth collision — the initial letter itself.

**Verdict:** shared root BREAKS; letter pattern HOLDS; Latin connection HOLDS for cocci (Greek → Latin) but does not extend to Creek, canyon, or California by etymology.
