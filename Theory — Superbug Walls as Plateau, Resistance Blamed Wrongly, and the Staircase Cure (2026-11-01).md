## Tests

### P1 — Resistance blamed wrongly on antibiotics
**BREAKS as a general claim, HOLDS in a narrower form.**
- Breaks generally: antibiotic selection pressure is the documented central driver of resistance patterns [D]. When an antibiotic kills susceptible organisms and leaves resistant ones, the resistant ones become the population — that is selection, not blame, and it is observed in hospitals, communities, farms, and laboratories alike. Resistance also spreads by horizontal gene transfer (plasmids carrying resistance genes between organisms) [D], which is a mechanism, not an alternative to selection — transfer spreads what selection favours.
- Holds narrower: antibiotics are not the *only* driver, and blaming them *alone* would be wrong. Natural resistance exists without human antibiotic use, poor sanitation and infection control spread resistant organisms whether or not a given patient took an antibiotic, and agricultural and environmental antibiotic use contribute alongside human prescribing [D]. So "blamed wrongly" holds if read as "blamed *solely* on antibiotics, as if nothing else contributed" — and breaks if read as "antibiotics do not drive resistance at all."
- This connects to tonight's earlier stewardship discussion (Patient as Broken Car file, P7): stewardship's documented rationale is resistance, and this postulate is his counter-position to that rationale. Both are filed; neither erases the other.

### P2 — Superbug wall as a plateau / double-height stair
**HOLDS as a model, [GAP] as a mechanism.**
- Holds as a model: treatment courses genuinely do hit plateaus — a patient improves, then stalls, then improves again — and some infections genuinely require longer courses, higher doses, or a step up in therapy before resolution [D, general]. The staircase image captures something real about non-linear recovery: progress is not a smooth slope.
- The "wall" language connects to his earlier work: the Oct 31 encapsular-shift theory (the wall hit with the superbug issue is where cure happens, in certain circumstances) and the Nov 1 morning superbug-wall test (wall = polysaccharide shift). Those files are the lineage of this postulate, and they are not re-tested here.
- Mechanism [GAP]: what the "double-height stair" *is* biologically — a capsule shift, a tolerance/persistence state, a biofilm, an immune threshold, a drug-penetration barrier — is not specified in this dictation. Each of those is a different mechanism with different implications, and this file does not choose among them. A plateau that is a biofilm is not treated the same as a plateau that is a capsule shift, so the model cannot substitute for the mechanism.

### P3 — Keep giving the medicine, then the superbug is cured over time
**BREAKS as a universal rule; HOLDS only in the narrow cases where the medicine still works.**
- Breaks universally, and this is the load-bearing correction: if an organism is *resistant* to a medicine — meaning the medicine at achievable concentrations does not kill or inhibit it — then continuing that same medicine does not cure it over time; it selects further for the resistant population and risks toxicity without benefit [D, general]. That is precisely why susceptibility testing exists and why treatment is changed when resistance is found.
- Holds narrowly in three distinct situations, which must not be collapsed into each other:
  1. **The organism is susceptible but the course is long** (e.g. tuberculosis, endocarditis, osteomyelitis): continuing the *effective* medicine for the full course is genuinely how cure is achieved, and stopping early causes relapse [D, general].
  2. **Tolerance/persistence, not resistance:** some organisms survive transiently without being genetically resistant (persisters, biofilm states); prolonged or changed therapy can overcome those states [D, general] — but that is persistence, not resistance, and the distinction matters.
  3. **Step-up therapy:** a "double-height stair" that is climbed by *changing* the medicine, the dose, or the combination — not by repeating an ineffective one. That is a step up, not a continuation.
- **No recommendation is made here about any person's medicine.** Whether a given superbug, plateau, or course should be continued, changed, or stopped is a prescriber's decision for that person, based on susceptibility results and clinical findings. This file tests the general theory only.

## Combined statement (his thought + tested thought)
His staircase image is a good model for something real — recovery stalls, then climbs, and long effective courses do cure — but it cannot carry the universal rule he attaches to it. Resistance is not wrongly blamed on antibiotics in general (selection pressure is documented, with other drivers beside it), and a truly resistant organism is not cured by continuing the medicine it resists; it is cured, when it is cured, by an effective medicine given for a full course, by overcoming persistence, or by stepping up to a different therapy. The plateau is real; the wall's mechanism is still open; and the roles he practices from — patient, researcher, aspiring physician, tribal medicine man, chosen by Chief Claus to travel — are the standpoint from which he sees the staircase, filed as his, not as a credential this file can grant.
