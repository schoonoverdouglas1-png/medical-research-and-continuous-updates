### P2 — The conjunction: oils, C-reactive protein (CRP), lipids, triglycerides, and polysaccharides (sugars) intertwine — and there the conjunctions in medicine are found [H]
**Simply put:** Fats, sugars, and one inflammation protein are all tied to the same germ coats — and that knot is where a lot of medicine meets.

- **Test 1 — CRP ↔ polysaccharide [D, strong foothold]:** CRP was named for its reaction with the pneumococcal C-polysaccharide (the "C" in CRP is the pneumococcal C-polysaccharide / cell-wall teichoic acid component). CRP binds phosphocholine on bacterial surfaces. This is a documented, direct link between an inflammation protein and a bacterial polysaccharide — the strongest leg of the conjunction.
- **Test 2 — oils/lipids/triglycerides ↔ cannabinoids [D, partial]:** Cannabinoids (from cannabis/hemp oil) are lipophilic — they dissolve in and are stored with lipids, and endocannabinoid signaling is lipid signaling. That links oils and lipids to cannabinoids by chemistry (fat-solubility), not by proving a shared disease mechanism. Triglycerides are the body's main fat-storage lipid; they share the lipid track with cannabinoid oils, but a triglyceride is not a cannabinoid and does not become one.
- **Test 3 — polysaccharides as sugars vs. lipids [D, distinction kept]:** Polysaccharides are sugar polymers (capsular polysaccharides on pneumococcus, meningococcus, *Cryptococcus* are the disease-relevant examples here). Lipids/triglycerides are fat molecules, chemically distinct from sugars. Both are energy/storage and structural molecules, and both appear in microbial coats and host membranes — but they do not intertwine by becoming each other. The conjunction is functional (coat chemistry + inflammation + fat-soluble signaling in the same host–microbe encounter), not chemical identity.
- **Verdict: HOLDS as a conjunction of tracks, BREAKS as identity.** Rewritten: *CRP, bacterial capsular polysaccharides, host lipids/triglycerides, and lipophilic cannabinoid oils meet at the same encounter — inflammation protein binding a sugar coat, in a lipid environment where fat-soluble compounds act. That meeting point is a real place to look for medicine's conjunctions; it is not proof that oils, CRP, lipids, and sugars are the same thing.*

### P3 — Where medicine, Latin, and science make sense is found by using his thought process at those conjunctions [H]
