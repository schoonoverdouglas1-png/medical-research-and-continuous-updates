# Source notes — read 2026-11-03 via web index (not live-browser verified unless noted)

1. Jingmenvirus Cameroon 2025 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12723927/ and https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-025-07111-4
   - HdJV in Ae. albopictus, Cx. quinquefasciatus, Cx. wansoni (Yaoundé) and An. gambiae s.l. (Garoua), 2022-2023, 0.9% prevalence; 96.4-98.9% identity to human plasma case same area. Authors: mosquitoes as *potential* vectors alongside ticks; involvement unknown before.
   - JMTV first 2014 from Rhipicephalus microplus, China; since in numerous ticks, mosquitoes, cattle, monkey, rodents, tortoise, humans with tick-bite history.
2. JMTV survey 2019 — https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4915/11/11/1071
   - JMTV/ALSV RNA in Ae. vexans, An. yatsushiroensis, Armigeres sp., Cx. pipiens, Cx. tritaeniorhynchus, lower prevalence than ticks, unknown transmission implications.
3. ALSV Russia — https://WWW.mdpi.com/1999-4915/12/4/362 ; ALSV review — https://microbiol.crie.ru/jour/article/view/18829
   - ALSV in I. persulcatus ticks (Russia), Cx. tritaeniorhynchus and An. yatsushiroensis mosquitoes (China), later I. ricinus (Finland/France); also Ae. vexans, Cx. pipiens pallens in China. Virion ~40.5 nm. 2026 lab study (DOAJ https://doaj.org/article/a397145f16454146b370dd7c9370ab57) shows I. persulcatus competent (microinjection, transovarial + bite to mice) — tick side only.
4. Sindbis tick competence — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2024.1334351 ; EID Finland — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2600146/
   - Mosquito-primary; isolated from Hyalomma marginatum ticks, Sicily (1978). Lab: larval/nymphal Rhipicephalus haemaphysaloides infected feeding on mice, replication in R. haemaphysaloides and Hyalomma asiaticum, midgut to salivary glands, transmission to new host. Experimental, not natural dual cycle.
5. Thogotovirus — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thogotovirus ; ScienceDirect overview https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/neuroscience/thogotovirus
   - Batken: Hyalomma ticks + Aedes/Culex mosquitoes, Kyrgyzstan. Dhori: Hyalomma ticks + Anopheles hyrcanus mosquitoes, Volga estuary, Russia. Thogoto sensu stricto: Amblyomma/Hyalomma/Rhipicephalus ticks.
6. Kenya Ijara — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777299/ ; wildlife — https://www.ijidonline.com/article/S1201-9712(14)00874-1/fulltext ; WNV tick genome — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13071-014-0542-2
   - 10,488 ticks, 1,520 pools: Bunyamwera 44, Semliki forest 25, Ndumu 2, WNV 3, Thogoto 3, Dugbe 5. Authors: previously unreported detection of mosquito-borne SFV and Bunyamwera in ticks; role of ticks unknown / potential only. SFV tick isolate clustered closely to Kenyan mosquito strains. WNV from Rh. pulchellus (cattle) and A. gemma; tick role equivocal.
7. WNV tick experiments — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493942/ (Amblyomma spp., transstadial but no transmission conclusion in snippet) ; https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0817/9/10/780/htm (I. ricinus: replication + transstadial 46.7% males, no transmission by females, unlikely competent vector) ; Lawrie 2004 via https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15200855/ (Ornithodoros moubata transmitted low level, I. ricinus could not maintain; ticks unlikely major role)
   - Ixodid transstadial study: 3 ixodid spp. acquired + transstadially passed WNV but vector competency NOT demonstrated, naive hosts not infected (via https://medluna.com/article/14680122 summary).
8. RVFV ticks — https://anipedia.up.ac.za/resources/rift-valley-fever/1167.html
   - Experimentally inoculated Hyalomma truncatum transmitted by bite, but ticks fed on viraemic hosts unsuccessful; ticks not thought to play role. Mosquito-only in practice.
9. Powassan mosquito — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC4850925/ ; 1982 Ae. aegypti study via https://eurekamag.com/research/044/207/044207418.php
   - POWV did not multiply, persisted ~2 wk in Ae. aegypti. No evidence mosquito competent for human transmission; no human POWV disease by mosquito.
10. CDC MMWR 2024 — https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7529a1.htm
    - Continental US nationally notifiable: 5 mosquito-borne (WNV, La Crosse, Jamestown Canyon, EEE, SLE) and 1 tick-borne (Powassan). 2024: WNV 1,808, POWV 60, LACV 37, JCV 27, EEE 19, SLE 2.
11. NE/Great Lakes POWV+JCV — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12457053/
    - Both in Great Lakes/Northeast, bimodal spring/late summer; POWV Ixodes scapularis/cookei, JCV Aedes/Culiseta/Coquillettidia, deer amplifying host. Co-infection case = two bites, two different viruses, not one shared virus.
12. PNW — https://doh.wa.gov/public-health-provider-resources/notifiable-conditions/arboviral-disease ; https://www.brainkart.com/article/Arbovirus-Disease--Specific-Arboviruses_20607/ ; https://jcvcd.gov/mosquito/surveillance/
    - WA: other than WNV, last locally acquired arboviral infection was SLE 2023, before that WEE 1988. WEE prevalent eastern WA Yakima valley, vector Cx. tarsalis; CTF throughout WA/OR/ID in Dermacentor andersoni; Jackson Co OR tests Cx. pipiens/tarsalis pools for WNV/WEE. No shared-virus record found for Cascades.
13. Senegal co-circulation — https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/tropicalmed/tropicalmed-11-00173/article_deploy/tropicalmed-11-00173-v2.pdf?version=1782985840
    - Sep 2022-Mar 2024: RVFV exclusively in Ae. vexans (Matam), CCHFV in multiple tick spp. (both regions/seasons). Different viruses, same study — not a shared virus.
14. Mechanisms — https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fmicb.2021.773211 (vector specificity, competence definition, salivary glands, midgut barrier) ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9121816/ (tick interface, RNAi little known in ticks, tick saliva/hemocoel bypass hypothesis) ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4188209/ (RNAi, JAK/STAT, Toll, Imd; midgut barrier) ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558446/ (mosquito midgut entry, LDLR family for alphaviruses, conserved receptors) ; https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2019.00022/full (temperature, midgut infection/escape/salivary barriers, nonviremic co-feeding) ; https://ictv.global/report_9th/RNApos/Flaviviridae (flaviviruses 40-60 nm; ~50% mosquito, 28% tick)
15. Size — alphaviruses 60-70 nm via http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/bv.fcgi?rid=mmed.section.2894 ; bunyaviruses ~100 nm and La Crosse body-size conflicting results via https://www.clinicalmicrobiologyandinfection.org/article/S1198-743X(14)60438-9/pdf . No source found stating virion diameter determines mosquito vs tick use.
16. CCHFV — Senegal paper + https://www.impactglobalhealth.org/insights/health-areas/crimean-congo-haemorrhagic-fever-and-rift-valley-fever (Hyalomma ticks; Aedes/Culex mosquitoes for RVFV respectively). No mosquito competence evidence found.
17. SFTSV mosquito negative — via https://infactpublications.com/article/1000203/sftsv-detection-in-blood-sucking-arthropods-and-vector-competence-of-tick-species-for-sftsv : Cx. pipiens pallens, Ae. aegypti, An. sinensis acquired but titres fell, no replication — mosquitoes not vectors.
