## Assistant response — topic-level summary
- Serotype, serogroup, genetic lineage, disease syndrome, and vaccine coverage are different groupings. Serotypes defined by capsular antigens; serogroups collect related serotypes but do not mean members are genetically identical; a single serotype can occur in multiple lineages. Vaccine inclusion is by specific serotype, not simply by shared serogroup or genetic similarity. Antibody protection is generally serotype-specific; do not infer cross-protection from molecular resemblance alone.
- PN23M is a 23-serotype antibody test panel, not a vaccine or disease classification; confirms the 23 types listed, including 9N and 9V, results as type-specific IgG measurements. The "union of 32" is a separate set-comparison claim across the named vaccine formulations; sources retrieved did not verify that exact union, so treat as supplied input, not independently confirmed finding. Evidence does support vaccines target selected capsular serotypes and formulations differ in coverage.
- Serogroups are useful shorthand, but membership needs careful handling. Model did not locate a reliable source verifying every membership in detailed group 9, 6, 15, and 20 lists, or complete proposed group 33 membership. Source found on group 33 discusses 33G as newly characterized type; not enough to validate full membership list supplied.

Disease groupings / serotype patterns (tendencies, not fixed one-to-one mappings; prevalence varies with age, geography, time, vaccine use):
- Historical European pediatric review: meningitis and bacteremia distributions featured 14, 19, 6B, 23F, 18C; also associated 1, 3, 5 with complicated pneumonia in some settings.
- Recent pediatric review: 19F, 3, 19A among frequently reported post-vaccine otitis-media isolates; 19A and 1 prominent in limited post-vaccine community-acquired-pneumonia studies.
- Study- and setting-specific patterns, not universal rankings. Invasive pneumococcal disease = infections at normally sterile sites; pneumonia can be invasive with bacteremia, but pneumonia without bacteremia is non-invasive.

Cross-organism parallels: Meningococcal A/B/C/W/Y/X, H. influenzae a–f, GBS Ia–IX are capsule-based serogroup/type systems; Salmonella serovars and E. coli O:H use different surface-antigen combinations. Cryptococcus A/B/C/D/AD uses separate classification context. Illustrate antigen-based classification broadly, but biological meaning, grouping rules, vaccine implications differ by organism. Measles useful contrast: generally one serotype despite genetic variants. Search did not independently validate every cross-organism label, so conceptual parallels, not equivalent taxonomies.

Features used:
- Molecular: capsule-biosynthesis genes, whole-genome sequence, strain/lineage relationships.
- Antigenic: expressed capsule or other surface antigens used to assign serotype, serogroup, or serovar.
- Vaccine: specific antigens included in a vaccine and protection demonstrated against them.
- Disease: clinical syndrome and observed distribution of typed isolates in defined population and period.
- Closing line: "Keep those layers separate: genetic similarity does not by itself establish antigenic identity, and antigenic relatedness does not by itself establish clinically meaningful vaccine cross-protection."
