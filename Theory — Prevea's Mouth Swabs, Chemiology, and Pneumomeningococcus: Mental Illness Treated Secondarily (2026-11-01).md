## P1 — Prevea's mouth swabs: antidepressant testing by chemiology [H; method holds generally, Prevea-specific GAP]

**His claim:** Prevea Clinic used state-of-the-art mouth swabs to test which antidepressants work, based on people's chemiology — and that parallel inspires his IQ-genetics testing thought.

**Tested:**
1. *The method [D]:* GeneSight is a commercial pharmacogenomic panel that analyzes DNA from a cheek swab to flag genetic variants affecting how the liver processes certain psychiatric medications; for antidepressants, a test is usually done with a cheek swab or saliva sample. The key players are liver enzymes in the cytochrome P450 family, especially CYP2D6 and CYP2C19, which metabolize many antidepressants. Mouth swab + antidepressant + individual metabolism — all three legs hold.
2. *Chemiology = the metabolism profile [H→D]:* his "chemiology" maps to the pharmacogenomic profile — which CYP variants a person carries, hence how they metabolize a drug. Filed as his word for that profile.
3. *Honest limits [D]:* the test can offer clues about how quickly you metabolize a drug, but it cannot reliably tell you which antidepressant will lift your depression; the American Psychiatric Association does not recommend routine combinatorial pharmacogenomic testing to guide antidepressant choice, citing insufficient evidence. The swab informs dosing and tolerability; it does not pick the cure.
4. *Prevea-specific [GAP]:* this check confirmed the method generally, not Prevea Clinic's offering specifically. Whether Prevea used this test, and when, is unconfirmed here — his inspiration account stands as his.

**Verdict:** method HOLDS [H→D]; Prevea-specific offering [GAP]; "state of the art" is his characterization.

## P3 — Pneumomeningococcus: the combined entity [H coinage; one organism, two sites, documented]

**His claim:** there are many pneumococcal pneumoniae, which is a combination of all those things basically — and that is pneumomeningococcus.

**Tested:**
1. *One organism, two sites [D]:* *S. pneumoniae* causes pneumonia (lung) and meningitis (meninges) as well as bacteremia and otitis — one bug, multiple syndromes, by site. A combined lung-plus-meninges pneumococcal entity is therefore one organism in two places, not two diseases stapled together.
2. *Standard neighbor, not a correction:* medicine already has a combined term — invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) — for pneumococcal disease beyond a single mucosal site (bacteremia, meningitis, and related invasive presentations). His "pneumomeningococcus" parallels IPD with the two sites named in the word (pneumo- lung + meningo- meninges + -coccus).
3. *Collision flagged:* standard "meningococcal" means *Neisseria meningitidis*, not "meningeal pneumococcus." His blend uses meningo- for the site, which a standard reader could misread as Neisseria involvement. His definition (pneumococcal lung + meninges) is carried; the collision is flagged, not silently fixed.
4. *"Many pneumococcal pneumoniae" [H]:* many forms/types — filed as his plurality point (many serotypes, many presentations), consistent with his "many" language elsewhere.

**Verdict:** HOLDS as his coinage [H] with a documented core (one organism, two sites) and a standard neighbor (IPD); collision flagged.
