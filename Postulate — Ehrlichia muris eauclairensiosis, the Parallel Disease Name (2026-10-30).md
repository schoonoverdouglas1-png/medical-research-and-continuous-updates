# Postulate — Ehrlichia muris eauclairensiosis, the Parallel Disease Name

Filed: October 30, 2026, at Douglas's direction — his "Yes" at 22:08 CDT, following his 21:31 CDT question.
Status: his coinage and proposal [H]; official names [D]; the parallel name is NOT a published term.

## The three names, kept side by side (old-with-new)

1. **The organism — the whole organism name [D]:** *Ehrlichia muris* subsp. *eauclairensis*, Pritt et al. 2017. Genus *Ehrlichia* (named for Paul Ehrlich, Moshkovski 1945) + species *muris* (Latin genitive of *mus*, mouse — "of a mouse," Wen et al. 1995, type strain AS145T from a wild mouse in Japan, 1983) + subspecies *eauclairensis* (Eau Claire + *-ensis*, "from Eau Claire"; type strain WisconsinT; first discovered in an Eau Claire County resident, 2009).

2. **The whole disease name — official [D]:** ehrlichiosis caused by *Ehrlichia muris* subsp. *eauclairensis*. General disease name: ehrlichiosis. Closest official subspecies-specific label: the case-classification subtype "Ehrlichiosis muris eauclairensis" (CSTE).

3. **The parallel name — his, Latin-based [H]:** *Ehrlichia muris eauclairensiosis*. Formed by taking the subspecies epithet *eauclairensis* and swapping the place-adjective ending *-ensis* for the disease-noun ending *-ensiosis* (*-osis*, a state or process), keeping the full genus + species in front.

## The postulate, stated formally [H]

A disease caused by a named subspecies deserves a subspecies-specific parallel disease name, formed by *-osis* on the subspecies epithet, kept alongside the official genus-level disease name — never replacing it. The official name stands; the parallel name stands beside it, defined on first use.

## Tested three ways

1. **Nomenclature systems [D]:** organism names (bacterial code, ICNP) and disease names (ICD/CSTE) are separate systems. A trinomial cannot be a disease name as published; the disease name is ehrlichiosis. Verdict: the distinction holds; official status for the coinage fails — it is a proposal, not a term of record.

2. **Precedent [D]:** disease names from organism stems + *-osis* are standard — ehrlichiosis from *Ehrlichia*, coccidioidomycosis from *Coccidioides*, candidiasis from *Candida*, aspergillosis from *Aspergillus*. But precedent uses the genus stem, never the full species/subspecies trinomial. Verdict: the suffix logic is precedented; the full-trinomial construction is novel — his form is a new step, not a repeat.

3. **The record's own answer [D]:** the classification tables already carry a subspecies-specific disease label, "Ehrlichiosis muris eauclairensis" — disease word first, organism words after, unfused. The need his coinage answers is real and already half-met officially; his fused one-word form compresses that label further. The 2011 New England Journal of Medicine paper likewise calls the illness "human ehrlichiosis" even for the new agent. Verdict: the parallel name sharpens, but does not replace, what the record already does.

## Simply put

The germ has a three-part Latin name ending in "from Eau Claire." The sickness it causes is officially called ehrlichiosis. His parallel name takes the germ's last word and turns it into a sickness word, so the Eau Claire part stays in the disease name too — official name first, his name beside it, both kept.
