- Doug (3 days ago): "cyanoborohydride"
- Claude: Cyanoborohydride is anion BH₃CN⁻, most commonly encountered as sodium cyanoborohydride (NaBH₃CN), mild reducing agent used in organic chemistry. Key points: milder than NaBH₄ — electron-withdrawing cyano group makes it less reactive, so it doesn't readily reduce aldehydes and ketones at neutral to mildly acidic pH (~6-7); main use is reductive amination — at around pH 6-7 it selectively reduces iminium ions over carbonyls, so aldehyde or ketone can be mixed with amine and imine reduced in situ in one pot; stable in acid and protic solvents (unlike NaBH₄, it tolerates methanol and mildly acidic conditions); other uses include reducing tosylhydrazones and bioconjugation (e.g., Schiff base reduction when labeling proteins). Safety: acidifying it (below ~pH 3-4) can release hydrogen cyanide gas, and reagent and its waste are toxic; work in fume hood and dispose of waste per institution's cyanide protocols. Sodium triacetoxyborohydride (STAB) is common, less toxic alternative for reductive aminations. Claude asked whether Doug wanted a procedure, mechanism, or alternatives.
