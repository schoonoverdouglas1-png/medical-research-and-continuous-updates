## 2. Serogroup vs serotype distinction
Serogroups defined by antigenic cross-reactivity of capsular polysaccharides, not genetic lineage. Vaccine containing one serotype (e.g. 9V) does not necessarily confer protection against other members of same serogroup unless explicitly demonstrated.
- 9: 9A, 9L, 9N, 9V
- 33: 33F, 33A, 33B, 33C, 33D, 33E, 33G, 33H
- 6: 6A, 6B, 6C, 6D, 6F, 6G, 6H (6E later refuted)
- 15: 15A, 15B, 15C, 15D
- 20: 20A, 20B, 20C

## 3. Disease-specific serotype patterns
- Invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD): 1, 3, 4, 5, 7F, 14, 19A, 19F, 23F, 33F (high-incidence in adults). PCV13 covers 1, 3, 4, 5, 6A, 6B, 7F, 9V, 14, 18C, 19A, 19F, 23F; PCV20 adds 8, 10A, 11A, 12F, 15B; PCV21 adds 15A, 15C, 16F, 17F, 20A, 22F, 23A, 23B, 24F, 31, 35B (broadest coverage).
- Pneumonia (non-invasive): 6B, 19F, 23F, 14, 19A (common in children). PCV7/13 dramatically reduced these in childhood pneumonia.
- Otitis media: 6B, 19F, 23F, 14, 15B (children). PCV13 reduced acute otitis media episodes by ~7% vs unvaccinated.
- Meningitis: 1, 3, 4, 5, 7F, 14, 19A, 23F (high case-fatality). PCV13 retains strong protection against serotype 3 meningitis; PCV15/20/21 add coverage for 15A/15C and 33F, emerging causes of adult meningitis.
- Invasive disease in older adults: 8, 10A, 11A, 12F, 15B, 22F, 33F (post-PCV13 era). PCV20 and PCV21 uniquely cover these, accounting for 54-85% of adult IPD.

## 4. Parallel serotype/serogroup systems (illustrative contrast)
- Meningococcus: A, B, C, W, Y, X; meningitis, septicemia; quadrivalent conjugate (A/C/W/Y) plus MenB protein-based.
- Hib: a-f; epiglottitis, meningitis, otitis media; Hib (type b) conjugate only.
- Salmonella: hundreds of serovars (Typhi, Typhimurium); gastroenteritis, typhoid; typhoid vaccine (Vi) covers only Typhi.
- E. coli: O-antigen (O:H), ~180 O groups; UTIs, diarrhoea, neonatal meningitis; no broad O-group vaccine.
- Cryptococcus: A, B, C, D, AD (capsular glucuronoxylomannan); meningoencephalitis; no licensed vaccine; serotype A predominates in HIV patients.
- GBS: Ia-IX; neonatal sepsis, adult bacteremia; no vaccine yet.
- Measles: single serotype (genetically monotypic); live-attenuated vaccine protects against sole serotype.
Conclusion: antigenic serogrouping does not guarantee cross-protection; each vaccine must be evaluated for specific serotypes it contains.

## 5. Molecular, antigenic, vaccine commonality
- Molecular: gene-level phylogeny, whole-genome sequence. 6A and 6B genetically close, but 6E was later shown not to be true separate serotype; molecular data alone do not dictate vaccine inclusion.
- Antigenic: same polysaccharide epitopes recognized by antibodies; defines serogroups. Group 9 shares some epitopes, yet only 9V is in PCV13/15/20, while 9N appears only in PCV21 and PPSV23.
- Vaccine: actual serotypes present in formulation, determined by regulatory approval, not serogroup membership. PCV15 includes 22F and 33F even though other 33-group members are absent.
