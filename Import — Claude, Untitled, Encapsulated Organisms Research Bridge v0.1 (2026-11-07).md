## The document (Version 0.1, dated 7 November 2026)
Title: "ENCAPSULATED ORGANISMS — Comparative Polysaccharide Architecture, Immune Evasion, and Pulmonary Ecology: A parallel research bridge linking Cryptococcus and Streptococcus." Status: "Initial evidence synthesis and testable research framework." Relationship: "Additive companion; does not modify or replace existing workbooks or audit documents."

- §1 Executive summary: Examines whether Cryptococcus and selected encapsulated Streptococcus species share useful comparative features in capsule chemistry, host immune interactions, and pulmonary ecology. Primary bacterial comparator is Streptococcus pneumoniae, because its capsular polysaccharide (CPS) is major virulence determinant, is organized into serotypes, and is target of licensed pneumococcal polysaccharide and conjugate vaccines. Strongest established commonality is functional rather than taxonomic or structural: both cryptococcal and pneumococcal capsules can reduce effective immune clearance, including interference with phagocyte-mediated defense and opsonic pathways. Their capsule architectures, biosynthesis, antigenic classification systems, and clinical biology are nevertheless distinct. Proposed organism-to-organism relationship remains unproven: published case reports document rare Cryptococcus neoformans–S. pneumoniae co-infection in cerebrospinal fluid, but these do not demonstrate preferential pulmonary co-occurrence, direct microbial communication, or causal interaction. More defensible model is that shared host conditions may influence probability, severity, or detection of different infections.
- §2 Research question and hypothesis hierarchy: Primary question — do Cryptococcus and encapsulated Streptococcus preferentially occur in overlapping host and tissue contexts, and are any associations explained by shared immune-state, tissue-environment, or microbiome variables rather than direct organism-to-organism interaction? Working hypothesis — host immune state and pulmonary microenvironment may act as common determinants of susceptibility; capsule-mediated immune evasion may be convergent functional strategy, even where underlying polysaccharide structures are chemically unrelated. Three claims that must remain separate: A. Comparative mechanism — "Supported in broad terms; mechanisms differ and require endpoint-specific comparison." B. Shared host context — "Biologically plausible; established risk factors exist for cryptococcosis, but joint risk needs direct study." C. Organism interaction — "Not established. Requires direct co-culture, model, or clinical evidence."
- §3 Organism scope and classification controls (table): Cryptococcus neoformans / C. gattii — encapsulated basidiomycetous yeasts (fungi); GXM-dominant capsule; historical serotypes A, B, C, D, AD and molecular/genotypic groupings; "Do not treat fungal serotypes or molecular types as equivalent to bacterial capsule serotypes." Streptococcus pneumoniae — encapsulated Gram-positive bacterium; capsular polysaccharide serotypes; related serogroups; capsule-locus and lineage data; "Keep capsular serotype, serogroup, sequence type and clonal complex as separate fields." S. agalactiae (optional extension) — capsular serotypes Ia, Ib, II–IX; "Lancefield group B is not a capsular serotype." S. pyogenes (optional extension) — Lancefield group A; emm/M-protein typing; "Do not assume that all strains have a pneumococcus-like capsule serotyping system."
- §4 Comparative capsule chemistry: Cryptococcus — GXM-dominant capsule, α-(1→3)-mannan backbone with glucuronic-acid and xylose substitutions; mannose O-acetylation contributes to structural and antigenic variation; smaller GalXM/GXMGal fraction and mannoproteins; "The complete capsule cannot be reduced to one invariant repeating-unit formula." S. pneumoniae — CPS highly diverse; each serotype defined by its capsular antigen with specific polysaccharide structure; biosynthesis encoded by cps locus; capsule impedes opsonophagocytic clearance and is antigenic basis of pneumococcal capsular serotyping and current polysaccharide/conjugate vaccine design. Five-row table (building blocks; linkages/repeat units; polymer organization; antigenicity; immunological function) states what can and cannot be compared — e.g., "A shared mannose or glucose residue does not mean shared antigen" and "Chemical resemblance does not prove serological cross-reactivity."
- §5 Host immunity (table): Macrophages/phagocytosis, complement, antibody, inflammation/cytokines — convergent function, distinct mechanisms; e.g., "Do not infer cross-protection from shared sugars or carrier proteins."
- §6 Pulmonary ecology and co-occurrence question: Cryptococcus is acquired by inhalation and commonly presents in lungs before dissemination; pneumococcus commonly colonizes nasopharynx and can cause lower-respiratory and invasive disease. Targeted literature search identified rare case reports of C. neoformans and S. pneumoniae co-infection in cerebrospinal fluid (reports from 1998 and 2015); no robust population-level evidence of preferential pulmonary co-occurrence was identified — "a gap to investigate, not proof that co-occurrence never occurs." "Sentinel-organism" interpretation is treated only as hypothesis about biomarkers of host or tissue state.
- §7 Testable hypotheses H1–H5: H1 — shared host immune-state variables explain some co-occurrence or sequential infection patterns. H2 — capsule phenotype predicts defined differences in complement deposition and phagocyte uptake across organisms. H3 — capsule chemical features explain within-organism antigenic variation. H4 — pulmonary inflammation or microbiome changes alter susceptibility to one or both organisms. H5 — direct inter-organism interaction modifies growth, capsule expression, or host response. Each with design/measurement and evidence needed.
