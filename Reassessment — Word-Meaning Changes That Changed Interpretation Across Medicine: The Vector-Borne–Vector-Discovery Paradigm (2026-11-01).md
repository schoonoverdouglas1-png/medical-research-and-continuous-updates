## The paradigm (his, from the 11:30 AM theory)

- **Vector-borne:** written, borne = carried — correct. Spoken, borne = born (homophones) — implies the virus was *born* in the vector, and implies the vector *caused* the disease. The word smuggles in birth and causation.
- **Vector discovery:** the vector is where the virus was *found and identified* — discovery site, not birthplace, not cause. The vector is a carrier, a component (a slice of Rothman's pie), not the causation.
- **The paradigm question, applied to every term below:** did the word's meaning imply a birth, a place, or a cause that the discovery later disproved — and did the interpretation change while the word stayed, or did the word change while the interpretation stayed?

## The yardstick — liturgy (his definition, resolving the Nov 1 flag)

**Liturgy, per Douglas:** the actual scientific definition of what the grouping of words means, in a way that always makes sense at the highest logical level.

Applied here: a medical term passes the liturgy test when its word-grouping still makes sense at the highest logical level after the discovery is known. A term fails when its words imply something the discovery disproved (birth, place-as-cause, agent-as-whole-pie) yet the term keeps being read that way. The table below grades each term against that yardstick.

## Findings — the pattern across the table

1. **Kept-words dominate.** Medicine usually keeps the old word and changes the interpretation underneath it (malaria, influenza, virus, immunity, cancer, sepsis). The word becomes a fossil of a disproved theory, and every new student must learn to read past it. That is the paradigm's main finding: interpretation changes far more often than words do.
2. **Place-words collide.** California, La Crosse, Creek, canyon — place names attach to diseases, then collide across diseases, then mislead by implying the place caused the disease. His American-first, Latin-second doctrine and his place-of-discovery critique are the remedy he already stated.
3. **Birth/cause implications are the recurring false note.** Borne→born, vector→cause, presence→disease, name→answer (idiopathic), time→fate (chronic). In each case the word implies more causation than the discovery supports — and in each case his elements doctrine (slices, not the pie) is the correction.
4. **Syndrome passes liturgy.** "Running together" is exactly what a disease name does in his system: groups symptoms. The word that best survives his highest-logical-level test is the one that admits it is a grouping.
5. **His terms earn their places as companions, not replacements.** "Vector discovery" fixes birth/cause but drops carriage; "causology" names the grouping but is not standard vocabulary; "liturgy" as defined here is the yardstick itself. Each is filed as his, beside the standard term, old-with-new.
