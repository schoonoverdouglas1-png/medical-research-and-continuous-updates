## PART 3 — MEETING HANDOUT (Shared Enclosure for Both Recipients)

### The core proposal in 30 seconds
The Mayo Clinic PN23M panel (*Streptococcus pneumoniae* IgG, 23 Serotypes) is currently used to audit vaccine response after the fact. SP23D proposes using it as a *real-time monitor* of ongoing antigenic load and systemic immune pressure. Titers are not just memory. They are engagement. [H]

*Simply put:* The test you already take after a vaccine could also be used, months later, to watch whether the load is still there. That is the idea. Everything else in this paper is how to test whether that idea is true.

### 1. The test itself — HOLDS [D]
PN23M measures IgG antibodies to 23 pneumococcal serotypes in serum, by bead-based multiplex immunoassay [D — Mayo Clinic Laboratories, PN23M]. The 23 are the PPSV23 set: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6B, 7F, 8, 9N, 9V, 10A, 11A, 12F, 14, 15B, 17F, 18C, 19A, 19F, 20, 22F, 23F, 33F [D]. It is a laboratory-developed test, not cleared or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration [D]. Its stated uses are assessing IgG response to pneumococcal vaccination and determining the ability to produce antibody to polysaccharide antigens, as part of evaluating humoral or combined immunodeficiencies [D].

### 2. Two testing windows
**A. Original window, 4–6 weeks after vaccination — HOLDS [D].**
Antibody responses develop in approximately 75% to 85% of nonimmunocompromised adults and older children approximately 4 to 6 weeks following immunization with purified polysaccharide vaccines [D — Mayo PN23M]. At 4–6 weeks the question is: *Did the vaccine work?* Elevation is expected from immunization.

**B. SP23D window, 6+ months after vaccination — [H], [GAP] for the interval.**
Douglas's proposal: wait 6 months and beyond, let the vaccine-driven surge clear, and what remains is ongoing, active engagement driven by continuous capsular antigen presence. [H]
No published SP23D window exists, and no source was found stating a 6-month vaccine-surge clearance rule for PN23M. The 6-month figure is his postulate, not a documented interval. [GAP]
A second gap sits beside it: Douglas's pneumococcal vaccination history and dates are not in the record on file. Without vaccination dates, no panel can be timed as "6+ months post-vaccine" at all. That history is needed before the window claim can be tested on his own panels. [GAP]

*Simply put:* The first window is in the lab's own book — about a month after the shot, check if it worked. The second window is Douglas's idea — wait six months, then check if anything is still driving the numbers up. The six months is his idea, not the lab's, and we do not yet have his shot dates to time it.

### 3. Threshold one: ≥0.3 µg/mL per serotype — BREAKS as a documented cutoff; HOLDS as a postulate to test
**What the record actually says, three ways:**
1. The WHO correlate of protection for invasive pneumococcal disease in infants is **0.35 µg/mL** of serotype-specific IgG [D]. That is protection, not illness.
2. Serotype-specific correlates are higher for some serotypes: **0.78–2.83 µg/mL** for 1, 3, 7F, 19A, 19F, and **0.14–0.20 µg/mL** for 6A, 6B, 18C, 23F [D]. On the Luminex platform the equivalent for 19A is **0.12 µg/mL** [D].
3. Quest's own report on Douglas's October 2026 panel says: published data suggest protection usually occurs at **≥0.3–0.50 µg/mL** for healthy children receiving conjugate vaccines, and **≥1.3 µg/mL** may be more relevant after polysaccharide vaccines or for immunocompromised patients [D — Quest report text]. Mayo's PN23M report gives **≥1.0 µg/mL** as its normal mark, with a ≥2-fold rise or ≥1.0 in >70% of serotypes (adults) consistent with a normal vaccine response [D — Mayo PN23M report].

**SP23D inverts that.** It reads ≥0.3 as active illness: continuous capsular antigen actively driving plasma-cell output, rather than sterile memory. [H]
That inversion is the paper's central claim, and it is the claim most in need of validation, because it runs directly against the documented protective meaning of the same number. It must be presented to Mayo and Quest as a postulate to be tested — in retrospective cohorts, stratified by time since vaccination, against CRP, symptoms, and clinical burden — not as a documented cutoff. At the 51% bar it enters as a postulate with a documented counterweight beside it; it does not enter as a finding.

**What his own panels show at ≥0.3 (arithmetic only, no clinical meaning):**
- Mayo, Oct 29, 2025: **11 of 23 serotypes ≥0.3**; 1 of 23 ≥1.0.
- Quest, Oct 26, 2026: **17 of 23 serotypes ≥0.3**; 8 of 23 ≥1.0.
- The jump from 11 to 17 is arithmetic. Whether it means anything clinically belongs to his clinicians, not to this paper.

### 4. Threshold two: the Systemic Load Index — BREAKS as written; the correction is the point
**The finding, three ways:**
1. Mayo's PNTOR test definition — the *total* pneumococcal IgG test — gives reference values **≥9.7 mcg/mL**, with low concentrations (<9.7) indicating a poor response to the pneumococcal vaccine [D — Mayo PNTOR, Nov 5, 2026 test definition]. That 9.7 is a **total IgG** value.
2. A total IgG value and a sum of 23 serotype-specific IgGs are different quantities, from different assays. Borrowing one number into the other is a category error. [LOGIC]
3. Douglas's own panel sums do not produce 9.7: Mayo sum ≈ **6.9 µg/mL** (two values <0.1, treated as ~0.05–0.1); Quest sum **26.7 µg/mL** (his assumption of 0.2 for each of six <0.3 values; the honest range is 25.5–27.24 if those six are 0–0.29). Neither sum is 9.7, and neither sum was derived from 9.7.

**Verdict:** 9.7 as a *sum-of-23* threshold **breaks**. It cannot stand as written. In this version it is kept only in its correct place: "9.7 is the Mayo total-IgG reference value. Whether a sum-of-23 index has any equivalent threshold is [GAP] — no derivation on file."

**Ember's addition, labelled as hers:** the Quest sum is dominated by a single serotype, **10A = 10.7 µg/mL** (Mayo 2025: 10A = 1.6, still the highest in that panel). A sum index that one serotype can dominate by 40% is not a load index; it is a 10A index with noise attached. Any future index needs a per-serotype cap, a median, or a count of active serotypes alongside the sum — or it will mislead. That is Ember's judgment, not Douglas's, and it is filed as hers.

*Simply put:* The 9.7 number came from a different test — one that adds everything up into a single total. This paper was using it as if it were the sum of the 23 separate numbers. Those are not the same thing. The paper now says so, and asks Mayo and Quest to help find what the right sum-number, if any, actually is.

### 5. Why serotypes matter beyond pneumococcus — the six tracks
- **Vaccine track [D]:** PCV7 / PCV13 / PCV15 / PCV20 / PCV21 and PPSV23 cover overlapping, non-identical serotype sets. PCV20's carrier is CRM197 only. The union across products is 32 serotypes, a calculated union, not a canonical quote.
- **Infection track [H→D]:** high-risk serotypes in chronic mucosal niches show beta-lactam insensitivity via mosaic PBP alterations, biofilm co-aggregation (notably with *S. aureus* and *Candida albicans*), capsular shedding that inhibits opsonization, and structural cross-links to fungal cell-wall polysaccharides.
- **Disease track:** pneumococcal disease is the disease named by the panel. No other disease is claimed from this panel alone. [GAP for any wider disease claim]
- **Illness / sickness tracks:** the paper's "systemic burden," "fluid-matrix friction," "immunological congestion," and "profound fatigue" language is Douglas's description of illness and sickness as he experiences and frames them [H]. No CRP, symptom score, or clinical burden correlation is in this paper to tie those words to a measured outcome. That correlation is proposed as validation step 2, not claimed as done. [GAP]
- **Virus track:** empty in this paper. No virus claim is made from PN23M, and none is smuggled in through the fungal or bacterial claims. [GAP — by design, not by omission]

### 6. Featured serotype: 19A — [PARTIAL]
**Bacterial side — HOLDS [H→D].**
19A–ST320 is a multidrug-resistant lineage, clonal complex 320 / GPSC1, resistant to penicillin, macrolides, cephalosporins, and increasingly carbapenems, via mosaic pbp1a / pbp2b / pbp2x [D — Lee et al., PLoS One 2025; Microorganisms 2026 review]. It is a post-PCV7 replacement driver [D]. In adult invasive disease series it is among the most common serotypes, and penicillin non-susceptibility is enriched among 19A and 19F [D].

**Fungal side — [PARTIAL].**
*Candida albicans* and *Streptococcus* spp. form mucosal biofilms together — documented for the mitis group, *S. oralis*, *S. gordonii*, and *S. mutans*, with co-aggregation, shared mannan / glucan architecture, and synergy in mucosal biofilms [D — Morales & Hogan, PLoS Pathog 2010; Candida–streptococcal mucosal biofilm study, Mol Oral Microbiol 2015]. **No 19A-specific *Candida* co-existence study was found.** The claim "19A co-exists with *Candida* mucosal biofilms" is therefore Douglas's hypothesis built on that general co-localization, not a documented 19A finding. [GAP for 19A-specific]
Meta's version said 19A "proves both sides at once." Ember's version downgrades that to [PARTIAL]: it proves the bacterial side, and it *suggests* the fungal side by analogy. That is the honest form of the claim at the 51% bar.

**His own 19A titers — transcribed only:**
Mayo 2025: **0.3 µg/mL**. Quest 2026: **0.6 µg/mL**. Both ≥0.3, both <1.0. 19A is not his highest serotype in either panel. 10A is, in both.

**Correction — Ember's, labelled as hers:** "methicillin-resistant" does not apply to pneumococcus. Methicillin resistance is *Staphylococcus aureus* mecA. For pneumococcus the correct term is penicillin / macrolide / carbapenem non-susceptibility via mosaic PBP. The first draft's "methicillin-resistant phenotypes" is corrected everywhere in this version.

**The other candidate, for the record:** Meta ranked serotype **3** first for signal clarity — it sheds free capsular polymer, evades opsonization, and co-aggregates with *S. aureus* [H→D]. Douglas chose 19A because he wants the one that proves fungal *and* bacterial elements. That choice is his, and it is kept. Serotype 3 is kept in the table as the runner-up, not erased. Old with new.

*Simply put:* 19A is a tough, drug-resistant strain — that part is solid. The part about it living together with Candida in a biofilm is a good guess built on how Candida and strep germs generally live together, but nobody has published that exact 19A-and-Candida pair yet. So the paper says "partly proven," not "proven."

### 7. What SP23D would change clinically — [H], proposed, not decided
*Current model:* one-time post-vaccine audit → immune / not immune. [D]
*SP23D model, proposed:* serial PN23M tracking at 6+ months post-vaccination → (1) identify active serotypes ≥0.3, (2) calculate a Systemic Load Index, (3) track load over time with symptoms and inflammatory markers, (4) use the serotype pattern to infer biofilm / fungal co-engagement, (5) guide integrated diagnostic tracking and combined therapeutic strategies. [H]
No treatment is proposed in this paper. No medication change is proposed. Every therapeutic question goes to his prescriber.

### 8. Next steps proposed for Mayo + Quest review
1. Validate any sum-of-23 index in retrospective PN23M cohorts, stratified by time since vaccination, with vaccination dates on file.
2. Correlate any index with CRP, symptom scores, and clinical burden.
3. Test the fungal–bacterial co-localization postulate in biofilm models, 19A-specific.
4. Define a serial monitoring interval only after (1) is done.
5. Evaluate laboratory feasibility for standardized SP23D reporting at 6+ months.

### Closing statement for discussion
SP23D does not replace existing pneumococcal interpretation. It adds a second layer — *if validated*: a quantifiable, trackable measure of systemic toll, made accurate by waiting for the vaccine response to clear. If validated, it gives clinicians a tool to see chronic, multi-kingdom persistence that single-pathogen testing misses. [H]
