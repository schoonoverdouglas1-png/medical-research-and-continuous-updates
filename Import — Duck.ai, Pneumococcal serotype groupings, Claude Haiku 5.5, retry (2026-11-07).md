Serogroups are antigenic groupings, not genetic clades:
- Serogroups defined by shared antigenic epitopes in capsular polysaccharide, so not direct readout of gene similarity. Serotype 6A and 6B antigenically related within group 6 and both in PCV13, but capsule biosynthesis loci differ, so antibodies raised against one do not reliably cover the other. Same logic applies to group 9 (9A, 9L, 9N, 9V), group 15 (15A–15D), group 20 (20A–20C). Response says serogroup assignment in prompt is reasonable antigenic framing, although group 33 membership (33E/G/H) and 6E refutation should be checked against current reference, since it did not verify them.

Molecular, antigenic, vaccine commonality distinct:
- Molecular similarity: capsule biosynthesis gene sequence and clonal lineage. Clonal complexes can contain several serotypes, serotype switching within a clone is common.
- Antigenic similarity: shared epitopes detected by serology. Defines serogroups, but can be partial, asymmetric, or affected by O-acetylation and linkage.
- Vaccine commonality: which serotypes a product includes. Manufacturing choice, selected by disease burden and immunogenicity, not by molecular or antigenic relatedness.
- Molecular closeness does not establish cross-reactivity. Two serotypes can share a clone and yet elicit little cross-protection, while antigenically related serotypes may still need separate inclusion in a vaccine.
