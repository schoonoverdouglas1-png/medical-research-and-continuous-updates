Under heading "The Trap of Panglossian Biology": "Your caution is spot on: it is dangerously easy to slide from 'this response has an evolutionary logic' to 'therefore, this response is always wise and beneficial.'" Under heading "From Warfare to Stewardship": "Shifting the primary question from 'What is the bad organism?' to 'What is the system trying to accomplish?' fundamentally changes how we conceptualize healing." Two models are contrasted. Reductionist (Warfare) Model: "Treats the body as a battleground. The goal is total eradication—kill the bug, suppress the pathway, block the receptor. Instead of just asking how to silence a fever or block an immune signal, it asks what systemic imbalance is triggering the response, and whether supporting the system—through rest, hydration, nutrition, or targeted modulation of an overactive pathway—will resolve the cause more safely than outright suppression." It adds: "This doesn't mean we throw out pharmacology or antibiotics—when a bacterial load is overwhelming, you use them. But it does mean we stop treating every symptom as an enemy to be crushed.
