# List — Detectable Diseases Based Upon Mouth Swabs (2026-11-01)

**Request (Douglas, Nov 1, 2026, ~12:14 PM CST):** a list of the detectable diseases based upon mouse swabs, for him to think about parallels from as an informational data set.

**Reading carried:** "mouse swabs" = mouth swabs, as established in the previous turn (Prevea cheek-swab context). Three different mouth swabs are separated below — they detect different things, and collapsing them would corrupt the data set:
- **Cheek (buccal) swab** — collects cheek cells for DNA. Detects gene variants: risk, carrier status, and drug metabolism. It does NOT detect an active infection.
- **Saliva sample** — detects pathogens and antibodies present in saliva/oral fluid.
- **Throat swab** — detects pathogens living on the tonsils and posterior pharynx.

**Detection vs. risk (read before using the list):** DNA results mean variant present / risk / carrier status — not "has the disease now." Pathogen swabs mean the organism (or its antibodies) was present in that sample at that time. A cheek swab and a throat swab answer different questions.

**Headline scale:** according to the CDC, there are more than 2,200 diseases that can now be detected through genetic testing. Saliva or blood give the same DNA with the same accuracy — so a cheek swab's DNA list is, in principle, the genetic-testing list, sampled from the cheek.

## A. Cheek-swab DNA — hereditary, cancer-risk, metabolic, carrier (variants/risk, not active disease)

**Single-gene and hereditary conditions (examples):**
- Cystic fibrosis
- Sickle cell disease / sickle cell anemia
- Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)
- Huntington's disease
- Duchenne muscular dystrophy
- Tay-Sachs disease
- Thalassemia
- Maple syrup urine disease
- Phenylketonuria (PKU)
- Ornithine transcarbamylase (OTC) deficiency
- Gaucher disease
- Wilson disease
- Porphyrias (acute intermittent porphyria, hereditary coproporphyria, variegate porphyria, ALAD deficiency, familial porphyria cutanea tarda, hepatoerythropoietic porphyria, erythropoietic protoporphyria, X-linked protoporphyria, congenital erythropoietic porphyria) — DNA testing requires only buccal (cheek) swab
- Down syndrome (chromosomal — detectable by genetic testing; prenatal context differs from cheek-swab screening)
- Spina bifida (associated variants/risk — not a single-gene cheek-swab diagnosis)
- Celiac disease (genetic predisposition, HLA-DQ types — risk, not diagnosis by swab alone)
- Alzheimer's disease (risk variants, e.g., APOE — risk, not diagnosis)

**Cancer predisposition (risk variants):**
- Breast cancer risk (BRCA1/BRCA2)
- Colon cancer risk (e.g., Lynch syndrome genes)
- Prostate cancer risk (hereditary variants)

**Heart-gene panels by cheek swab (example panel scans 48 heart genes):**
- Hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR, TTR gene)
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (MYBPC3, MYH7)
- Dilated cardiomyopathy (TTN, LMNA)
- Rhythm conditions (panel-dependent)
- Storage diseases mimicking heart disease: Fabry disease (GLA), Danon disease (LAMP2)

**Carrier screening (healthy carrier, no symptoms — recessive/X-linked, 1,008 genes across 13 clinical categories in one cited panel):**
- All single-gene conditions above, as carrier status — including cystic fibrosis, sickle cell, SMA, Tay-Sachs, thalassemia, PKU, Gaucher, Wilson, maple syrup urine disease, OTC deficiency
- Carrier screening checks whether a healthy parent silently carries a gene that could pass on if both partners carry the same faulty gene

**Pharmacogenomic (NOT diseases — drug metabolism; the Prevea parallel):**
- CYP2D6, CYP2C19, CYP2C9, CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2B6 metabolizer status — governs how antidepressants, antipsychotics, and many other drugs are broken down
- Listed here because the cheek swab detects these, but they are drug-response typings, not diseases

## B. Saliva / oral fluid — infections and antibodies present in saliva

- COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2 — saliva antigen/PCR; about 85% as accurate as nose-and-throat swab in one cited comparison)
- Rhinovirus (common cold)
- Influenza virus (flu)
- Epstein-Barr virus (mononucleosis / mono)
- Herpes simplex virus type 1 (cold sores)
- Streptococcus bacteria (strep — Group A Strep in saliva/throat)
- Hepatitis B and hepatitis C (viral markers in saliva)
- Cytomegalovirus (CMV — a risk during fetal development)
- HIV antibodies (oral-fluid screening — reactive screening requires confirmatory testing)

## C. Throat swab — pathogens on tonsils and posterior pharynx

**Bacterial:**
- Strep throat — Group A Streptococcus (*Streptococcus pyogenes*) — NOTE: this is *S. pyogenes*, NOT *S. pneumoniae*. His pneumomeningococcus is pneumococcal; throat strep is a different streptococcus. Do not conflate.
- *Staphylococcus aureus* (including MRSA screening swabs)
- Diphtheria (*Corynebacterium diphtheriae* — throat culture; his diphtheria thread)

**Viral:**
- Influenza A and B
- RSV (respiratory syncytial virus)
- COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)
- Herpes simplex virus (HSV)
- Varicella zoster virus (VZV)

**Fungal:**
- *Candida* species (oral thrush)

**Multiplex note:** one sample can now differentiate SARS-CoV-2, influenza A, influenza B, and RSV together (multiplex kits) — the same swab, four answers.

## D. Adjacent but NOT mouth swabs (kept out of the list, noted to prevent contamination)

- Nasal swabs — influenza, COVID-19, RSV (often the same multiplex kits, different site)
- Mouth-swab drug tests — detect drugs in saliva (workplace/legal), not diseases

## Parallels-ready notes (for his thinking, not conclusions)

- **Latency thread:** EBV, HSV-1, VZV, and CMV are all latency viruses detectable from mouth samples — his latency thesis has a mouth-swab data set.
- **Fungal thread:** *Candida* on a throat swab is a fungal detection from the mouth — beside his fungal track.
- **Strep distinction:** throat strep (*S. pyogenes*) vs. pneumococcus (*S. pneumoniae*) — two streptococci, two data sets; his pneumomeningococcus belongs to the second.
- **Typing thread:** cheek-swab DNA (CYP types, HLA types, serotype parallels) is the same method as his serotype work — type first, then treat — applied to the genome instead of the capsule.
- **Scale note:** the genetic list (2,200+) dwarfs the pathogen list (a dozen-ish) — because DNA swabs read the person's whole variant library, while pathogen swabs read one sample at one time.
