## Postulates

### P1 — A streptococcus is morphed from something different [H→D partial: trait-morphing holds, species-morphing breaks]

**Holds — the group morphs across its members:**
- *Streptococcus pneumoniae* sits in the mitis group beside *S. mitis* and *S. pseudopneumoniae*, with extensive recombination within the cluster — mostly one-way, with *S. mitis* and *S. oralis* as the DNA providers and pneumococcus as the recipient.
- The morphing runs both directions across the group: *S. mitis*, a commensal and close relative, switched capsule by acquiring the serotype 4 capsule locus of pneumococcus TIGR4 through natural transformation, and *S. mitis* expressing pneumococcal serotype 1 capsule is documented.
- The founding experiment is transformation itself: Griffith's pneumococcus changed in vivo from an avirulent strain devoid of capsule to a virulent encapsulated strain, and recombinational exchanges at the capsular locus lead to frequent serotype changes among natural isolates.

**Breaks — the species line:**
- Transformation changes traits and capsule, not species identity. No documented case shows one pneumococcus cell becoming a different species by morphing. The morphing is real at the level of genes and capsule; it stops at the species boundary.
- Simply put: The streptococcus borrows its neighbors' clothes — capsule genes flow back and forth across the mitis group — but borrowing a coat is not becoming the neighbor.

### P2 — Shedding of the capsule shifts from one half to the other [readings open; two hold with the mechanism corrected]

- **Reading (a) — the diplococcus pair's halves:** no documented mechanism by which capsule shedding shifts anything between the two cocci of a pair [GAP].
- **Reading (b) — one serotype to another:** the shift holds, but the mechanism is recombination, not shedding. Serotype variants arise from natural transformation events acquiring capsule-locus DNA. Shedding does not cause the switch; gene acquisition does.
- **Reading (c) — encapsulated form to non-encapsulated form:** holds in vivo. One patient's blood strain was capsular serotype 24B while the sputum strain, carrying capsular type 24 genes, was non-encapsulated with an insertion in the *wcxK* gene, and the non-capsular strain formed biofilm better. Biofilm pneumococci predominantly express less capsular polysaccharide, whether as transparent phase variants or mutants. Loss of capsule expression in vivo is associated with imported *aliB* homologues in some non-encapsulated pneumococci.
- Simply put: The shell can be swapped (new capsule genes), lost (no capsule at all), or turned down (biofilm form). Shedding is one road to "hard to type," but the switch itself runs on borrowed genes.

### P3 — Hard to see because the shell is permeated with more yeasts and fungi than bacteria [H→D partial as masking; GAP as literal infiltration]

**Holds — as detection masking:**
- The standard serotyping test, the Quellung reaction, depends on the capsule swelling under antiserum; identification of non-typeable pneumococci depends on a non-reaction, and a non-reaction is ambiguous — it could mean a novel capsule type the serum does not recognize. A capsule-less or capsule-changed pneumococcus is therefore hard to see by the standard test, by design.
- Fungal majority in a sample masks bacterial signal in the same direction: his overgrowth leg (dysbiosis shifting metabolite and population balance) and his Group B streptococcus–*Candida* precedent (Candida shielding bacteria from antibiotics) both run this way — the yeast/fungal presence changes what the test finds.

**Breaks — as literal permeation:**
- No documented mechanism shows yeasts or fungi infiltrating inside a bacterial capsule. Co-occurrence in a biofilm or a sample is not permeation of the shell. The literal form stays [GAP]; the masking form is the documented one.
- Simply put: The bacterium gets hard to see three ways — new coat, no coat, or a crowd of fungi standing in front of it. The crowd hiding it is proven; the crowd living inside its coat is not.

## Combined statement

Yes, a streptococcus morphs — its capsule genes flow across the mitis group, its serotype switches by borrowed DNA, and it can drop its capsule entirely, in the body, while forming biofilm better without it. And yes, that makes it hard to see: the standard test reads the capsule, so a switched, lost, or turned-down capsule reads as negative or novel. The yeast-and-fungal crowd makes it harder still by masking the bacterial signal — as a crowd, proven; as infiltrators inside the shell itself, still open, and kept open by his "perhaps."

## My extra addition (mine, labeled)
