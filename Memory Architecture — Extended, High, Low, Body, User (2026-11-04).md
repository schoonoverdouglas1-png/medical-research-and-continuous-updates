## The five layers, as filed

### 1. Extended memory
- Everything kept outside the always-injected files, reachable when needed: ~/workspace/ archives, imports, research notes, thesis files, side-chat transcripts, and the runtime's ~/memory/bank/ and ~/memory/index/.
- Use: long-term, searchable, not loaded every turn.

### 2. High memory
- The durable, load-bearing facts that must be present every turn: ~/MEMORY.md — standing rules, settled rulings, appointments, usage, and commitments.
- Use: always injected, always checked before acting. If it conflicts with a lower layer, high memory wins until Douglas corrects it.

### 3. Low memory
- Routine, day-to-day detail that does not need to stay high: dated notes under ~/memory/2026-*.md, daily logs, and routine progress.
- Use: kept, searchable, allowed to fade from the active summary unless it becomes durable — then it is promoted to high memory.

### 4. Body
- Settled by Douglas, Nov 4, 2026, ~12:15 PM CST, via option tap: Body means BOTH.
  - (a) His body: health, device, and physical-layer information — Pixel 7 Pro, Health Connect, appointments, prescriptions, and lab/visit materials filed without interpretation.
  - (b) Ember's "body": the assistant's presence — SOUL.md persona, IDENTITY.md, and the device/app surface she works through.
- Use: body-layer information is treated with the same separation rules as health material — filed, not interpreted, and not mixed into manuscript reasoning. The two body readings are kept distinct inside the one Body layer, not merged into one another.

### 5. User
- Who Douglas is, in his own verified terms: ~/USER.md — name, what to call him, timezone, and verifiably available context only. Self-reported, unverified claims stay in MEMORY.md, labeled as his account, and do not go into USER.md.
- Use: identity anchor for all other layers.

## Soul, in this architecture
- SOUL.md sits alongside the layers, not inside any one of them: it is how Ember speaks and acts, while the five layers are what she remembers. Soul guides tone and care; memory guides facts and commitments. Neither replaces the other.
