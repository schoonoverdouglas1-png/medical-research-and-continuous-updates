## Postulates, split

### P1 — Antibody testing runs in parallel with the disease
**His statement, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS, with the parallel kept honest.** Antibody testing runs alongside the disease, not on top of it: IgM marks a more recent immune response, IgG a later or persistent one, and paired acute and convalescent samples show a rise over time. The antibody track and the disease track are parallel lines — they move together, but one does not replace the other. An antibody can outlast the disease, arrive after it, or cross-react with a related organism, so the parallel can mislead if it is read as identity.
- **Simply put:** the antibody line and the sickness line run side by side. Side by side is not the same line.

### P2 — Virus, bacteria, and fungi each get their own parallel track
**His statement, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS, and this is the six-tracks rule applied to testing.** Virus, bacteria, and fungi are separate tracks — and, in his taxonomy, yeast and mold are separate again — each with its own antibody patterns, its own timing, and its own cross-reactivity traps. A viral antibody does not speak for a bacterial infection, and a fungal antibody does not speak for a viral one. Collapsing the tracks into one "infection antibody" reading is where false causation is born.
- **Simply put:** three (or five) separate lines, never one blended line.

### P3 — The procession of information swirls down to one causation within the body
**His statement, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS as a final common pathway, BREAKS as a single outside cause.** Many different outside causes do swirl down into one sickness inside the body: different viruses, bacteria, and fungi can all produce the same syndrome — pneumonia, encephalitis, sepsis — through the same final pathways of inflammation, tissue injury, and organ strain. That convergence is real and documented. What breaks is reading the convergence backwards: one sickness inside does not prove one cause outside. The funnel narrows going in, but it does not narrow to a single origin — it narrows to a single *showing*.
- **Simply put:** many roads in, one sickness showing — but the showing does not name the road.

### P4 — The sickness is what needs to be treated, regardless of cause from the outside
**His statement, tested, with the boundary stated.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS for the syndrome, BREAKS if read as replacing cause-specific treatment.** Treating the sickness itself — supporting breathing, circulation, and organ function, calming a harmful inflammatory response — is necessary whatever caused it, and it is often what keeps a person alive while the cause is still being found. But it does not replace cause-specific treatment where one exists: an antibacterial does not treat a virus, an antifungal does not treat a bacterium, and supportive care alone does not clear an organism that keeps driving the syndrome. "Regardless of cause" holds for the urgent syndrome; it breaks as a complete treatment rule.
- **Simply put:** treat the fire in the house whatever started it — and still find what started it, because the extinguisher differs.

## Combined statement (his thought + the record)
His swirl is real if it is drawn as a funnel with two ends: many outside causes — virus, bacteria, fungi, each on its own antibody track — spiral down into one sickness within the body, and that sickness is what presents, what suffers, and what must be steadied first. The funnel's mistake would be to run it backwards and claim the one sickness proves one cause, or that steadying the sickness finishes the job. The antibody tracks stay parallel precisely so the cause can still be named after the sickness is steadied.
