# Thesis — SARS-CoV-2 as Mini-Microbial or Nano-Microbial Infection

Filed: October 31, 2026, standalone — not attached to any book unless Douglas links it himself.
His dictation (verbatim sense): "I call the SARS-CoV-2 mini-microbial or nano-microbial infection because it's the bugs of the bugs within us that makes us sick, and those viruses are shared across all animals that can have that size of virus."
Split into four postulates so no sub-idea is lost. Tier tags: [D] documented · [H] his, filed as his · [H→D] his with a documented foothold · [LOGIC] inference with explicit steps · [GAP] unknown.

## P1 — "Mini-microbial infection" as the name [H]

His claim: SARS-CoV-2 infection should be called a mini-microbial infection.
- Foothold: SARS-CoV-2 is far smaller than a bacterium — virions run roughly 60–140 nm across, commonly ~80–100 nm. Smallness is real [D].
- Honest line: "mini" has no measured anchor. It is a relative word, not a scale. Nothing in the record ties "mini" to a number.
- Verdict: stands as his name [H]; no documented anchor for "mini" specifically.
- Simply put: He wants a name that says "smaller than the small germs."

## P2 — "Nano-microbial infection" as the name [H→D]

His claim: SARS-CoV-2 infection should be called a nano-microbial infection.
- Foothold [D]: SARS-CoV-2 virions take a roughly spherical or ellipsoidal shape with an average diameter of 108 ± 8 nm by cryo-electron tomography; other measurements put the mean diameter between ~85 and 110 nm at the long axis. One nanometer is 10^-9 meters — this virus lives on the nano scale by measurement, not by metaphor.
- Second foothold [D]: the bugs-of-bugs themselves — bacteriophages — run 30 to 150 nm, the same size class as SARS-CoV-2. The nano scale is where both stories live.
- Honest line: "microbial" is loose for a virus. Strictly, microbes are microscopic organisms; viruses are often counted among microorganisms in common usage but are not living organisms. The name works as his coinage, not as a taxonomic rank.
- Verdict: holds as a name with a measured foothold [H→D]. Of his two candidate names, this is the tighter one.
- Simply put: The virus is about 100 nanometers wide. "Nano" is the true measurement; "mini" is just "small."

## P3 — The mechanism: it is the bugs of the bugs within us that make us sick [H]

His claim: sickness comes from the bugs of the bugs within us.
- Foothold 1 [D]: bugs of bugs are literally real. A bacteriophage is a virus that infects and replicates within bacteria; phages are among the most common and diverse entities in the biosphere, estimated at more than 10^31 on the planet, found wherever bacteria exist. Phages do not infect humans — but countless phage particles are in the human body, given the extensive human microbiome.
- Foothold 2 [H→D]: his own prior theses, kept as his — the nested doll (Oct 18: SARS-CoV + rotavirus inside RSV inside the pneumonia organism; literal nesting [GAP], footholds: carriers, co-presence + synergy, satellite-virus/virophage dependencies); the activation/cleansing thesis (Oct 16: the universal bugs inside us get sick with the sicknesses we get sick with; the bugs get better when we get better); co-sickness (Oct 19: bugs sick with the same things simultaneously within us).
- Honest line [D]: as the literal mechanism of COVID-19, this breaks. SARS-CoV-2 infects human cells — it enters via the ACE2 receptor and replicates in human cells. It does not infect our bacteria. COVID-19 is human-cell infection plus the immune response to it, not the virus infecting our microbiome.
- Verdict: HOLDS as a nested-ecology framing [H→D] — we carry bugs, those bugs carry viruses, and sickness happens inside that nested ecology, with the microbiome's immune role established. BREAKS as the literal mechanism of SARS-CoV-2 disease [D]. Both readings stay explicit, like VNA.
- Simply put: There really are viruses that infect our bacteria, and they live inside us by the trillions. But this virus makes us sick by infecting our own cells, not our bacteria.

## P4 — The sharing: those viruses are shared across all animals that can have that size of virus [H]

His claim: viruses of that size are shared across all animals that can have them.
- Foothold [D]: SARS-CoV-2 has an unusually broad host range. Natural and experimental infections are documented across mustelids, canines, felines, primates, rodents, and deer — cats, dogs, ferrets, Syrian hamsters, non-human primates, farmed mink, white-tailed deer. Its broad species tropism is attributed primarily to the ability to use ACE2 receptors from multiple species.
- Honest line 1 [D]: "all animals" overreaches. Sustained animal-to-animal transmission with spillback to humans is clearly documented only in white-tailed deer and farmed mink; other species show limited or no onward spread — they are spillover hosts, not reservoirs.
- Honest line 2 [D]: size is not the criterion. Sharing is governed by receptor compatibility (ACE2 and entry factors), tissue expression, and ecology — not by being ~100 nm. Many ~100 nm viruses are not shared across all animals, and many animals cannot be infected by SARS-CoV-2 despite being able to "have" viruses of that size.
- Verdict: HOLDS in the weak form [H→D] — SARS-CoV-2 is shared across many animals. BREAKS in the strong form [D] — size does not decide sharing, and "all animals" is not met.
- Simply put: This virus really does jump between many animals — deer, mink, cats, dogs, and more. But what opens the door is the ACE2 lock fitting the key, not the size of the virus.

## Combined statement (his thoughts + my thoughts, the correction that fits)

SARS-CoV-2 is a ~100-nanometer virus — nano-scale, the same size class as the phages that infect our bacteria — and it moves across many mammals because their ACE2 receptors fit it, not because of its size. We carry bugs within us, and those bugs carry viruses of their own, and sickness happens inside that nested ecology — but COVID-19 itself is the virus infecting our own cells, not the virus infecting our bugs.

## My extra addition (mine, labeled)

Two recommendations. First, if one name is adopted, adopt "nano-microbial": it carries the measured anchor (~108 nm) that "mini" lacks, and it keeps the coinage honest. Second, define "bugs of the bugs" on first use as phages plus microbiome bacteria — that one definition stops the mechanism claim from being misread as "SARS-CoV-2 infects bacteria," which the record does not support, while keeping his nested-ecology insight intact.
