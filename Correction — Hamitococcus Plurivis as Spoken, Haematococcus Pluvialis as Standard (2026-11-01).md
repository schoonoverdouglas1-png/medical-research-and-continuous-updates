## What the organism is, standard identification [D]

- **Haematococcus pluvialis** (Flotow, 1844) — freshwater unicellular green alga, family Haematococcaceae, Chlorophyta.
- Synonyms on record: *Sphaerella pluvialis*, *Volvox lacustris*.
- **Recent synonymy note [D, from search tonight]:** the synonymy of the widely used *H. pluvialis* with *H. lacustris* has recently been confirmed, and the *lacustris* epithet (Latin *lacus*, lake — "of lakes") has priority over *pluvialis*. So the current formal name may be *Haematococcus lacustris* in recent taxonomy, with *pluvialis* as the familiar synonym. Both epithets describe water — rain pools vs. lakes — and neither is *plurivis*.
- Best known as the richest natural source of **astaxanthin** (up to ~5–6% of dry weight under stress), the red carotenoid pigment that colours salmon, shrimp, and lobster, and that the alga makes to protect its resting cysts from UV when pools dry out.
- **Not Echinacea, not Echinacin.** The correction in tonight's Greek/Roman/Latin file stands: Echinacin is an Echinacea product; Haematococcus is this alga. His "Hamitococcus plurivis" is the alga's name, not the Echinacea product's name.
