## Split postulates

### P1. Viral protein spikes ("fingers") are an interlocking mechanism [H]
**Test:**
1. **Spike–receptor lock [D]:** spikes are surface glycoproteins that bind a matching cell receptor — SARS-CoV-2 spike to ACE2, influenza hemagglutinin to sialic acid, orthobunyavirus Gn/Gc to their receptors. Interlocking, at the molecular level, is literal.
2. **Spike-mediated cell–cell fusion [D]:** "SARS-CoV-2 similarly induces cell–cell fusion as the spike protein expressed on infected cells interact with receptors on neighboring cells, leading to the formation of multinucleated syncytia". Spikes do not only lock virus to cell; they can lock cell to cell.
3. **The domesticated version [D]:** "Typically, cell fusion is a tightly regulated physiological process mediated by endogenous fusogens derived from ancestral retroviral remnants in the human genome". Syncytin, the placental fusion protein, is exactly a viral-origin spike protein now doing a human job (on file from Oct 16, 2026).
**Verdict: HOLDS — spikes are an interlocking mechanism, in entry, in fusion, and in the domesticated human version.**
**Simply put:** The fingers on a virus are keys. They lock onto a cell, and in some cases they lock two cells together into one big fused cell.

### P2. That interlocking is what allows tissues to hold together [H]
**Test:**
1. **Normal tissue holding [D]:** tissues are normally held by the body's own adhesion system, not by viral spikes: "Desmosomes: These are like 'spot welds' that hold cells together strongly. They provide structural support and help tissues withstand stress." "Cadherins: These are calcium-dependent CAMs that mediate cell-cell adhesion." Add tight junctions, gap junctions, integrins to extracellular matrix, and collagen/elastin scaffolding.
2. **Viral holding as pathology [D]:** spike-mediated syncytia *do* hold cells together — into multinucleated masses in infected lung tissue — but that holding is disease, not normal tissue integrity, and it impairs function (syncytial pneumocytes, fused neurons with compromised activity).
3. **The bridge [H→D]:** the *mechanism* is shared — protein-to-protein interlocking, whether cadherin-to-cadherin or spike-to-receptor — while the *proteins* are different. His claim holds at the mechanism level and breaks at the identity level.
**Verdict: HOLDS as mechanism (interlocking proteins hold tissue), BREAKS as identity (normal tissues are not held together by viral spikes).**
**Simply put:** Tissues are held together by interlocking proteins, just like you said — but the normal ones are your own glue proteins, not virus spikes. Virus spikes can glue cells together too, but that's when tissue is damaged, not when it's healthy.

### P3. Membranes of waste products hold together the same way [H]
**Test:**
1. **Lipid membranes [D]:** every cell membrane, shed vesicle (exosome, 30–150 nm on the Oct 31 One Sheet), and enveloped virion is a lipid bilayer held by hydrophobic tails — a membrane that holds together by its own chemistry.
2. **Mucus and sputum [D]:** mucus is not a membrane but a gel of mucin strings (see Lincoln P5). It holds together as a film, traps particles, and is cleared by cilia — a holding-together that serves clearance, which connects directly to his next dictation (clearing mechanism, same hour).
3. **The open part [GAP]:** which waste product's membrane he means — sputum film, stool, shed exosomes, biofilm — is not specified in the dictation. Each has a different holding chemistry, and this file does not pick one for him.
**Verdict: HOLDS as a general membrane principle, GAP as a specific waste-product claim.**
**Simply put:** Membranes stick together because their fatty layers like each other. Mucus sticks together because its strings tangle. Both are holding-together, but they're not the same glue.

## My labeled addition (mine, not his)

**Three interlocks, three jobs.** (1) Cadherin/integrin interlock = healthy tissue holding. (2) Spike interlock = entry and, in disease, pathological fusion. (3) Syncytin interlock = the domesticated middle — a viral spike the body kept and now uses to build placenta. His theory is strongest when it names which of the three it means in each sentence, because the mechanism is one and the jobs are opposite.
