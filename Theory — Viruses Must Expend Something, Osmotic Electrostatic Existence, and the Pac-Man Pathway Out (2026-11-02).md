## Postulates, split

### P1 — Viruses must consume and expend something to exist
**His statement, tested generally.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS rewritten, BREAKS literally.** A virus has no metabolism of its own and does not eat, breathe, or burn fuel — that literal reading was already tested and broke in his Nov 1 virus theories. What holds is that a virus expends *host* resources: once inside a host cell, it uses the cell's energy, ribosomes, nucleotides, and membranes to copy itself. Existence outside a cell costs it nothing measurable; replication inside a cell costs the host a great deal. "Consume and expend" holds if the subject is the host cell's budget, not the virus's own stomach.
- **Simply put:** a virus is a set of instructions with no kitchen. It cooks only in somebody else's kitchen, at their expense.

### P2 — Unless viruses are completely osmotic and electrostatic, existing homeostatically
**His alternative, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS as a partial physical description, BREAKS as a complete existence account.** Outside a host cell, a virus particle (virion) is indeed held together and moved by physical forces — electrostatic charge, osmotic pressure, and the stability of its capsid or envelope in its medium — and it can persist in that state without metabolizing anything. That is a real, homeostatic-looking persistence. But persistence is not replication, and it is not life in the metabolic sense: a virion in a droplet is stable, not active, and it does nothing until it meets a susceptible cell. Osmotic and electrostatic stability explains how it waits; it does not explain how it multiplies.
- **Simply put:** a virus can sit still like a seed in dry ground. Sitting still is not growing.

### P3 — Viruses are just stuck inadvertently and trying to get away from us, like Pac-Man finding a way out of a maze
**His metaphor, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS as a metaphor for egress, BREAKS as intention.** Viruses do not intend, try, or want — but viral egress is real and central: after replication, new virions must leave the host cell, and they do so by budding, lysis, or exocytosis, then by shedding from the host into the environment. A virus that cannot leave cannot spread, and a virus that is trapped in a non-permissive cell or a dead-end host is, in effect, stuck in the maze. The Pac-Man image captures egress and dead ends well; it fails only where it implies a mind choosing a route.
- **Lineage:** this continues his Nov 1 viruses-as-clearing theory (lytic lysis as clearing) and his Oct 31 Nesting-Doll/Scrubber Model — the smallest doll must get out of the bigger dolls to reach the next host.
- **Simply put:** no mind, but a real exit problem. Copy, leave, find the next cell — or stop.

### P4 — A virus might just need a pathway out
**His proposal, tested generally.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS as a research framing, not as a treatment.** Egress pathways are a real research target: blocking budding or release, or opening a clearance pathway, changes the course of infection in laboratory models. But a "pathway out" for a virus already inside a person is not a therapy a patient can open by reasoning, and no pathway is proposed here for any person. The pathway question belongs to laboratory research, not to a personal plan.
- **Simply put:** asking how a virus leaves is a good lab question. It is not, by itself, a cure.

## Combined statement (his thought + the record)
A virus spends nothing of its own and everything of its host's: it waits, stable and electrostatic, outside a cell, and it copies at host expense inside one. If it looks like it is trying to escape, that is egress without intention — Pac-Man without a player — and the maze is real: a virus that cannot leave a cell or a host goes nowhere. The question he is really asking is not whether viruses eat, but what they spend, where they wait, and how they get out.

## My addition (mine, for his approval)
Split "exist" into three verbs and never merge them again: *persist* (stable outside a cell, physical forces only), *replicate* (inside a cell, host energy spent), and *egress* (leave the cell and the host, the Pac-Man move). Most confusions in this thread come from using "exist" for all three at once.
