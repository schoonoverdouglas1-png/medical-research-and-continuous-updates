# Theory — Excretion as the Exit, Not the Origin (2026-10-31)

**Question:** Douglas, Oct 31, 2026, ~2:09 PM CDT — "Is a virus exiting the biome through excretion of excrement the source of where viruses come from from the bugs within us and or the biome within us?"

**Short answer:** No to the first half, yes to the second. Excretion is the exit, not the source. But the viruses exiting that way do come from the bugs within us — in the proximate sense, they are made inside the biome and then leave through it.

## Three levels, kept apart

### 1. Ultimate origin — not the biome [D]

Viruses predate humans and predate the gut biome by billions of years (virus-first hypothesis, ~3.5 billion years; endogenous retroviruses at ~8% of the genome). The biome cannot be the ultimate source of viruses, because viruses were here before biomes like ours existed.

### 2. Proximate source — replication inside the bugs and the gut [H→D]

This half holds, and the numbers are large:
- The gut virome is mostly dominated by bacteriophages — viruses that infect the gut bacteria, i.e., the bugs' bugs.
- The human intestinal virome established at birth is dominated by bacteria-infecting viruses.
- The gastrointestinal tract contains an estimated 10^15 phages, with *Caudovirales* overwhelmingly predominant.
- Phages replicate two ways inside their bacterial hosts: lytic (burst the cell, spill out) and temperate (sit as prophage, then induce and spill out). Enteric viruses replicate in gut cells the same way. That replication — inside the bugs, inside the gut — is where the exiting viruses are made.

### 3. Exit route — excretion [D]

- One gram of human feces contains around 10^8–10^9 virus-like particles, and the identifiable fraction is primarily bacteriophages, including dsDNA *Caudovirales*, ssDNA *Microviridae*, and the predominant CrAssphage.
- The eukaryotic fecal viruses include *Astroviridae*, *Anelloviridae*, *Picornaviridae*, *Caliciviridae*, *Herpesviridae*, plus rotavirus, enterovirus, sapovirus, and bocavirus found even in healthy children.
- Excretion disperses them: fecal-oral transmission to the next host, and CrAssphage is so reliably fecal that it serves as a marker for fecal contamination. The exit stream carries the viruses onward; it does not create them.

## Combined statement

Viruses do exit the biome through excrement, in the hundreds of millions per gram, and most of them are the bugs' own viruses — phages made inside the gut bacteria. But excretion is the door, not the factory: the factory is replication inside the bugs and the gut, and the ultimate origin is older than any biome. In his bug-chemistry language: the exit stream carries both what the bugs make and the bugs' bugs themselves.

- Simply put: The bugs make the viruses; the bowel lets them out. The leaving is not the making.

- Viral track: gut virome; enteric eukaryotic viruses; fecal-oral transmission.
