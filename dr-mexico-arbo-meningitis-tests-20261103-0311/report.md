# Arbovirus & Meningitis/Encephalitis Tests: Dominican Republic and Mexico vs US Regional Panels
## — Blood-PCR-only scope, international parallels, equine viruses, and CSF-to-blood adaptation

**Date of research:** 3 Nov 2026 (index searches; see Sources). **Status: index only — not verified live in a research browser.** No page was opened in a signed-in session and no specimen was ordered.
**General laboratory information only, not a recommendation for any person.** Do not use this report to order a test for a specific patient; a clinician and the performing laboratory must confirm current availability, specimen, timing, and approval.

## Summary — answer first

1. **The two US panels the requester already knows do NOT cover the main DR/Mexico travel arboviruses.**
   - Mayo **ARBOP** — *Arbovirus Antibody Panel, IgG and IgM, Serum* — is serum IFA for only California (La Crosse), Eastern equine, St. Louis, and Western equine encephalitis viruses. It contains **no dengue, Zika, chikungunya, West Nile, Oropouche, Mayaro, yellow fever, or Venezuelan equine test**.
   - Mayo **CSFME** — *Meningitis/Encephalitis Pathogen Panel, PCR, Spinal Fluid* — is the BioFire FilmArray panel, **CSF only**, for 14 targets: *E. coli* K1, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Listeria monocytogenes*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, *Streptococcus agalactiae*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, CMV, enterovirus, HSV-1, HSV-2, HHV-6, human parechovirus, VZV, and *Cryptococcus neoformans/gattii*. It contains **no arbovirus at all**.
2. **Blood-PCR-only filter (follow-up scope).** The recommended list in §2 is **blood PCR only: serum, plasma, or EDTA whole blood**. CSF-only panels are **listed only in §5 as EXCLUDED** because they require a spinal tap/lumbar puncture, and are **not presented as options**. Serum antibody tests (e.g. ARBOP itself, Mayo CHIKV/DENGM/VZIKM) are blood tests but are **not PCR**, so they are also **outside the narrowed blood-PCR-only recommended list** and are noted separately, not recommended here.
3. **The practical blood-PCR parallels are not a single “international panel.”** They are a patchwork:
   - **Commercial/provider send-outs in the US:** Mayo DENGS, CHIKS, VZIKS, WNVS, POWVS; Quest Oropouche PCR (from July 2025).
   - **US public-health send-outs, clinician + health-department routed, not direct patient mail-order:** Florida BPHL Arbovirus PCR codes, NY Wadsworth / APHL Arbovirus Reference Center Travel Panel 6150 and Summer Panel 5470, CDC Fort Collins via a state public-health laboratory.
   - **Canada as the clearest “other-country” parallel with a published code/name:** Public Health Ontario (PHO) Venezuelan equine and Eastern equine serology/PCR, referred to CDC Fort Collins / Canada's National Microbiology Laboratory (NML) — but only with PHO microbiologist approval, and for Ontario clinicians, not a US patient self-mail route.
   - **In Mexico and the Dominican Republic:** the reference work is InDRE (Mexico) and Laboratorio Nacional de Referencia Dr. Defilló / Laboratorio Central de Salud Pública (DR) RT-qPCR and sequencing. **No verified direct-to-US mail-order code, kit, or patient self-order route was found for either country's public laboratory.** Private in-country PCR exists as a general method (e.g. TaqMan Arbovirus Triplex in Mexican state labs), but a named, orderable-by-mail test for a US-based person was **not verified — a gap, not a finding**.
4. **Equine-encephalitis gap is sharpest for Western equine and Madariaga.** Eastern equine has a human serum PCR route (Florida 1682; PHO case-by-case). Venezuelan equine has a human serum PCR route (PHO → CDC). **No human blood-PCR code for Western equine encephalitis virus was found** in Mayo, Florida, Wadsworth, or PHO human menus searched. Veterinary equine codes (Zoologix, Guelph, Texas A&M) are **animal tests and must not be mistaken for human parallels** (§4).
5. **CSF-to-blood adaptation: not “it works both ways” as a general rule.** A CSF-cleared panel run on blood is a **modified FDA-cleared test / laboratory-developed test** under CLIA, requiring its own matrix validation. **No laboratory was found offering the BioFire/QIAstat meningitis/encephalitis panel validated on blood as a clinical test.** The documented opposite-direction repurposing (blood-culture panel on CSF) lost sensitivity — ~90% when Gram stain positive, ~50% when Gram stain negative — showing repurposing is not equivalence. Matrix alone changes sensitivity: Mayo's own West Nile page notes whole-blood PCR up to 87% vs serum 26% for acute WNV. Biology also changes it: a CNS pathogen may be absent from blood, so a negative blood result does not exclude CNS infection (§6).
6. **US forefront: yes for reference, multiplex, and FDA-authorised work, no for sole leadership.** CDC Fort Collins / Puerto Rico developed Trioplex, the first FDA-authorised Zika PCR; BioFire (US) and QIAstat are the FDA-cleared CSF syndromic panels. But the US lagged commercially on Oropouche until Quest July 2025, still routes Mayaro and Venezuelan equine essentially only through CDC, and relies on PAHO/WHO, InDRE, Fiocruz-type regional reference work for endemic-country surveillance. “Forefront” therefore depends on the virus (§7).
7. **Naming differences are not different viruses in most cases.** They are (a) historic place-of-first-isolation names retained despite WHO 2015 advice against new geographic names, (b) ICTV taxonomic renaming (genus *Flavivirus* → *Orthoflavivirus*, 2023; binomial species names, virus common names unchanged), (c) serogroup vs species (California serogroup = many viruses, La Crosse is one member used as the assay target), and (d) splits (North American EEE lineage I stays EEEV; Central/South American lineages II–IV are now Madariaga virus) (§7).

## 1. What is endemic / commonly tested in the Dominican Republic and Mexico

### Dominican Republic
- **Dengue is endemic, with regular outbreaks.** A 2021 analysis states: “Dengue is endemic in the Dominican Republic, and causes regular outbreaks, whereas Zika and chikungunya are emerging infections in the area,” with chikungunya in 2014 and Zika in 2016 as the first/only reported outbreaks of each at that time. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7794562/
- 2026 Dominican Ministry data cited in press (epidemiologic week 41, 11–17 Oct) reported 474 confirmed dengue cases in 2026 vs 265 in the same period of 2025, still described as within the endemic corridor. Source: https://deultimominuto.com/en/uncategorized/dengue-cases-increase-by-nearly-79-in-the-dominican-republic-during-2026/
- **Co-circulation context for the Americas:** dengue, chikungunya, Zika, plus Mayaro, Oropouche and yellow fever are described as concurrently circulating in the Americas. Source: https://www.scielo.cl/article_plus.php?pid=S0716-10182024000300421&tlng=es&lng=en
- **Oropouche risk is flagged for DR travel in general travel guidance** (mosquito protection “also against other mosquito-borne diseases such as Zika, chikungunya, oropouche, malaria”), but **no DR-specific Oropouche endemic case count was verified** in this research — do not overstate DR Oropouche endemicity. Source: https://www.healthytravel.ch/country-iframe?pageurl=CE9E53FB-B8A4-4906-AB0D-2075C9E58788&lang=fr
- **Meningitis in DR:** no DR-specific meningitis panel was found. The Global Meningococcal Initiative summary notes invasive meningococcal disease in the Dominican Republic fell from 18 cases in 2016 to 2 in 2021, i.e. meningococcal disease occurs but is not a DR-specific panel target. Bacterial meningitis pathogens in DR/Mexico are the same global set covered (in CSF) by CSFME — see §5. No evidence was found of a DR-only meningitis pathogen that would require a different blood PCR.

### Mexico
- **Mexico and Central America are described as endemic for arboviruses, “predominantly dengue, Zika and chikungunya,”** with historical yellow fever and encephalitis viruses, and serologic traces of Mayaro in Veracruz. Source: https://www.idsociety.org/science-speaks-blog/2025/the-growing-risk-of-arboviruses-in-the-americas/
- **Dengue is hyperendemic in Mexico, all four serotypes DENV-1–4 circulating**, with DENV-3 predominance in 2023 (63.9% in one 2023 surveillance analysis). Source: https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202403.0992
- **Chikungunya first recorded in Mexico in 2014, epidemic in 2015, then declining; Zika first reported in southern Mexico in November 2015, peak 2016, then declining; both now considered endemic with possible outbreaks expected.** West Nile is present in several Mexican states but only 13 officially confirmed human cases 2002–2023. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001706X23002516
- **Venezuelan equine encephalitis is described as endemic in the Americas and “hidden under the dengue umbrella,”** and as circulating in Latin America along with West Nile, chikungunya and Zika. That is an epidemiologic claim, not proof of a routine Mexican human test — see §4 for the actual test route.
- **Other Mexico-relevant arboviruses in animal/human serosurveys:** dengue 1–4, St. Louis encephalitis, T'Ho virus, West Nile, Zika and chikungunya were the PRNT panel in a 2023–2024 Chiapas bat/opossum study. T'Ho is a research finding, not a routine human blood-PCR test — do not add it to a clinical list. Source: http://mdpi.com/1999-4915/17/5/590

### Meningitis / encephalitis pathogens — why there is no separate DR/Mexico “meningitis panel”
- The CSFME 14 targets are the common community-acquired bacterial, viral and yeast causes, not country-specific. Arboviral meningoencephalitis is **additional to** CSFME, not inside it: chikungunya rare complications include meningoencephalitis, myelitis and Guillain-Barré; Zika rare complications include meningoencephalitis/encephalopathy, myelitis and Guillain-Barré; Oropouche rarely causes meningitis/encephalitis; dengue can cause meningoencephalitis, transverse myelitis, ADEM and Guillain-Barré. Source: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/cd/dengue-chikungunya-zika-oropouche-testing.pdf and http://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9476420/
- In other words, for a DR/Mexico exposure the **meningitis/encephalitis gap is not a different bacterial panel, it is the arboviral CNS gap**: dengue, chikungunya, Zika, West Nile, St. Louis, Eastern/Western/Venezuelan equine, Oropouche and Mayaro can all present with CNS disease, and none is on CSFME. Their blood versions are in §2; their CSF versions are excluded in §5.

**How “ordered by mail” actually works — read before the table.** In every verified example below, “mail/send-out” means: a clinician draws blood, the local clinic/laboratory ships the separated serum (or EDTA whole blood) by courier/overnight to the reference laboratory, and results return to the ordering clinician. It does **not** mean a patient orders a kit online, collects blood at home, and posts it themselves. No direct-to-patient home blood-PCR kit for these arboviruses was verified. Public-health tests additionally require health-department approval before shipment. If a vendor claims otherwise, that claim is unverified here.

## 4. Equine encephalitis viruses — human blood parallels, and the veterinary trap

“Equine” in these names means the virus was first associated with equine disease, **not** that the test is for horses. Human and veterinary tests share virus names but have different codes, laboratories, and validity. **Do not order a veterinary test for a human.**

### 4a. Eastern equine encephalitis virus (EEEV)
- **Human blood PCR — verified:**
  - Florida BPHL **EEE 1682**, acute serum <5 days post onset (with CSF/brain tissue as alternative forms, excluded here). Pre-approval via county health department. Source: https://www.floridahealth.gov/community-environmental-public-health/public-health-laboratories/testing/virology/arbovirus-pcr/
  - PHO **Eastern Equine Encephalitis Virus – Serology and PCR**: serology is preferred (HI screen, CDC Fort Collins PRNT confirmation); **PCR by RT-PCR at NML is case-by-case, “not routinely recommended,” “not a sensitive test,” and should be considered only for immunocompromised patients** per PHO, with specimens as soon as possible after onset. The PHO page does not give a separate numeric code in the excerpt — **code gap**. Source: https://WWW.PUBLICHEALTHONTARIO.CA/en/Laboratory-Services/Test-Information-Index/Eastern-Equine-Encephalitis-Serology
- **Human blood non-PCR (out of PCR list):** Mayo **EEEP** (Eastern Equine Encephalitis Antibody, IgG and IgM, Serum) as a component of ARBOP; PHO HI + PRNT (serum).
- **Veterinary — NOT human:** Guelph Animal Health Laboratory **eeepn** (EEE PCR, brain, dead horse), **xeeevme** (EEE IgM ELISA, live-horse serum, sent to USDA), **xeeevvn/xeeprnt** (PRNT, serum, external lab). Zoologix **S0055** (EEE RT-PCR, 0.2 mL EDTA whole blood or CNS tissue) is listed on an equine assay sheet. These are animal tests.

### 4b. Western equine encephalitis virus (WEEV)
- **Human blood PCR — NOT FOUND.** No Mayo, Florida, Wadsworth, or PHO human WEE PCR code was found in searches. Florida's Arbovirus PCR menu lists EEE, SLE, DEN, WN, CHIK, ZIK, YF, MAY — **no WEE**. Wadsworth's summer/travel menus list WNV, Powassan, Heartland, dengue, chikungunya, Oropouche, yellow fever, Zika — **no WEE PCR**.

## 6. Can a CSF PCR panel be adapted to blood? — general laboratory question, not a recommendation

**Short answer from what laboratories actually do: a CSF-validated assay is not automatically valid on blood. Changing the specimen changes the test's regulatory status and requires a new validation. No laboratory was found offering the BioFire or QIAstat meningitis/encephalitis panel on blood as a validated clinical test.**

### 6a. What the cleared intended use actually says
- BioFire FilmArray ME Panel: “a qualitative multiplexed nucleic acid-based in vitro diagnostic test capable of simultaneous detection and identification of multiple bacterial, viral, and yeast nucleic acids **directly from CSF specimens obtained via lumbar puncture** from individuals with signs and/or symptoms of meningitis and/or encephalitis.” Its CLIA verification note repeats the same CSF-via-lumbar-puncture intended use and notes CLIA requires verification of performance for **unmodified, moderate-complexity FDA-cleared tests**, with the laboratory director ultimately responsible. Sources: https://clevelandcliniclabs.com/test/meningitis-encephalitis-panel-by-pcr/ and http://www.biomerieux-diagnostics.com/content/dam/biomerieux-com/service-support/support-documents/technical-notes/meningitisencephalitis-tech-notes/FLM1-PRT-0165-06-FA-ME-Laboratory-Verification-Technical-Note.pdf
- QIAstat-Dx ME Panel (US): “FDA-cleared test to analyze 9 bacterial, viral and fungal nucleic acids **from cerebrospinal fluid specimens obtained via lumbar puncture**.” Source: https://www.qiagen.com/es-us/products/diagnostics-and-clinical-research/infectious-disease/qiastat-dx-syndromic-testing/qiastat-dx-na
- The only FDA-approved multiplex PCR assays for CSF cited in a 2025 ASM study are BioFire ME and QIAstat-Dx ME, “both of which target community-acquired meningitis/encephalitis … **in CSF obtained from lumbar punctures**” and are **not FDA-approved for CSF from indwelling devices** — a narrower point that shows even a different CSF source is not automatically covered, let alone blood. Source: https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.02770-25

### 6b. What specimen-type validation requires (CLIA)
- **Unmodified FDA-cleared test → verification:** accuracy, precision, reportable range, reference interval.
- **Modified FDA-cleared test or laboratory-developed test (LDT) → full establishment of performance specifications, before reporting patient results,** under 42 CFR 493.1253: (i) accuracy, (ii) precision, (iii) analytical sensitivity, (iv) analytical specificity **including interfering substances**, (v) reportable range, (vi) reference interval, (vii) any other characteristic required for performance. Sources: https://molecular.mlsascp.com/assay-validation-verification.html and https://sso.aphl.org/programs/infectious_disease/std/Documents/ID_2009Oct15_Checklist-STD-Performance-Specs.pdf
- **Changing specimen type is explicitly listed as a modification that moves a test into LDT-style validation:** “Common modifications that can move a test into LDT territory include a **different specimen type**, a different extraction method or instrument, changed reagent volumes or cycling conditions, and a new intended use,” and “Modifying an FDA-cleared test, such as changing specimen type or extraction method, generally moves it into LDT-style validation.” LDT validation should also evaluate **matrix effects**, carryover, and related characteristics. The same source notes FDA's 2024 LDT-as-device rule was **vacated by a federal court in March 2025 and reverted in September 2025, leaving CLIA as the primary framework**. Source: https://www.labmanager.com/qpcr-and-dpcr-in-clinical-diagnostics-validation-under-clia-and-cap-36122
- **Matrix validation in practice means:** proving the assay works in that specific body fluid — extraction efficiency, limit of detection (LOD, typically 95% detection in replicate dilution series), interference testing, and clinical accuracy with characterised positive/negative specimens in the **true matrix** (spiked true specimens are acceptable in the APHL/CDC checklist, but the matrix must be the real one, e.g. blood, not CSF). A laboratory director must sign the validation summary before the test is used on a patient.
- **Consequence for “run CSFME on blood”:** a laboratory that did so without that validation would be reporting an off-label, unvalidated result. It could still physically produce a signal — PCR chemistry does not know what tube the sample came from — but the **performance claims (sensitivity, specificity, LOD) would not apply**, and the result would require a disclaimer and director-approved LDT validation to be reportable as a clinical test. That distinction — *physically possible vs clinically validated* — is the whole answer, and it is **not speculation**: it is the CLIA framework described above.

### 6c. What laboratories actually do instead of swapping specimens
- **They validate the same pathogen separately for each specimen.** Mayo is the clearest example: chikungunya has CHIKS (serum) **and** CHIKC (CSF) as separate test IDs; dengue has DENGS (serum) **and** DENGC (CSF); West Nile has WNVS (serum) **and** WNVC (CSF, antibody) / WNVC PCR forms; Powassan has POWVS (serum), POWVC (CSF), POWVU (urine) as separate IDs with separate LODs. If swapping were accepted, separate IDs, volumes, and LODs would be unnecessary.
- **They validate multi-matrix assays from the start, when they intend multi-matrix use.** CDC Trioplex was authorised for serum, whole blood, urine, CSF and amniotic fluid (with paired-serum conditions for non-serum types in state implementations); Wadsworth travel PCR lists a validated specimen per virus (e.g. dengue plasma/serum, yellow fever serum/urine/whole blood). That is **prospective multi-matrix validation**, not retrospective adaptation.
- **They study repurposing and report the loss.** The ASM HCAVM study repurposed the BioFire **Blood Culture Identification 2 panel (a blood-culture panel) for CSF**: ~90% sensitivity when organisms were seen on Gram stain, but **only ~50% when Gram stain was negative**, vs standard-of-care testing. That is the opposite direction (blood → CSF), but it is the best documented analogue for “run a panel on a specimen it was not designed for”: it can work in high-burden cases and fail in low-burden cases, and the authors present it as a **study of utility, not as a validated clinical offering**. Source: https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/spectrum.02770-25
- **No blood ME panel was found in searches.** Searches for “meningitis/encephalitis panel blood/plasma/serum LDT” returned CSF panels, blood-culture panels, and individual blood PCRs (e.g. HSV blood PCR, Children's Minnesota HSVPB, EDTA whole blood, LOD HSV-1 30 TCID50/mL in blood vs 5–40 in CSF — i.e. **different LODs by specimen even for the same assay**), but **no syndromic 14-target ME panel validated on blood**. Absence in search is not proof of universal absence, but it is the evidence position: **unverified, and not orderable on any code found**.

### 6d. Documented limits when a CSF assay is run on blood — analytic and biologic
- **Matrix inhibitors (analytic):** heme/haemoglobin from blood binds DNA polymerase and inhibits amplification; immunoglobulins, lactoferrin, heparin, bilirubin and lipids are also documented PCR inhibitors in blood/plasma/serum, while CSF is described as having **rarely** endogenous polymerase inhibitors by comparison. Even bloody CSF shows the effect: enterovirus RT-PCR positivity was 26% in CSF without red cells vs 9.2% with red cells, with an acceptable upper limit of ~108 RBC/mm³ in that study; heme products are cited as the mechanism. Sources: https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1590/0004-282X20160119?id=&lang=en and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC523566/
- **Internal controls mitigate but do not eliminate the problem:** Mayo's large inhibition study (386,706 specimens) found overall inhibition <1% for most matrices when controls were used correctly, except urine and FFPE tissue — but that study is about **validated assays in their validated matrices with inhibition controls**, not about an unvalidated matrix swap. Diluting to reduce inhibitors also dilutes target below LOD — a trade-off that must be re-validated per matrix.
- **Background and target concentration (analytic + biologic):** whole blood has high human background DNA and requires efficient lysis; CSF has low target concentration but low background. The same pathogen can therefore have a **different LOD and different clinical sensitivity in blood vs CSF**, as shown by HSV (blood LOD higher than CSF LOD in the Children's Minnesota listing) and by West Nile (whole blood up to 87% vs serum 26% — even **within blood fractions** sensitivity differs). Source for WNV: https://test.mayocliniclabs.com/api/sitecore/TestCatalog/DownloadTestCatalog?testId=608436
- **Compartment biology (the decisive limit for CNS disease):** many ME-panel pathogens cause CNS disease without sustained viraemia/bacteraemia at the time of testing. HSV encephalitis is the textbook case: **CSF PCR is the diagnostic method of choice; serum HSV antibody is “not of utility in the diagnosis of HSV encephalitis in adults”** (IHMF guidance summarised in the literature), and a negative CSF PCR early (≤72 h) still does not exclude disease. By extension, a negative **blood** PCR for a CNS pathogen is even weaker evidence against CNS infection — the pathogen may simply not be in blood. Conversely, a positive blood PCR for a latent virus (e.g. HHV-6, which integrates into host DNA and is detectable in blood lymphocytes in a large fraction of healthy people) does not prove CNS disease; the BioFire ME evaluation reported HHV-6 sensitivity 85.7% in CSF and high false-positivity on discrepancy analysis. Blood and CSF results are therefore **not interchangeable in either direction**.
- **Practical consequences of an unvalidated blood ME result:** false negative (inhibition + low/absent blood target + wrong timing) risks missed CNS infection; false positive (latent virus, contamination, background) risks wrong therapy; and without a validated reference interval/LOD, neither a positive nor a negative can be interpreted with the panel's published sensitivity/specificity (meta-analysis for CSF ME panel: ~90% sensitivity, 97% specificity in CSF — **those numbers do not transfer to blood**).
- **What is NOT speculation:** the CLIA validation requirement, the CSF-only intended uses, the repurposing sensitivity loss, the inhibitor mechanisms, and the per-specimen LOD differences are all documented above. **What IS speculation and is flagged as such:** any claim that a specific laboratory *could* validate ME-on-blood successfully, what its sensitivity *would* be, or that a future multi-matrix ME cartridge *will* appear. Vendor marketing about “one cartridge handles CSF, blood, or swab” (found in a market report) is **not** evidence of a validated blood ME panel and should not be cited as such.

## 7. Is the US at the forefront? Why do the same/related viruses have different names?

### 7a. Forefront — a qualified yes
- **Reference and assay development:** CDC's Arbovirus Diseases Branch, Fort Collins, is the US reference laboratory for arbovirus diagnostics; its Diagnostics and Reference Laboratory chief (since 2000) is described as responsible for developing assays for dengue, chikungunya, West Nile and Zika. CDC's Puerto Rico Dengue Branch developed Trioplex, described as “the first Zika PCR test to gain FDA authorization for use in diagnostic laboratories” (March 2016 EUA context). Source: https://sta.aphl.cloud.sitefinity.com/docs/default-source/technical/aphl_a-complex-virus_a-coordinated-response_public-health-laboratories-battle-zika_150ppi_web.pdf?sfvrsn=4167a030_1
- **Syndromic panels:** BioFire FilmArray ME (US, FDA-cleared 2015/2016, K160462) and QIAstat-Dx ME (FDA-cleared Nov 2024, K242256) are the two FDA-cleared CSF syndromic panels. Mayo's LDT menu (separate serum/CSF PCRs, Powassan lineage PCR, etc.) is unusually broad for a single commercial laboratory.
- **Commercial translation lag:** Oropouche had no US commercial PCR until Quest July 2025, despite circulation in the Americas for decades; Mayaro and VEE remain CDC-only in the US; WEE has no human blood PCR found. That lag is not a lack of science — it is market size, regulatory cost, and the fact that rare-virus assays are not commercially attractive (a point made in PCR-validation literature: commercial kits are unlikely for small-scale specialist tests for rarely occurring pathogens, so LDTs/public-health assays fill the gap).
- **Non-US leadership that qualifies “forefront”:** InDRE is a WHO Collaborating Centre for Arboviruses; PAHO/WHO sets regional arbovirus laboratory guidelines and kit evaluations (InDRE evaluated dengue/Zika/chikungunya IgM kits, dengue qPCR, Zika+Dengue+Chikungunya qPCR); Brazil/Fiocruz-type and European (Institut Pasteur, altona RealStar kits used in Mayo's chikungunya assay validation, QIAstat EU 16-target IVDR version) work is part of the same ecosystem. The US is at the forefront of **FDA-authorised multiplex and reference PRNT work**, not of every virus or every country's routine testing.

### 7b. Why names differ — four mechanisms, with examples
1. **Historic place-of-first-isolation (vernacular virus name, retained).** Most arbovirus common names are the place where the virus was first isolated, not where it is now found: Zika (Zika Forest, Uganda), West Nile (West Nile district, Uganda), Ebola (Ebola River, DRC — cited as the classic example), La Crosse (La Crosse, Wisconsin), Jamestown Canyon, Powassan (Powassan, Ontario), Oropouche (Oropouche River, Trinidad), Mayaro (Mayaro, Trinidad), St. Louis, California encephalitis (first California serogroup virus found in California, 1943, though most serogroup members are not from California). WHO's 2015 Best Practices for naming **new** human diseases prohibit geographic, person, animal, and fear-inducing terms for new names, precisely because first-detection place ≠ origin and because geographic names stigmatise and mislead — but the guidance **does not rename diseases already in common usage**, so Zika, West Nile, Ebola etc. stay. Sources: https://ans-names.pitt.edu/ans/article/download/2276/2267/4831 and https://www.asianscientist.com/2020/04/features/coronavirus-covid19-naming/
2. **ICTV taxonomic renaming (species/genus name, virus common name unchanged).** In April 2023 ICTV renamed genus *Flavivirus* → ***Orthoflavivirus*** (“true flaviviruses”) because *Flaviviridae* (family) and *Flavivirus* (genus) shared the stem “flavi” and caused ambiguity with other genera (*Hepacivirus, Pegivirus, Pestivirus*). All species got binomial names: yellow fever → *Orthoflavivirus flavi*, dengue → *Orthoflavivirus denguei*, Zika → *Orthoflavivirus zikaense*, West Nile → *Orthoflavivirus nilense*. **Virus names (dengue virus, Zika virus) remain unchanged** — only the taxonomic species name changed. A laboratory that still writes “Flavivirus genus” (as Mayo's Powassan pages do) is not wrong about the virus; it is using pre-2023 taxonomy. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoflavivirus and https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/1237595
3. **Serogroup vs species (antigenic grouping vs genetic species).** “California encephalitis” is both (a) a virus (California encephalitis virus, CEV) and (b) a **serogroup** — the California serogroup of orthobunyaviruses — comprising La Crosse, Jamestown Canyon, snowshoe hare, Tahyna, Inkoo, Trivittatus, Guaroa and others, grouped by antigenic cross-reactivity. Mayo's ARBOP target is listed as “California Virus (La Crosse)” because **La Crosse virus is the actual target** in the Focus IFA (per APHL footnote), and cross-reactivity means a “California” result may reflect another serogroup member; laboratory standards require a ≥4-fold higher neutralising titre to identify the specific virus. Hence a result can be reported as “California serogroup,” “California (La Crosse),” or “La Crosse” depending on assay and laboratory, without implying three different infections. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/articles/PMC6433036/ and http://www.phac-aspc.gc.ca/lab-bio/res/psds-ftss/msds27e-eng.php and https://aphl.org/docs/default-source/infectious-diseases-documents/vector-borne-diseases/arbovirusreferencecenter_testmenus.pdf?sfvrsn=75ba9d05_2
4. **Splits and synonyms (one old name → two species; old group names → family/genus names).**
   - **Eastern equine → EEEV + Madariaga:** historically four lineages; lineage I (North America, Caribbean, Central America, most human cases) stays Eastern equine encephalitis virus; lineages II–IV (Central/South America, mainly equine disease) are now **Madariaga virus**, named for General Juan Madariaga, Argentina, where first isolated in 1930/1936. Pre-split Central/South American literature that says “EEEV” actually refers to MADV. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEEV and https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4915/18/7/692
   - **Old “Arbovirus Group A / Group B” → Alphavirus / Flavivirus (now Orthoflavivirus):** Mayo ARBOP aliases still list “Alphavirus (old Arbovirus, group A)” and “Flavivirus (old Arbovirus, group B)” — “arbovirus” itself is not a taxonomic group at all, it is an ecological term (arthropod-borne virus) spanning Togaviridae, Flaviviridae, Peribunyaviridae and others. A test called “arbovirus panel” in one country and “alphavirus panel” or “flavivirus panel” in another may be the same viruses under a different grouping.
   - **Dengue serotypes vs species:** dengue virus is one species (*Orthoflavivirus denguei*) with four serotypes DENV-1–4, which are clinically and epidemiologically distinct (second infection with a different serotype drives severe disease risk). A test that says “dengue” without typing, a test that types 1–4 (Wadsworth 2510, Florida Trioplex with typing in the Reference Center menu), and a test that says “DENV-1–4” are not different viruses — they are different levels of resolution.
   - **West Nile lineages, Powassan lineages:** Powassan lineage 1 vs lineage 2 (deer tick virus) are both reported as “Powassan” in Mayo POWVS/POWVC, with lineage breakdown as a result field, not a separate test. Similarly, “St. Louis encephalitis” vs “Saint Louis” is spelling, not a different virus.
