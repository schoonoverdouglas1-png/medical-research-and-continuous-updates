# Theory — Streptococcus, Cryptococcus, Coccidioides, and Oocysts: Fungally Related or Name-Related (2026-10-31)

**Dictation:** Douglas, Oct 31, 2026, ~07:34 CDT — "Streptocercus Cryptococcus and Cocciidiitis and Coccidioides and their other oocysts are all fungally related. And the other names are associated with them are as well."

**Readings flagged, his to correct:** "Streptocercus" is read as *Streptococcus* (no genus *Streptocercus* exists in the record; if he meant another organism, this file is wrong on that leg). "Cocciidiitis" is read with two readings kept open: (a) coccidioidomycosis, the fungal disease; (b) coccidiosis, the protozoan disease — and the two readings are themselves the trap this theory turns on, see P3. "Their other oocysts" is read as the oocysts of the organisms under discussion and of related organisms; no organism in the list makes oocysts, see P3.

## Postulates (split, never collapsed)

### P1 — *Cryptococcus* and *Coccidioides* are fungally related [H→D HOLDS]

- Both are fungi by kingdom: *Cryptococcus* a yeast (yeast track in his §209 system), *Coccidioides* a dimorphic mold (fungal track).
- Their diseases are fungal diseases: cryptococcosis and coccidioidomycosis (valley fever).
- Their old names are fungal names: Sanfelice named the cryptococcal yeast *Saccharomyces neoformans* in 1894 before Vuillemin renamed it *Cryptococcus neoformans* in 1901.
- Simply put: These two are genuinely kin — both fungi, both fungal diseases, fungal names from the start.

### P2 — *Streptococcus* is fungally related [BREAKS as nature; HOLDS as association]

- *Streptococcus* is a bacterium, not a fungus. As a claim about what the organism is, the fungal relation breaks [D].
- As a claim about association, it holds [H→D]: streptococci co-infect and co-colonize with fungi (the Oct 28 Group B streptococcus–*Candida* thesis established exactly this pattern: association holds, composition breaks). Polymicrobial membranes and mixed infections are his documented theme.
- Simply put: The bacterium is not a fungus, but it keeps fungal company. Association yes, identity no.

### P3 — The oocysts are fungally related [BREAKS — the name trap]

- Oocysts belong to the Coccidia, which are apicomplexan protozoa, not fungi: "Coccidia, characterized by thick-walled oocysts excreted in feces. In humans: Cryptosporidium, Isospora, Cyclospora, Sarcocystis, Toxoplasma". *Toxoplasma* is "a member of the protozoan subgroup coccidia, which also includes the GI pathogens Cryptosporidium, Isospora and Cyclospora".
- *Coccidioides* makes no oocysts at all. It is a pathogenic soil fungus whose tissue form is round, thick-walled spherules containing endospores, and whose culture form carries barrel-shaped arthroconidia. Spherules and arthroconidia, not oocysts.
- The trap is the syllable: *Coccidioides* (fungus) versus Coccidia (protozoa) share "cocci-" and nothing else. Same syllable, different kingdoms.
- Simply put: The oocysts are protozoan eggs, not fungal spores. The fungus and the protozoa share a first syllable the way two strangers share a first name.

### P4 — All the other names associated with them are fungally related too [BREAKS as universal; HOLDS for the fungal pair's names]

- Sorted by kingdom, the associated names sort with their organisms: the fungal pair's names are fungal (valley fever, San Joaquin fever, cryptococcosis, the old *Saccharomyces* name); *Streptococcus*'s names are bacterial; the oocyst-makers' names are protozoan (cryptosporidiosis, toxoplasmosis, cyclosporosis, isosporiasis, coccidiosis).
- So the universal form breaks [D], while the restricted form — the fungal pair's names are fungally related — holds [H→D].
- **The name-level pattern underneath (documented, and worth keeping):** two collisions, not relations. "Crypto-" appears in a fungus (*Cryptococcus*, hidden berry) and a protozoan (*Cryptosporidium*, hidden spore). "Cocci-" appears in a fungus (*Coccidioides*) and in protozoa (Coccidia). The collisions are real at the level of names — which is his Architecture lens: names can mask and collide — and false at the level of biology.
- Simply put: The names travel with their organisms. Where the names look alike across kingdoms, that is a collision of spelling, not a relation of blood.

## Combined statement

*Cryptococcus* and *Coccidioides* are fungally related, truly. *Streptococcus* is related to them by association, not by nature. The oocysts are not fungally related at all — they are protozoan, and the only thing tying them to the fungus is the shared syllable "cocci-," with a second shared syllable, "crypto-," tying a different fungus to a different protozoan. The theory holds for the fungal pair and its names, breaks for the bacterium and the oocysts, and leaves behind a sharper rule for the book: same syllable is not same kingdom.
