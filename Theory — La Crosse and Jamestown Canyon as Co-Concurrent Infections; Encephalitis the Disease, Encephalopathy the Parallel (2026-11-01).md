## P2 — Testing itself may be why not all second infections are found [H→D, strong]

**His claim:** it is not impossible that testing itself is why we are not finding all of those seconds.

**Tested:**
1. *Misdiagnosis by cross-reactivity [D]:* Jamestown Canyon cases have been misdiagnosed as La Crosse virus due to cross-reactivity of neutralizing-antibody responses; neutralizing antibodies generated to individual viruses often neutralize other members of the same group, which can complicate serologic diagnostics.
2. *Ascertainment, the same point as Oct 31 [D]:* an IgM test developed for Jamestown Canyon increased detected clinical cases from an average of 3–4 cases per year before 2013 to 40–70 cases per year in the United States after 2013. The virus did not multiply; the testing did.
3. *His own record:* Oct 31 part-vs-whole theory — his arbopanel carries a single "California (La Crosse)" analyte with no separate Jamestown Canyon analyte, so it cannot separate the two by design; plus his tested-showing thesis (what shows most may be what was tested).

**Verdict:** HOLDS [H→D] — three independent legs (cross-reactivity misdiagnosis, the 2013 ascertainment jump, his panel's single-analyte design) all say testing determines what is found. "Not impossible" is the right hedge, and it is kept.
- Simply put: The test can mistake one cousin for the other, and for years it couldn't even see the second one at all. Of course seconds go unfound.
