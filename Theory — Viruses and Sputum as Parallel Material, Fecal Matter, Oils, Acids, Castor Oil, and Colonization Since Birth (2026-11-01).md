## Split postulates

### P1. Viruses and sputum are paralleled with each other and are actually the material of each other [H]
**Test, three ways:**
1. **Sputum contains viruses [D]:** "Sputum is a substance many people notice when they cough during a respiratory illness. It is the material that is coughed up from the lungs and lower airways, often containing mucus, immune cells, microorganisms, and other particles that the body is trying to remove." Its list includes "Bacteria or viruses".
2. **Sputum is mostly not virus [D]:** "Mucus is 95% water, 3% proteins (including mucin and antibodies), 1% salt and other substances." Viruses are passengers in the mucus, not the bulk material of it.
3. **Parallel, not identity [LOGIC]:** sputum and virus travel together, appear together, and are read together in diagnosis — that is a genuine parallel. "Material of each other" breaks if read as identity (sputum = virus, virus = sputum), because sputum without virus is routine (asthma, COPD mucus) and virus without sputum is routine (blood-borne, latent, gut viruses).
**Verdict: HOLDS as parallel / carrier relationship, BREAKS as literal identity.**
**Simply put:** Sputum is the truck, viruses are cargo in the truck. They ride together and you can read one from the other — but the truck isn't made of cargo.

### P2. Coughing things out of the lungs is the body cleansing itself [H]
**Test:**
1. **Mucociliary clearance [D]:** mucus traps particles, cilia sweep it upward, cough expels it. That is a documented cleansing route, and it is the exit half of the clearing relay filed the same hour.
2. **Cough as symptom and as clearance [D]:** cough both clears and signals — a productive cough expels material, a dry cough in early infection may expel little. Cleansing is the function, not a guarantee in every cough.
3. **What is cleansed [D]:** mucus, dead cells, white blood cells, bacteria, viruses, dust — the sputum list above. "Toxins" in the loose sense (irritants, microbial products) ride out with it; specific named toxins need their own test.
**Verdict: HOLDS — coughing out sputum is a cleansing route.**
**Simply put:** Yes. Coughing up phlegm is your lungs taking out the trash.

### P3. Poop is a fungus, poop is a bacteria, poop is a "medical element" [H, his coinage]
**Test:**
1. **Poop contains all three kingdoms [D]:** "bacteria also make up to 60% of the dry mass of feces. Fungi, archaea, and viruses are also present in the gut flora". Feces are roughly three-quarters water, with the dry remainder dominated by bacterial biomass plus undigested food, shed cells, and microbial products.
2. **Poop is not any one of them [D]:** calling it "a fungus" or "a bacteria" breaks as identity — it is a mixture, and the largest single dry component is bacterial biomass, not fungus.
3. **"Medical element" [H, kept]:** his coinage is useful as a doctrine term — stool as a readable element that carries the body's microbial record. Old doctors reading stool (color, blood, parasites, consistency) is the historical version of modern stool testing (culture, PCR, calprotectin, occult blood). The reading practice holds; the single-kingdom identity does not.
**Verdict: HOLDS as "stool carries fungus, bacteria, and virus together and can be read medically"; BREAKS as "stool is a fungus" or "stool is a bacterium."**
**Simply put:** Poop isn't one germ. It's a mixed bag — mostly water, then mostly bacteria by dry weight, with fungi and viruses mixed in. That's exactly why doctors, old and new, read it.

### P4. The body hangs onto fecal matter when sick in order to have a place to get rid of toxins [H]
**Test:**
1. **Stool as an exit route [D]:** the gut *is* an exit route — bile-excreted substances, shed cells, and microbial products leave in stool. Retention does not create that route; it delays it.
2. **Constipation during illness [D]:** constipation during illness is common and usually explained by dehydration, reduced intake, immobility, fever, and medicines — not by an intentional holding strategy. The record does not show the body retaining stool *in order to* store toxins.
3. **The partial save [H→D]:** if "hangs onto" means "transit slows and material sits longer," that part is real (slow transit in illness). If it means "sitting longer helps detoxify," that part breaks — longer colonic residence generally means *more* reabsorption of water and some solutes, not more elimination.
**Verdict: HOLDS that transit slows in illness and stool is an exit route; BREAKS that retention is a designed toxin-storage strategy.**
**Simply put:** When you're sick you often do get constipated, and stool is one way things leave. But holding it in doesn't help get toxins out — it slows the exit down.

### P5. That hanging on is literally a lack of oils and acids [H]
**Test:**
1. **Oils as lubrication [H→D]:** dietary oils and intestinal secretions do lubricate passage, and bile (which contains bile acids) does affect motility — too little bile, or bile-acid malabsorption in the other direction, both change bowel habit. A partial foothold exists.
2. **Acids as motility signals [D]:** bile acids are signaling molecules for gut motility and secretion, not merely "acid" in the stomach sense. Stomach acid, bile acids, and short-chain fatty acids (bacterial products) are three different acids with three different jobs — the dictation does not separate them, and this file does not collapse them.
3. **The limit [D]:** constipation has many causes (fiber, water, motility, nerves, medicines, obstruction) that a single oils-and-acids lack does not cover.
**Verdict: HOLDS partial — oils and bile acids genuinely affect passage; BREAKS as a sole literal cause.**
**Simply put:** Oils help things slide and bile acids help the gut move — that's real. But constipation has a lot of causes, and "not enough oil and acid" doesn't cover them all.

### P6. In the old days they shoved us full of castor oil hoping to get rid of the chunk stuck in us since birth [H]
**Test:**
1. **Castor oil history [D]:** "The first mention of it as a laxative can be found in 3500-yearold Ancient Egyptian papyrus scrolls." "Castor oil is known primarily as an effective laxative". The old-days use is documented.
2. **Mechanism [D]:** ricinoleic acid, released in the intestine, activates the EP3 receptor on intestinal muscle cells, "which in turn stimulates intestinal activity". It works by contraction, not by dissolving a lifelong chunk.
3. **The chunk [GAP / BREAKS as stated]:** a single chunk "stuck in us since birth" that "grows with us" is not a documented anatomical entity. Fecal impaction is real but is a *condition* (hardened stool in rectum/colon), not a lifelong growing mass present from birth. What *does* grow with us from birth is the colonization in P7 — and the two must not be merged.
**Verdict: HOLDS on castor oil as a historical stimulant laxative; BREAKS on a lifelong growing chunk as the target.**
**Simply put:** Old doctors really did use castor oil, and it really does make the gut squeeze. But there isn't a single lump you've carried since birth for it to dissolve.

### P7. That chunk / colonization is how we get sick [H]
**Test:**
1. **Colonization from birth [D]:** "During birth and rapidly thereafter, bacteria from the mother and the surrounding environment colonize the infant's gut." "Microbial colonization and the establishment of a microbial community shortly after birth, as well as its maintenance, are critical for the development of the immune system."
2. **Colonization shapes sickness [D]:** dysbiosis (altered diversity/composition) is associated with inflammatory, metabolic, and immune disease in the gut literature filed the same hour. A skewed colony can contribute to getting sick.
3. **The separation [LOGIC]:** colonization (a living community that grows with us) holds; the "chunk" (a dead mass that grows with us) does not. If the theory is rewritten with colonization in the chunk's place, it becomes the strongest version of his idea — and that rewrite is offered here as a combined statement, with his original wording preserved above.
**Verdict: HOLDS with colonization substituted for chunk; BREAKS with chunk kept literal.**
**Simply put:** What really grows with you since birth is your gut community — trillions of microbes that train your immune system. When that community goes wrong, you can get sick. That's the colonization story, and it doesn't need a lump.

## My labeled addition (mine, not his)

**Two exits, one record.** Lungs and bowel are the body's two great *reading* exits: sputum reads the airway, stool reads the gut. Both carry the same three kingdoms (bacteria, fungi, viruses) in different proportions, and both are clearance routes, not storage vaults. His "medical element" works best as a name for that pair — the readable, excreted record of the biome — and it keeps his old-doctors point intact without needing a lifelong chunk: old doctors read stool because stool *is* a record, and modern labs read it for the same reason, with better tools.
