## Split postulates

### P1. There is an imbalance within the size of viruses [H]
**Test, three ways:**
1. **Range [D]:** viruses span roughly 20 nm (parvoviruses) to 300 nm (poxviruses), with the ~100 nm band crowded by coronaviruses, orthobunyaviruses, arenaviruses, retroviruses, and influenza. Any one sample is therefore skewed — it is not an even spread across sizes.
2. **Virome composition [D]:** "Dysbiosis in viral communities—characterized by changes in activity of temperate phages, reductions in viral diversity, or expansion of pathogenic viruses—are associated with mucosal inflammation, microbial dysbiosis, and systemic disease." In inflammatory bowel disease, studies show "an increase in abundance of Caudovirales, an order of tailed dsDNA bacteriophages but a decrease in general viral diversity and richness in individuals with IBD". Imbalance, in the sense of a skewed community, is documented.
3. **The limit [D]:** "imbalance within the size" is not a standard measurement. Studies measure abundance, diversity, and richness by *kind*, not by a size-balance score. Size imbalance as a *cause* is GAP; size skew as a *description* of a skewed virome holds.
**Verdict: HOLDS as a description — viromes are skewed, not balanced; GAP as a size-specific cause.**
**Simply put:** In any one person's virus mix, some sizes and kinds crowd out others. That's real. Whether the size itself causes the trouble is not shown.

### P2. Small and large viruses are often not conjoined / contingent [H]
**Test:**
1. **Independence [D]:** most infections are single-virus events; a small virus does not require a large partner to infect, and vice versa. Co-infection happens, contingency does not.
2. **Co-circulation without linkage [D]:** California serogroup viruses co-circulate in the same woods and mosquitoes, and cross-react on tests, without being joined particles. Together in a place is not together in a body.
3. **The exception that sharpens it [D]:** satellite viruses and virophages *are* contingent on a helper virus — the exception proves the rule is "often," not "always."
**Verdict: HOLDS — small and large viruses are usually independent, not conjoined.**
**Simply put:** Small and big viruses usually travel alone. Sometimes they share a neighborhood, rarely they share a ride.

### P3. Certain people might be short a certain virus [H, three readings]
- **(a) Short of antibodies to a virus:** seronegative because never exposed. Common, normal, not a shortage in the disease sense.
- **(b) Short of a virome member:** reduced diversity, as in the IBD pattern above. Documented as association, not yet as a proven missing-piece cause for a named person.
- **(c) Short of a beneficial virus:** the phage version — loss of phages that kept a bacterium in check, letting that bacterium expand. Documented in the gut literature as a mechanism, per-virus proof still developing.
**Verdict: HOLDS partial under (a) and (b) as descriptions; the causal "missing virus makes this person sick" claim is GAP until a specific virus, a specific person-pattern, and a specific test are named.**
**Simply put:** You can be missing the *defense* against a virus, or missing *variety* in your virus mix. Those are two different shortages, and the test for each is different.

### P4. The body might need immunoglobulin therapy to fix that problem [H]
**Test — and this is where the direction matters:**
1. **What immunoglobulin is [D]:** "Intravenous Immune Globulin (IVIG) is a blood product prepared from pooled human plasma containing a concentrated solution of antibodies. It is primarily used as replacement therapy for patients with primary immunodeficiency disorders characterized by hypogammaglobulinemia or defects in antibody production."
2. **What it fixes [D]:** "The goal is to provide immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies to those who lack them." Indications include "common variable immunodeficiency (CVID), congenital agammaglobulinemia (X-linked agammaglobulinemia), severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)" and related antibody defects.
3. **What it does not fix [LOGIC]:** IVIG contains antibodies, not viruses. It cannot replace a missing virus, and it does not work by supplying one. If the shortage is antibodies (reading a), IVIG is the documented replacement. If the shortage is a virus itself (readings b/c), IVIG points the wrong way — it gives the defense *against* viruses.
**Verdict: BREAKS as stated (immunoglobulin does not replace a missing virus); HOLDS reframed (immunoglobulin replaces missing antibodies). The reframe is the combined statement, not a dismissal of the idea.**
**Simply put:** Immunoglobulin therapy doesn't give you a virus you're missing. It gives you the antibodies you're missing. If the gap is antibodies, that's exactly the fix medicine already uses. If the gap is a virus, that's a different fix, and nobody has a proven one yet.

## My labeled addition (mine, not his)

**Two tests, never one.** His idea becomes testable only when split: (1) a serology panel — are antibodies to virus X present or absent? (2) a virome sequence — is virus X itself present or absent in the sample? A person can fail either test independently. Immunoglobulin therapy answers test 1. Nothing in the current record answers test 2 as a therapy. Keeping the two tests apart is what stops "short a virus" from sliding, unnoticed, into "short an antibody."
