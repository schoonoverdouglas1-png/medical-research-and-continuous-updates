## Postulates, split

### P1 — Mosquitoes and ticks might carry the same viruses
**His statement, tested against the Cascade research already on file (Nov 3, 2026).**
- **Verdict: HOLDS in a few documented cases, BREAKS as a general rule.** Most arboviruses are vector-specific: Eastern, Western, and Venezuelan equine viruses are mosquito-borne; Colorado tick fever virus and Powassan virus are tick-borne. But dual-host logic is real — an arbovirus replicates in both an arthropod and a vertebrate — and a small number of viruses are documented across vector groups or across tick and mosquito in experimental or incidental reports. A shared virus is therefore possible, not typical. The research question is which viruses, if any, are genuinely shared, not whether sharing happens in general.
- **Simply put:** most viruses pick one kind of bug. A few might not. Find the few, don't assume the many.

### P2 — The same viruses in the same indigenous areas
**His statement, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS as geography, BREAKS as proof of sharing.** Mosquitoes and ticks do overlap in the same indigenous areas — the Cascades, for example, hold both *Culex* mosquitoes and *Ixodes* / *Dermacentor* ticks — and the same vertebrate hosts (birds, rodents, deer, people) move through both. Geographic overlap is documented. Geographic overlap alone does not prove the same virus circulates in both vectors in that area; that needs virus detection in both vector species from the same place and time.
- **Simply put:** same woods, same animals, different bugs — overlap is a starting point, not a result.

### P3 — This research needs to be looked upon
**His direction, accepted as a research task.**
- **Verdict: ACCEPTED, not yet answered.** A dedicated research pass is started from this file: which viruses are documented in both mosquitoes and ticks, in which regions, with what evidence, and where the evidence is experimental, incidental, or established. Gaps stay marked, never filled by guessing.
- **Simply put:** ask the question properly, with sources, before claiming the answer.
