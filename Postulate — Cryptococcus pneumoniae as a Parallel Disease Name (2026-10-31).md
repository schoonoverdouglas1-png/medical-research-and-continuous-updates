# Postulate — Cryptococcus pneumoniae as a Parallel Disease Name (2026-10-31)

**Dictation:** Douglas, Oct 31, 2026, ~07:14 CDT — "Cryptococcus pneumoniae should certainly be a disease name, though I know it doesn't fit taxonomically."

**His concession, kept intact:** he states himself that it doesn't fit taxonomically. This postulate therefore claims it as a parallel disease name, never as a taxonomic name, and never as a replacement for the official name.

## The postulate, stated formally

A disease of the lung caused by *Cryptococcus* deserves a parallel disease name in organism-plus-lung form — *Cryptococcus pneumoniae* — kept alongside the official name, never replacing it, and always tagged as a disease name rather than a species.

## Tested three ways

**1. Precedent — the organism-plus-pneumonia form already names diseases [H→D].**
- *Pneumocystis* pneumonia (PCP) is a formal disease name in exactly genus-plus-pneumonia form, caused by *Pneumocystis jirovecii*.
- "Mycoplasma pneumonia" is used as a disease heading in pathology sources, for the disease caused by *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*.
- "Cryptococcal pneumonia" is already an established descriptive term for the same disease this postulate names.
- The pairing itself, then, is not novel. What is novel in his form is the ending: *-ae* instead of *-al*.

**2. Collision — the taxonomic misfit he concedes [D].**
- A two-word Latin form with a *-ae* second word reads as a species binomial. *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* are both real species names in exactly that shape. His form therefore invites misreading as a species that does not exist.
- The misfit is real, and it is the reason the name must travel with its tag: "parallel disease name, not a species." With the tag, the collision is marked rather than hidden; without it, the name would mislead.
- Simply put: The words are fine as a disease name. The ending is what makes them look like a germ's name, so the label has to travel with them.

**3. His system — additive naming permits it, track separation disciplines it [H→D].**
- Additive naming (his standing doctrine): new names are added beside old ones, nothing stripped. This postulate follows the *Ehrlichia muris eauclairensiosis* precedent exactly — a parallel name, kept alongside the official one, old with new.
- Track separation (his six tracks): the name deliberately crosses tracks, putting an organism-track genus into a disease-track name. That crossing is the point of the parallel form, and it is why the tag matters — the crossing must stay visible, never collapsed into a claim that the disease IS the organism.

## Verdict

**HOLDS as a parallel disease name [H→D]; BREAKS as a taxonomic name [D] — the second leg conceded by him in the dictation itself.** The postulate stands on the precedent leg and the additive-naming leg, and its honesty depends on the tag.
