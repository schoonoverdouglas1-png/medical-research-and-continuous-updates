**Belief stated:** Douglas, Oct 31, 2026, ~2:14 PM CDT — "I truly believe that if you take like the arbo panel for instance we have regional areas where the people and the bugs are both sick and the biome within us contain the same viruses and that is why they're passed around and shared like a good woman if you're a man like me."

**Filing note:** the dictation is kept verbatim above, simile included, as his words. The verdicts below test the substantive claim; the simile itself is his illustration of mutual sharing, not a tested claim.

## Postulates

### P1 — Regional areas where people and bugs are both sick [H→D partial]

**What holds:** arboviruses circulate regionally between arthropod vectors and vertebrate hosts — that is the enzootic cycle, and it is why his arbopanel is regional by construction: California (La Crosse) in the upper Midwest, Eastern equine in the eastern United States, St. Louis and Western equine in their own ranges. People and bugs in the same region carry the same virus. That is the documented shape of his example.

**The seam — "both sick":** the shared virus holds fully; shared sickness holds only partially. Vectors are typically infected carriers, not diseased the way humans are — a mosquito carrying La Crosse virus is not sick with encephalitis; it carries and transmits. Some arboviruses do cost the vector fitness or lifespan, so "both affected" has a foothold, but "both sick" in the human sense overreaches. Tightened form: regional areas where people and bugs both *carry* the same viruses, and the people sicken from them.
- Simply put: Same region, same virus, both carrying it — the people get the disease, the bugs mostly just carry it onward.

### P2 — The biome within us contains the same viruses [H→D partial]

**What holds:** for arboviruses, the same virus is present in the vector's body and in the human's blood and tissues — one virus, two biomes. More broadly, people sharing a region and household share more of their virome with each other than with strangers, because the circulating strains are the local ones.

**The seam:** "the same viruses" holds for the circulating arbovirus and for shared regional strains; it does not hold as every person's whole virome being identical — individual viromes vary widely (today's gut-virome numbers show high inter-individual variation). Regional sharing, not universal sameness.
- Simply put: The region's viruses show up in our biomes because we live where they circulate — shared strains, not identical viromes.

### P3 — That is why they are passed around and shared [H→D]

The shared regional cycle is the mechanism of passing around: vector to human to vector, human to human through shared environment and close contact, and (per today's excretion theory) out through exit streams to the next host. This rejoins his Oct 17 co-sick thesis (ticks and people sick together) and his cancer-thesis P6 (humans made to share suffering together) — the sharing is ecological before it is anything else.
- Simply put: They are passed around because people and bugs live in the same regional loop, carrying the same viruses through it.

## Combined statement

Take the arbopanel as the example and the belief holds in its tightened form: regional areas have people and bugs carrying the same viruses, the biome within us contains those regional viruses, and that shared regional loop is why they are passed around — carried together, sickening the people, carried onward by the bugs.
