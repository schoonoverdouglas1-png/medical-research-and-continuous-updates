## Fact-check second [D], beside his logic, not over it

### What holds in general
- Inadvertent / accidental inoculation through wounds is a real route, in general terms, for zoonotic and wound infections.
- Deliberate inoculation in the 19th and early 20th centuries used living, hand-worked material (arm-to-arm lymph, then calf lymph), with documented bacterial contamination in general terms, and with other infections documented as traveling with vaccine lymph in that era (syphilis in arm-to-arm, erysipelas / tetanus / septicaemia in contaminated lymph, in general).
- A lasting scar after scarification smallpox vaccination was historically expected as a "take" sign — consistent with his earlier scar account in general terms, not proof of her case.

### What breaks as a universal claim
- "We're fighting the effects" as a universal statement about all present disease is **not established**. Present disease has many documented causes — genetics, environment, infection, lifestyle, ageing, and others — and a past inoculation effect cannot be assigned as the cause for a present condition without a specific exposure, a specific mechanism, and a specific outcome, tied case by case.
- "However it happened, it happened" is a valid stance for keeping a hypothesis open, but it is not evidence for a specific mechanism. It keeps the question alive; it does not settle it.

### What is GAP, kept open
- GAP: Which specific inoculation his great-grandmother received around 1920 — smallpox scarification, or something else — and from what material, by whom, and where.
- GAP: What specific effect, if any, that inoculation had, in her case or in a later generation. No effect is inferred here.
- GAP: Whether "inadvertent inoculation" in this postulate means (a) accidental wound inoculation in general, (b) a deliberate inoculation that inadvertently contained something else, or (c) both. His wording covers both readings; he has not settled which one he means here.
