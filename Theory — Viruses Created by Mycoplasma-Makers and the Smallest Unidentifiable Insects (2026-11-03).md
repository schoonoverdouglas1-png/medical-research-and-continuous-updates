## Postulates, split

### P1 — Viruses are mostly created by the things that create mycoplasma
**His statement, tested generally.**
- **Verdict: BREAKS as a creation account, HOLDS in a narrower host sense.** Viruses are not, in the documented record, created by mycoplasmas or by whatever creates mycoplasmas. The standard origin accounts for viruses are different: escaped host genes that gained independence, reduced parasites that lost their free-living machinery, and ancient virus-first lineages — none of which makes mycoplasma the maker. What does hold: mycoplasmas, as the smallest free-living bacteria (no cell wall, tiny genomes), are hosts in their own right, and they carry their own viruses — mycoplasma phages — just as larger bacteria carry phages and fungi carry mycoviruses. A virus can live in a mycoplasma; that does not make the mycoplasma its creator.
- **Simply put:** a virus can rent a room in the smallest house. Renting is not building.

### P2 — The smallest things above them are unidentifiable insects or species
**His statement, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS as a discovery claim, BREAKS as a category.** It is true that the smallest and least-studied insects and other species carry viruses we have not yet identified — insect viromes are vast, and new insect-specific viruses are found every time a new insect species is sequenced. "Unidentifiable" needs care, though: most such species are unidentified *so far*, not unidentifiable in principle, and an unidentified insect is not the same as an unidentified virus inside it. The insect and its virus are two separate identifications, and either can be missing while the other is known.
- **Simply put:** new tiny bugs mean new tiny viruses waiting to be found — but "not yet named" is not "cannot be named."

### P3 — Viruses mostly come from the smallest end of life
**His broader pattern, tested.**
- **Verdict: HOLDS partially, BREAKS as "mostly."** Small hosts do carry many viruses — phages in bacteria, mycoviruses in fungi, insect-specific viruses in insects — and the smallest free-living cells are a rich virus habitat. But viruses are not mostly from the smallest end alone: they infect every branch of life, from archaea and bacteria through plants, fungi, insects, and mammals, and the largest known viruses (giant viruses of amoebae) are bigger than some bacteria. Smallness is a rich source, not the whole source.
- **Simply put:** the smallest houses have many tenants, but viruses live in houses of every size.

## Combined statement (his thought + the record)
If his "things that create mycoplasma" means the smallest living hosts, then the pattern holds as habitat, not as creation: mycoplasmas and the tiniest insects are virus carriers, and unsequenced tiny species are the likeliest place to find viruses we have not named yet. What does not hold is the creation arrow — viruses are not made by mycoplasmas, and a virus found in a tiny insect is not proved to have been made by that insect.
