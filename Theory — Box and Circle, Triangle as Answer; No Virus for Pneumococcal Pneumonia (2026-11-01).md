## P1 — There is no known virus for pneumococcal pneumonia [H]

**Simply put:** Pneumococcal pneumonia is bacterial by definition. No virus *is* the pneumococcus.

**Test, three ways:**
1. Definition [D]: "Pneumococcal pneumonia is a type of bacterial pneumonia that is caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (pneumococcus)."
2. Contrast [D]: "Unlike a cold, the flu or COVID-19, which are caused by viruses, pneumococcal pneumonia is caused by bacteria."
3. The seam that keeps P1 narrow [D]: viruses do not *become* the pneumococcus, but they do *open the door* to it — see P2. Read broadly as "no virus is ever involved," P1 BREAKS. Read narrowly as "no virus is the pneumococcal agent itself," P1 HOLDS.

**Verdict: HOLDS narrow, BREAKS broad.** The narrow reading is the one his sentence needs, and it is documented.

## P2 — The showing of SARS and influenza could be a third tenacious way of seeing the whole package as a circle [H]

**Simply put:** The way SARS and flu *show* in a patient might give a third viewpoint — the round, whole view — on top of the other ways of seeing.

**Test, three ways:**
1. Showing overlap [D]: COVID-19 and influenza were built into one combo test precisely because "symptoms of COVID-19 and influenza can be similar" (INDICAID intended use, Nov 1 strip work). The showing track is real.
2. Secondary web [D]: "Post-viral secondary bacterial infections, such as those caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, are strongly associated with influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)." Influenza → pneumococcus → pneumonia is his documented chain from Oct 17.
3. The divergence that splits P2 [D]: in the VA study of 188,721 hospitalized patients, "both influenza and RSV infections were associated with increased odds of *S pneumoniae* infection (odds ratio [OR]: 2.39 and 2.50, respectively). However, prior COVID-19 infection decreased the odds of *S pneumoniae* infection (OR:.56)." And a triple co-infection case (influenza A + SARS-CoV-2 + *S. pneumoniae*) exists, but is described as rare.

**Verdict: HOLDS as a way of seeing, BREAKS as a single way.** SARS showing and influenza showing are not the same third way: influenza showing strongly predicts a pneumococcal second showing; COVID showing, in that large study, did not — it went the other way. So the "third way" holds if it means "read the showings as a whole," and breaks if it means "SARS and influenza showings behave alike toward pneumococcus." What the first two ways are — [GAP], his to name. Candidates (mine, labeled): the box way and the circle way themselves, with the third way being the two seen together.

**Verdict: HOLDS as doctrine, [GAP] as to which three points.** The triangle's corners are his to name. ## The combined statement (mine, labeled — his thoughts + my thoughts)

Pneumococcal pneumonia has no virus of its own — it is bacterial. That does not cut viruses out of the picture: influenza showing opens the door to a pneumococcal second showing, while COVID showing, in the largest study, does not do the same. Seeing that whole package takes both views at once: the box that sorts (which organism, which serotype, which line) and the circle that holds the whole (carriage, showing, second showing, clearing). Read together, they form the triangle — and the triangle is the answer because no single corner explains the package alone.
