## Verdict: HOLDS [H→D], with one refinement

**The classic sequence holds:** influenza first, pneumococcal disease second is the textbook secondary bacterial pneumonia pattern. Influenza damages airway epithelium, impairs mucociliary clearance, and weakens local immune defenses; *S. pneumoniae* then invades. The 1918 pandemic's deaths were largely secondary bacterial pneumonia, pneumococcus among the chief agents, and the pattern repeats with RSV, SARS-CoV-2, and other respiratory viruses in the package list's Tier B.

**The refinement — carriage comes first, disease comes later:** the bacterium itself usually does not arrive later. Pneumococcus commonly sits already in the nasopharynx as silent carriage; the virus opens the door, and the *disease* comes later. So the tightened form of the thought is: **carriage first (silent), virus second (the opener), pneumococcal disease third (the follower).**

**The boundary:** the reverse order and simultaneous co-infection also occur, so the sequence is the classic pattern, not a universal law. And in his chain model (virus as the small piece, bacterium stuck to the chain in between), this sequence is the chain in motion.

- Simply put: Flu first opens the door; the pneumococcus that was already sitting there walks through it second.
