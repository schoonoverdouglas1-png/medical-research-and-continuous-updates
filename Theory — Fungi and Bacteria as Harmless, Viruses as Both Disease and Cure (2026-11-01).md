## Split postulates

- **P1:** Fungus and bacteria are actually harmless.
- **P2:** Viruses are both part of the disease and part of the cure.

**Simply put.** Most germs that live with us don't hurt us — and the viruses, of all things, are the ones that both cause sickness and can be turned into treatment.

## Tests

### P1 — Fungi and bacteria are actually harmless
**BREAKS as a universal, HOLDS for the majority of colonizing species.**

- Breaks universally: many fungi and bacteria are documented pathogens, including the organisms at the centre of his own work — *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (pneumonia, meningitis, bacteremia), *Neisseria meningitidis* (meningitis, bloodstream infection), *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (meningitis, epiglottitis), invasive *Candida* and *Aspergillus* species, *Coccidioides* (Valley fever, including coccidioidal meningitis) [D]. A theory that called all fungi and bacteria harmless would erase the disease tables he has spent weeks building.
- Holds for the majority: most bacterial and fungal species a person encounters are non-pathogenic colonizers or environmental organisms that never cause disease in a healthy person [D, general]. The human microbiome — gut, skin, airway — is overwhelmingly composed of bacteria (and fungi) that are harmless or beneficial in place, and disease arises when balance, barrier, or immunity changes, not because every colonizer is a pathogen. That is tonight's colonization file, restated: the stuff is colonized there, and most of it is harmless *there*.
- The sharpened version: *most fungi and bacteria are harmless in place; a minority are pathogens, and harmless colonizers can become harmful when place, balance, or immunity changes.* That version holds, and it preserves both his observation and the pathogen record.

### P2 — Viruses are both part of the disease and part of the cure
**HOLDS, in documented senses that must be named separately — because "cure" here means several different things.**

**Viruses as part of disease [D]:** uncontroversial in his own work — arboviruses (La Crosse, Jamestown Canyon, and the California serogroup), influenza, coronaviruses, enteroviruses, herpesviruses, and others cause disease directly, and viral infection can precede or enable bacterial disease (the influenza/RSV → pneumococcal synergy in his Box/Circle file).

**Viruses as part of cure, sense 1 — phages kill bacteria [D]:** bacteriophages are viruses that infect and kill bacteria, and phage therapy — using phages to treat bacterial infections, including antibiotic-resistant ones — is a documented, actively studied treatment approach. Here the virus is literally the cure for a bacterial disease: the disease agent and the cure agent are both viruses/bacteria in opposite roles.

**Viruses as part of cure, sense 2 — oncolytic and vector viruses [D, general]:** oncolytic viruses are engineered or selected to infect and kill cancer cells; viral vectors (modified adenoviruses, lentiviruses, adeno-associated viruses) deliver genes or vaccine antigens and are the basis of several licensed vaccines and gene therapies. Here the virus is a *tool* — its disease-causing parts removed or redirected — not a wild virus curing by itself.

**Viruses as part of cure, sense 3 — mycoviruses attenuating fungi [D, from the Nov 1 mycovirus search]:** mycoviruses infecting fungi can reduce fungal virulence (hypovirulence) and change drug resistance and toxin production — a virus making its fungal host *less* harmful. That is a virus as part of a cure in the most literal ecological sense: a virus of the pathogen, weakening the pathogen.

**Viruses as part of cure, sense 4 — immune training [H→D, partial]:** viral infection leaves immune memory that can protect against later infection (the basis of live-attenuated vaccines), and some viral exposures are hypothesized to shape immune regulation. This sense holds as vaccine-mediated protection; it breaks if read as "getting a viral disease cures you" in general — wild viral disease is not a treatment, and the cure in this sense is the *attenuated or inactivated* virus in a vaccine, not the disease itself.

**What does NOT hold:** a universal "viruses cure the diseases they cause." A wild arbovirus does not cure arboviral disease; a wild influenza virus does not cure influenza. The cure senses above all involve a *different* virus, a modified virus, or a virus of a *different* organism (a phage of a bacterium, a mycovirus of a fungus) — never the same wild virus curing its own disease by itself.

## Combined statement (his thought + tested thought)

His observation is right in its shape and needs its quantifiers: most fungi and bacteria are harmless in place, and the harmful minority are the ones his tables track — so "harmless" is true of the majority and false as a universal. And viruses genuinely sit on both sides of the disease/cure line, but not in the same act: as disease agents in their own right, and as cures when they are phages killing bacteria, vectors delivering therapy, mycoviruses weakening fungi, or attenuated viruses training immunity. The virus is not both at once in one infection; it is disease in one role and cure in another role, and naming the role is what makes the claim true.

## My addition (labeled mine)

For the booklet, teach P2 as a role table, not a riddle: four rows — wild virus → disease; phage → bacterial cure; vector/oncolytic virus → therapy tool; mycovirus → fungal weakening. Students who see the four roles will stop asking whether viruses are "good or bad" and start asking *which virus, in which role* — which is the question that actually has an answer.

## Companion note — Arvaris = arbovirus (resolved Nov 1, 2026, 11:19 PM CST)

Douglas confirmed by option tap: **"Arvaris" means arbovirus.** That resolves the open referent from 11:18 PM CST (first occurrence, no prior hits in files or memory).

**Sentence tested under that confirmed reading:** "Arvaris [arbovirus] is made from our red and white blood cells."

- **BREAKS as a composition claim [D].** An arbovirus particle is made of its own viral genome plus viral proteins, and sometimes a lipid envelope taken from a host cell membrane during budding. It is not made from red or white blood cells.
- **HOLDS in two narrower readings:** (1) arboviruses circulate *in* blood (viremia) — that is why arbopanels test blood, and why red and white cells are the *sample*, not the material; (2) some arboviruses infect white blood cells or other host cells as part of their replication, so blood cells can be *hosts* or *carriers*, not ingredients.
- **Red blood cells specifically:** mature red blood cells have no nucleus and cannot support viral replication; they can carry virus particles on their surface or in plasma, but they do not make them. White blood cells, by contrast, can be infected and can produce virus — again, as hosts, not as raw material.
- **Sharpened sentence:** *Arboviruses travel in our blood and can infect our white blood cells, but they are made from their own viral parts, assembled inside host cells — not made from red or white blood cells themselves.*
