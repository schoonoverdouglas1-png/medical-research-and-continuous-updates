## Tests against the record (three ways)
- Way 1, documented crystal forms: ACS Crystal Growth & Design, 2014 — "only four crystal forms have been reported until now. These are doxycycline hyclate (DOX.HYC), which is a hydrochloride salt hemiethanolate-hemihydrate; its isomorphous hydrobromide, hydrochloride salt dihydrate (DOX·HCl·2H2O); and doxycycline monohydrate (DOX·H2O)." Two new forms reported there were a hydronitrate salt hemihydrate and an acetic acid solvate dihydrate. No hydride, monohydride, or trihydrate in that list.
- Way 2, naming: hyclate vs. monohydrate are different salt forms of the same active ingredient, doxycycline. Monohydrate = one water; hyclate = hydrochloride hemiethanolate-hemihydrate; dihydrate = two waters. A trihydrate would logically be three waters, but it is not in the reported list above — [GAP], not documented.
- Way 3, hydride vs. hydrate: hydride is H-, a reducing ion (as in borohydride / cyanoborohydride); hydrate is water in the crystal (as in monohydrate). They are different chemistry, not interchangeable endings. A "doxycycline hydride" would not be a salt/hydrate form in the usual sense — no documented doxycycline hydride form was found.
