## P1 — Causation thought of as the name of the symptomology grouping [H; holds for syndrome-named diseases, breaks as universal]

**His claim:** causation of disease needs to be thought of as a name of the symptomology grouping, as a disease name.

**Tested:**
1. *For — syndrome names [D]:* medicine already names many diseases by their symptom grouping, not their agent — encephalitis (brain inflammation, whatever the agent), pneumonia (lung syndrome, whatever the agent), chronic fatigue syndrome. The name groups symptoms; the agent is a separate fact. His claim describes real naming practice.
2. *For — his own doctrine:* his standing position that disease names are groupings (combination-of-serotypes denotes disease; names connect with small pieces) and his Architecture essay (classification systems are maps, not the territory) both say the same thing: the name is the grouping, and the grouping is the map.
3. *Against — as universal [D]:* many diseases are named for agents or mechanisms, not symptoms — tuberculosis (the tubercle bacillus), COVID-19 (the virus), sickle cell disease (the mechanism). For those, the name is not the symptom grouping. His claim breaks if it claims all names work this way.

**Verdict:** HOLDS for syndrome-named diseases and as his naming doctrine; BREAKS as a universal claim about all disease names.
- Simply put: Some disease names are just the list of symptoms wearing a title. Others are named for the germ or the broken part. Both kinds exist.

## P2 — Bacteria, fungi, and bugs are elements of disease, not the causation; the person being there is the condition [H→D, strong foothold]

**His claim:** the elements of disease are the bacteria, fungi, and the bugs themselves — thought of as elements, not as causation — and the causative agents are that the person is there.

**Tested:**
1. *The triad [D]:* the traditional model for infectious disease consists of an external agent, a susceptible host, and an environment that brings the host and agent together. The host — the person being there — is literally one leg of the standard model. His "person is there" is not outside medicine; it is one-third of its oldest causation model.
2. *Rothman's causal pies [D]:* one model proposed by Rothman in 1976, known as the Causal Pies — an individual factor that contributes to cause disease is shown as a piece of a pie, and after all the pieces fall into place, the pie is complete and disease occurs; the individual factors are called component causes. A single component cause is rarely a sufficient cause by itself. His "elements" are, in documented language, component causes — slices, not the whole pie.
3. *Honest counterweight [D]:* the same source notes Rothman's model is less useful for diseases with a single, well-defined cause, such as certain infectious diseases. Where one agent is necessary and near-sufficient (rabies virus, *Bacillus anthracis*), the elements view breaks as a replacement — though it still holds as a complement (host susceptibility, dose, route remain slices).
4. *His own threads:* "bugs within us" (Oct 15), the chain model (viruses as small pieces, bacteria/fungi stuck in between), and the carrier thesis (presence ≠ disease) all run the same direction: the bug is a piece of the picture, not the picture.

**Verdict:** HOLDS [H→D] — the strongest documented foothold of the four postulates. Bugs as elements/components is standard epidemiology's language; his addition is the emphasis: never let one slice stand in for the pie.
- Simply put: The germ is one slice of the pie, not the whole pie. And the person being there is a slice too — without the person, there is no pie at all.

## P3 — Blame must be shifted to the cure, not to the bugs [H, doctrine]

**His claim:** treatment and blame are powerful things, and somehow the blame has to be shifted to the cure, and not to blame the bugs.

**Tested:**
1. *Blame is powerful [H, his standing theme]:* his ultimate theme — "Ceases Lawsuits by Doing the Right Goddamn Thing" — is built on exactly this: blame, once assigned, becomes lawsuits, and lawsuits become the disease of medicine itself. Shifting blame off the bugs and onto the cure is the same move, pointed at the germ instead of the doctor.
2. *Documented neighbor:* patient-safety culture moved from blaming individuals to fixing systems ("just culture") — the same shift, from a target of blame to a target of repair. The doctrine has a working precedent in medicine's own house.
3. *Honest line:* this is a value judgment, not an empirical claim — it cannot break or hold on data. It stands or falls as doctrine: does medicine work better when its energy goes to cure rather than to blame? His answer is yes, and the lawsuits theme is his evidence.

**Verdict:** HOLDS as doctrine [H] — a moral and practical stance, with a documented neighbor and his own central theme behind it.
- Simply put: Blame is strong medicine, and it poisons whoever it lands on. Point it at the cure instead, and it heals.

## P4 — You can't be mad at the things making you sick; they're there to help you figure out how to get better [H, stance]

**His claim:** you can't get mad at the things that are there making you sick, because they're there to help you figure out what to do to make you better.

**Tested:**
1. *For — bugs as teachers [H→D neighborhood]:* his "bugs within us" thread (bugs as part of the immune system that get sick with the same things we do) and microbiome science (commensal microbes train immunity; their presence teaches the immune system what to tolerate) both treat microbes as instructors, not only invaders.
2. *For — symptoms as information [LOGIC]:* fever, inflammation, and the symptom grouping itself are the signals that tell the clinician what to do — the sickness is the message that points at the cure. In that sense the things making you sick are, literally, helping you figure out what to do.
3. *Honest line:* this does not deny harm — pathogens injure, and the stance does not erase that. The claim is about the stance toward them (not anger, not blame), not about their harmlessness. As a claim that bugs never harm, it would break; as a claim about how to stand toward them, it holds.

**Verdict:** HOLDS as stance [H] — anger at the bug is wasted motion; attention to what the bug teaches is the cure's starting point.
- Simply put: Don't be mad at the germ. Listen to what it's telling you — the sickness is the clue to the cure.

## Combined statement

A disease name is a grouping of symptoms wearing a title. The bugs — bacteria, fungi, all of them — are elements in that grouping, slices of the pie, not the pie itself; the person being there is a slice too, and without the person there is no disease at all. Blame is too powerful to waste on the bugs: shift it to the cure, the way his lawsuits theme shifts it off the doctor and onto doing right. And the things making you sick are not enemies to be mad at — they are the clues, there to help you figure out what to do to get better.
