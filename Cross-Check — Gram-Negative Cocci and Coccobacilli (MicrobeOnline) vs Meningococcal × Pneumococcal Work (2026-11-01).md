## Simply put
Some germs are round, some are short rods that only *look* round. The shape alone can't name the germ — where it was found, and what lab tests it passes or fails, are what name it. That is the whole reason a code that separates shape from disease makes sense.

## 1. The split the page insists on [D]
- **Gram-negative cocci** (true round cells, often in pairs): *Neisseria* (Neisseriaceae), *Moraxella* (Moraxellaceae), and the anaerobe *Veillonella*.
- **Coccobacilli** (very short rods, easily mistaken for cocci, especially viewed end-on): a mixed group across several families — *Kingella* (also Neisseriaceae) and *Acinetobacter* (also Moraxellaceae) among them.
- Page's own caution, carried verbatim in substance: morphology guides the workup; it does not establish species identity. Schematics are not to scale.

### 2b. Moraxella placement — RESOLVES a standing reading [D]
- *Moraxella catarrhalis* is a **Gram-negative diplococcus** (cocci list), not a coccobacillus. It can resemble *Neisseria* on Gram stain.
- Distinguishers [D]: oxidase positive, DNase positive, butyrate esterase positive; "hockey-puck" colonies that can be pushed across agar intact; most clinical isolates produce beta-lactamase.
- Diseases [D]: otitis media, sinusitis in children; acute COPD exacerbations in adults. Common upper-respiratory colonizer — presence in a respiratory specimen must be read with specimen quality and clinical findings.
- **For the Moraxella theory file:** this settles the shape question (diplococcus, Neisseria-like) while leaving the theory's other postulates untouched. The earlier "new morcacus = *Moraxella catarrhalis*" reading is now [H→D] on identity, shape still to be tested against the theory's claims.

### 2c. Neisseria pair — biochemical distinction filled [D]
- *N. meningitidis*: typically acid from **glucose and maltose**; capsule central to invasive disease, resisting phagocytosis and complement-mediated killing; colonizes nasopharynx without illness; invasive disease = meningitis, bloodstream infection, or both; petechial/purpuric non-blanching rash may be absent early.
- *N. gonorrhoeae*: typically acid from **glucose alone, not maltose**; no conventional polysaccharide capsule; intracellular diplococci in symptomatic male urethral secretions have strong diagnostic value; cervical/rectal/pharyngeal specimens need site-specific testing (Gram stain performance differs).
- Memory aid on the page: "M for meningococcus, M for maltose."
- **Gap filled:** the existing cross-reference named the meningococcal pair without this biochemical split. It is added here, not edited into the original.

### 2e. Veillonella — cocci roster completed [D]
- Obligately anaerobic Gram-negative cocci, often in pairs; normal oral, gastrointestinal, and genital microbiota; usually low pathogenic potential; polymicrobial infection participant; invasive disease possible, significance depends on specimen (blood/sterile site raises concern; mucosal specimen may be accompanying flora).

### 2f. Specimen-context rule — fills the "showing" gap [D]
- Same shape, different meaning by specimen: urethral intracellular diplococci in a symptomatic man = strong gonorrhea value; respiratory specimen = may be colonizing flora; cerebrospinal fluid Gram-negative diplococci = urgently suggest meningococcal disease, prompt communication and organism confirmation required.
- A negative smear does not exclude infection.
- **For the Lab-Detectable doctrine file:** this is documented support for "showing depends on where it was found," distinct from "detectable = sick." It does not change that file's verdicts; it is filed beside them.

## 3. Test against the Gemini shard-code proposal [LOGIC, tested]
Gemini's Nov 1 proposal: a 4-tier code — SYN- (primary syndrome, e.g. SYN-MENG), MOR- (morphology/stain, e.g. MOR-COCCB), VEC- (transmission vector, e.g. VEC-RESP), ICD- (diagnostic anchor, e.g. ICD-G00.0 for *H. influenzae* meningitis).
- **HOLDS as a separation principle:** this page proves the need for it — *Neisseria* and *Moraxella* share diplococcal shape but differ by DNase/butyrate esterase; *Acinetobacter* shares coccoid appearance but differs by oxidase; short rods viewed end-on mimic cocci. Shape alone cannot carry identity, so a code that keeps MOR- separate from organism and syndrome is not illogical.
- **BREAKS as a replacement for organism-first listing:** this page itself lists by organism, not by syndrome, and its lab clues are organism-specific (X/V factors, maltose, DNase). A syndrome-first code would still need the organism tier to use those clues. The two orderings are complementary, not interchangeable — filed as such, not collapsed.
- **ICD anchor caution [GAP]:** the page confirms ICD-10 bundles infectious disease primarily by organism rather than syndrome (Gemini's premise), but this page does not test whether ICD-G00.0 maps cleanly to the shard example. That mapping stays [GAP], not assumed.

## 5. My addition (labeled mine, per standing rule)
A one-line decoder for the whole page, for the Medical Elements / herbology work: **shape is the first word, not the last word.** Cocci vs coccobacillus tells you what the cell looks like in a smear; DNase, maltose, X/V factors, oxidase, and specimen site tell you what it is and whether it matters. Any future table built from this page should carry all three columns — shape, lab clue, specimen — or it will repeat the exact confusion the page warns about.
